Weeping Willow · Book One

The Absence: Cast

by Kristopher Michael

Who connects to whom

In detail

Iris Vale

Protagonist · data recovery specialist

A systems-recovery tech, one of the few people who will take jobs inside sealed sites. She is known for getting in, getting the data out and not asking questions. After Meridian she is the only known survivor of direct contact with Willow, and the one thing taken from her is her love for her younger sister Lena. She still remembers Lena and knows she should care. She doesn't.

There is collateral damage too: delayed emotional responses, unreliable decisions, memory gaps and false certainties. She catalogs her own losses like system logs ("Expected: frustration. Observed: none."). Her drive is not revenge or justice but understanding. Because the Taking was interrupted, she can sense Willow nearby as pressure in her head, and that makes her the only person who can track it. Every step closer costs her more.

She is controlled, observant and dry on the surface, and constantly monitoring herself underneath. Before the incident she was protective, impatient, funny and sharp, but the reader only ever sees that version in what other people expect of her and don't get.

Willow

The entity · antagonist

The thing Iris meets inside Meridian. It is elongated and almost human, with a face of "features learned from observation rather than born to them." It moves with no wasted motion.

It takes by touch. A hand on the chest, a flash of a person's most charged memories, then the feeling is stripped off them and the memory follows. Up close, it gives off a calming field that dulls sound and slows reactions. Nothing it does looks cruel. It is silent for the whole of Book 1.

Lena Vale

Iris's younger sister

She is warm, direct and persistent. She refuses to accept that her sister has changed and keeps fighting for the relationship. In the café scene (Ch10) she tells Iris, "You sound like you're managing a problem," and Iris says, "I am." Lena: "You are the problem." She is the emotional core of the book because she is exactly what is missing. Love only flows in one direction now.

"I love you." / "I love you." The words matched. Tone aligned. No delay.

Caleb Ward

Field tech · the reader's anchor

An independent tech runner Iris has worked with for years. He is sharp, funny, conversational, a little reckless, and one of the few people who knew her before. He notices she has changed and tells her so. Ch12, the midpoint, is his POV, the only chapter in the book told from his point of view, and it is the on-page Taking. Afterward he functions perfectly and he is not Caleb anymore.

"If something's off, you can just say that. You don't have to… optimize the answer."

Daniel Kade

Client · handler · ally

A former Meridian logistics and site coordinator, not a scientist. He hired Iris for the job (ref MN-44-7) and keeps feeding her the adjacent sites. He is practical and controlled, and he doesn't speculate. His guilt is logistical rather than dramatic: he moved crates and data units to locations he never asked about. His Meridian access codes still work.

"I moved things through here. Crates. Equipment. Data units. Didn't ask what was in them."

Dr. Elena Rourke

Behavioral health authority · the skeptic

Lowport's medical authority on the rising cases. She is analytical, precise and competent, with hundreds of cases under one classification ("Behavioral flattening. Dissociative presentation. Emotional attenuation.") and no full recoveries. She is right about the symptoms and wrong about the cause. She isn't a villain, just a wall. She hands Iris redacted case files anyway, pragmatically, and notices Iris presenting with the same markers.

"They're moving." / "They're not moving. They're appearing."

Lila Mercer

The first fully Taken person

The first fully Taken person Iris meets face to face (Ch4). She is calm, present and polite, and completely hollow. She sets the tone for the whole book: no drama, only absence. Iris's body reacts even though her emotions can't.

"Do you feel like anything is missing?" / "No."

Joel & Leah

The first case (K-01)

Joel is a north-side dock and logistics worker who was gone for two days and came back "fine." His answers are correct and empty: "I know what is necessary." His wife Leah is frightened and confused, a smaller mirror of what Lena is living with. His case leads to Iris's report to Kade: "Something has been removed."
