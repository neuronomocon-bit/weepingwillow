Chapter 9 — The City Feels It
Lowport continued to function.
Transit ran on schedule. Platforms filled and cleared in steady cycles. No visible disruption.
Iris stood among them.
People moved with purpose. Efficient. Conversations occurred in short exchanges — questions answered, transactions completed, movement resumed.
She boarded with the others.
The car was partially filled. Seats taken in practical distribution.
A man across from her spoke into a handheld device.
"Yes," he said. "That's fine."
"Understood."
The call ended. He lowered the device and remained still for a moment longer than required. Then stood at the next stop and exited.
At the next stop, two people collided lightly while entering.
"Sorry," one said.
"It's fine," the other replied.
They separated immediately. No additional acknowledgment.
Iris exited two stops later.
The market was open. Vendors arranged in regular spacing. Foot traffic steady.
Iris paused near a produce stall. A customer asked about availability. The vendor answered. The exchange ended. The customer remained for a moment, as if expecting more.
Nothing followed.
They moved on.
She checked the case updates.
Rourke's dataset had expanded. New entries. No change in classification. Behavioral attenuation. Stable cognition.
She opened one of the newer files. Rourke's language had shifted. Fewer qualifiers. Fewer attempts to explain. The notes were shorter than earlier cases — more direct, less commentary.
Iris closed the file.
She crossed into an older residential block. Familiar layout. She had been here before.
A group stood outside a building entrance. Three people. Conversation in progress.
"Did you call them," one asked.
"Yes."
"What did they say."
"They're sending someone."
"When."
"Soon."
The exchange ended. They remained standing. No one spoke.
Then one checked their device and walked away. The others followed without comment.
Iris continued down the street.
A woman sat on the steps of a building halfway down the block. Coat pulled tight. Eyes forward. A child stood beside her, maybe four, holding a small toy close to his chest.
The child dropped the toy. It hit the step and bounced once.
He looked at it.
The woman looked at it.
Neither moved.
The child bent down after a moment, picked it up, held it again. Same posture. Same grip.
The woman returned her gaze to the street.
No word. No touch. No correction. No comfort.
Iris stopped.
She watched the space between them. Mother and child. Sitting together. No connection visible in any direction.
The child did not seem distressed. The woman did not seem neglectful. Both were present. Both were accounted for.
The interaction had completed without occurring.
Iris moved on.
She slowed.
Not because of what she saw.
Because of what wasn't occurring.
She tried to recall how this block used to feel — noise, overlap, irregularity.
The information was there. Accessible. Not immediate.
A delay.
She waited for it to resolve.
It didn't.
Her phone signaled. Coordinates from Kade. Two new sites. No additional context.
She read them once. The response did not form immediately. Another delay — duration longer than prior instances.
She typed "Received" and sent it. The action completed.
She continued through the neighborhood. Past buildings she recognized. Entrances she had used. Corners she had turned before.
She adjusted her route toward the new coordinates, then stopped. Checked the map again. The route she had chosen bypassed the closer site in favor of the farther one. No reason for the preference. She couldn't reconstruct it.
She reversed the order and continued.
She stopped at an intersection and looked down both directions.
A memory surfaced. Standing here. Different time. Different context. Someone with her.
The image held.
The identity did not.
She waited.
Nothing followed.
She moved on.
The city continued around her. Uninterrupted.
She had known this place.
That fact remained.
The feeling did not.
She could not determine if the difference was external.
Or if it was her.
She did not resolve it.
She continued walking.
The city did the same.
