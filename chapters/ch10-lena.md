Chapter 10 — Lena
Lena chose the place.
A small café off a residential street. Not crowded. Tables spaced close enough for conversation without carrying.
Iris arrived first.
She selected a table near the window. Sat with a clear line of sight to the entrance. Hands still. Posture neutral.
Lena arrived three minutes later.
She saw Iris immediately and smiled—open, unguarded. The expression reached her eyes without delay.
“I thought I might be early,” Lena said as she approached.
“You’re not,” Iris said.
Lena set her bag down and sat across from her. She looked at Iris for a moment longer than necessary.
“You look…” Lena started, then stopped. “You look fine.”
“I am.”
Lena nodded, but didn’t look convinced.
A server approached. Lena ordered without checking the menu. Iris declined.
“You sure?” Lena asked.
“Yes.”
The server left.
Lena leaned forward slightly.
“I wasn’t sure you’d come,” she said.
“I said I would.”
“You say a lot of things.”
“I follow through.”
“Most of the time.”
Iris held her gaze.
Lena studied her face, searching.
“How’s work?” Lena asked.
“Busy.”
“That sounds more like you.”
Iris gave a small nod.
“You can’t tell me what you’re working on.”
“No.”
“Can you tell me if you’re okay?”
“Yes.”
Lena paused.
“Actually okay,” she said. “Not just saying it.”
“I’m okay.”
The answer was smooth. Immediate.
Lena watched her a moment longer.
“When did you last sleep?” she asked.
“Last night.”
“How much?”
“Enough.”
Lena exhaled, a hint of frustration beneath it.
“You’re doing that thing again.”
“What thing.”
“Answering without answering.”
“I’m answering.”
“You’re not engaging.”
“I am.”
Lena shook her head slightly.
“Do you hear yourself?”
“Yes.”
“And that doesn’t feel strange to you?”
Iris held the pause just long enough.
“No.”
Lena leaned back.
“That’s the problem,” she said.
Iris adjusted her posture slightly. A mirror of Lena’s earlier movement. Subtle. Correct.
“You seem tense,” Iris said.
Lena let out a short breath.
“I’m not tense. I’m worried.”
“I understand.”
“No, you don’t.”
Iris didn’t correct her.
Lena leaned forward again, more direct now.
“You’ve been different since that incident,” she said. “And I don’t know what that means.”
“It was contained.”
“That’s not what I’m asking.”
“I know.”
“Then answer what I’m asking.”
Iris considered.
“I’m functioning normally,” she said. “Work is stable. No issues with memory or coordination.”
Lena stared at her.
“I didn’t ask for a report.”
“It’s relevant.”
“It’s not.”
Lena looked away, then back.
“You used to push back,” she said. “You used to argue with me. You used to care if I was annoyed with you.”
Iris met her eyes.
“I still care.”
The words came clean. No hesitation.
Lena held the gaze.
“Do you?” she asked quietly.
“Yes.”
“How?”
Iris didn’t answer immediately.
“In the ways that matter,” she said.
Lena studied her face, searching for something behind the words.
“And what ways are those?”
Iris held the pause.
“Showing up,” she said. “Staying in contact. Maintaining—” She stopped. Adjusted. “Not disappearing.”
Lena didn’t move.
“That’s not what I meant,” she said.
“I know.”
Silence.
The server returned with Lena’s drink. Set it down. Left.
Lena wrapped her hands around it but didn’t drink.
“I talked to someone,” she said. “Just to ask questions.”
“Yes.”
“I described… this.” She gestured slightly. “The way you are right now.”
Iris nodded.
“They said it could be stress,” Lena continued. “Or dissociation. Something temporary.”
“That’s possible.”
“You don’t sound concerned.”
“I’m monitoring it.”
“That’s not concern.”
“It’s appropriate.”
Lena let out a quiet, humorless laugh.
“You sound like you’re managing a problem,” she said.
“I am.”
“You are the problem.”
Iris didn’t respond.
Lena’s voice softened.
“I miss you,” she said.
Iris held her gaze.
“I’m here,” she said.
“That’s not what I mean.”
“I know.”
Lena looked down at the table.
“I don’t know how to reach you,” she said.
“You are.”
Lena shook her head.
“No,” she said. “I’m talking at you.”
Iris adjusted slightly in her seat.
“That’s not my intent,” she said.
“I know it’s not.”
Lena looked up again.
“That’s what makes it worse.”
Silence settled between them.
Not heavy. Not sharp. Just present.
Lena picked up her drink. Took a small sip. Set it down.
“I keep thinking this is going to pass,” she said. “That you’re going to come back to normal.”
Iris didn’t respond immediately.
“I’m stable,” she said.
“That’s not the same thing.”
“No.”
Lena held her gaze for a moment longer.
Then nodded once.
“Okay,” she said.
She reached for her bag and stood.
Iris remained seated.
“I have to go,” Lena said.
“Yes.”
Lena hesitated.
“I love you,” she said.
“I love you,” Iris said.
The words matched. Tone aligned. No delay.
Lena watched her.
Something in her expression shifted—uncertain, searching, almost convinced.
Then not.
She nodded.
“Okay,” she said quietly.
She turned and left.
Iris remained at the table for a moment.
The chair across from her was empty.
She stood, paid, and stepped outside.
The city moved around her.
She joined it.
