Chapter 7 — The Pattern
The apartment was not designed for this.
It had been adjusted.
The table was cleared—everything unnecessary pushed aside or stacked out of reach. The surface was covered now. Printouts. Screens. Notes.
The screens weren’t synchronized. Different datasets. Different resolutions. One held case files. One a city map. One timelines. Another looped fragments—interviews, responses, pauses.
Iris stood in the center of it.
Looking.
She had been there for hours. Long enough for the light outside to shift into evening. Long enough for the system to dim the room and then correct itself when she overrode it.
The data resisted organization.
Case locations plotted across Lowport.
Individually, they didn’t resolve. No clustering. No clear origin.
Rourke was right about that.
Together—
Iris adjusted the map.
Filter. Time.
The earliest cases faded. The recent ones sharpened.
The distribution shifted.
She narrowed the window further.
Two points dropped out.
They didn’t fit.
Not because they contradicted the pattern—but because they didn’t belong to it.
The remaining points aligned.
She stepped closer.
Joel K-01. North side.
Lila Mercer.
She placed the printouts side by side. No visible connection.
She shifted back to the map.
Grid overlay.
Transit routes. Utility corridors. Maintenance access.
She overlaid Meridian-adjacent systems only.
The map changed.
Subtly—but enough.
The points weren’t isolated anymore.
They intersected something beneath them.
The overlay was incomplete. Old data. Sections marked decommissioned without confirmation.
It didn’t matter.
It was already there.
She adjusted the timeline again.
The path advanced.
Earlier points held. Newer ones extended past them.
Progression.
She shifted to the transcript screen.
Joel.
Lila.
Two additional cases.
She replayed short segments.
Questions.
Answers.
Stops.
The responses ended cleanly.
No trailing. No searching.
Completion.
Then nothing.
She logged it once.
Stripped content. Left only form.
Question → Answer → Stop.
Repeated across cases.
Different words.
Same shape.
She stepped away from the table.
Opened a line to Kade.
It connected on the second attempt.
Audio only.
“Yeah.”
“I have something,” Iris said.
“Pattern,” he said.
“Yes.”
“Go ahead.”
“They’re not independent cases.”
“I figured.”
“They’re connected.”
“That’s still not saying much.”
“They follow a path.”
That held.
“What kind of path?”
“Geographic. Chronological.”
“Everything is chronological.”
“This is directional.”
Silence.
“Explain.”
“The earlier cases cluster north,” she said. “Industrial edge. The later ones extend outward. Same vector.”
“Vector to where?”
“Out.”
“That’s not a location.”
“No.”
A breath on the line.
“You’re saying it’s moving?”
“Yes.”
“People move.”
“This isn’t people.”
“You don’t know that.”
“I do.”
“That’s not what I asked.”
“It’s accurate.”
“What’s the mechanism?”
“I don’t know.”
“Then what are you actually saying?”
Iris looked at the map.
“It’s not a condition,” she said.
“Rourke would disagree.”
“Yes.”
“What is it, then?”
She brought up the grid overlay.
“The cases align with Meridian-era systems.”
“What are you seeing?”
“Meridian-era systems.”
“Yes.”
Silence.
“That were decommissioned.”
“Partially.”
“That’s what the reports say.”
“They’re incomplete.”
“You checked?”
“No.”
“Then how do you know?”
“I can see it.”
“That’s not verification.”
“No.”
Another breath.
“You’re asking me to accept a moving pattern tied to Meridian-era systems that may not even be active.”
“Yes.”
Kade exhaled.
“Send me everything,” he said.
“I will.”
“And the outliers.”
“Yes.”
“Don’t clean it.”
“I didn’t.”
“You’re sure about the direction?”
“Yes.”
“How sure?”
“It doesn’t deviate.”
Another pause.
“Alright,” he said. “Send it.”
“I am.”
“Iris.”
“Yes.”
“You’ve changed.”
“Yes.”
The line closed.
The files transferred.
Iris stood in front of the map.
The path remained.
Unlabeled. Unexplained. Stable.
She traced it once with her eyes.
From origin.
Through progression.
Outward.
Something in the direction held.
Recognition.
Faint.
She stopped.
The sensation didn’t increase.
Didn’t resolve.
Then it was gone.
She noted it.
Location: frontal.
Duration: brief.
Intensity: minimal.
No pain.
No interference.
She looked at the map again.
At the path.
At the continuation.
No deviation.
No branching.
It advanced.
She said it out loud.
“It’s not a condition.”
No response.
“It’s not environmental.”
The room remained unchanged.
She looked at the direction one more time.
“Something is moving.”
Flat. Exact.
“And it touches people.”
Nothing followed.
The data didn’t change.
The map held.
Iris stood there a moment longer.
Then turned away.
The transfer completed behind her.
The pattern remained where it was.
Not solved.
Not contained.
Just visible.
For now.
