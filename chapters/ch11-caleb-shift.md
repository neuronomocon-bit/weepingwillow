Chapter 11 — Caleb Shift
The site was active.
Outer zone. Industrial edge of Lowport where maintenance blurred into reconstruction. Equipment staged in ordered clusters. Crews distributed across marked sections. Movement constant, coordinated.
Iris arrived on foot.
Kade had sent the coordinates that morning. No additional context. Just a name attached to the site log.
Caleb.
She located him quickly.
He stood near a temporary rig, speaking to two workers over the noise of a running compressor. His posture was loose, balanced. One hand resting against the frame, the other gesturing as he talked.
He saw her before she reached him.
A brief pause.
Then a grin.
“Iris,” he said. “Didn’t think I’d see you out here.”
“You’re assigned to this site,” she said.
“Yeah. Rotating through a few of them.” He nodded toward the rig. “This one’s been acting up.”
He dismissed the other workers with a quick gesture. They moved off without question.
“You tracking something,” he asked.
“Yes.”
“Here.”
“Yes.”
Caleb glanced around, taking in the site as if seeing it differently for the first time.
“Anything I should be worried about.”
“No.”
He studied her face for a second.
“You sure about that.”
“Yes.”
A small pause.
“Alright,” he said.
The rhythm of it was intact. Casual. Familiar.
But the pause held a fraction longer than expected.
Caleb pushed off the rig and started walking. Iris matched his pace.
“You want the official version or the useful one,” he said.
“Useful.”
“Nothing’s broken,” he said. “Everything’s just… off.”
“How.”
He took a second.
“Timing,” he said. “Stuff lags. Not enough to fail. Enough to notice.”
“Consistent.”
“Yeah.”
He kicked lightly at a cable run along the ground, not moving it—just checking its position.
“Like the system’s thinking about it before it does anything,” he added.
Iris watched him as he spoke.
The words were correct.
The tone was correct.
The spacing between them was slightly irregular.
“How long,” she asked.
“Couple days.”
“Progressing.”
“Hard to tell.”
He glanced at her.
“You’re going to ask if it’s getting worse.”
“Yes.”
“It’s not getting better,” he said.
They reached the far side of the site. A quieter section. Equipment powered down, waiting.
Caleb stopped near a control panel.
“This unit here,” he said. “It checks out fine. Diagnostics clean.”
He tapped the casing once.
“Still doesn’t feel right.”
Iris stepped closer.
“What doesn’t feel right.”
He opened his mouth to answer.
Paused.
Not searching.
Not uncertain.
Just still.
Then—
“Response time,” he said. “Like I said.”
The answer landed slightly late.
Iris noted it.
Caleb turned back toward her.
“You’re looking at me like I’m part of the problem,” he said.
“I’m observing.”
“Same thing, usually.”
“Not here.”
He held her gaze.
Then smiled again.
“Fair enough.”
The smile stayed a moment too long.
Then dropped.
“You been out here before,” he asked.
“Yes.”
“Feels different, doesn’t it.”
“Yes.”
He nodded, satisfied with that.
They stood in silence for a few seconds.
Not uncomfortable.
Caleb shifted his weight.
“You want me to walk you through the rest of it.” He asked.
“Yes.”
They moved through the site.
He explained the layout. The flow. Where things should be. Where they weren’t.
He made a joke about one of the older units—something about it outlasting everyone who worked on it.
The timing was off.
The line landed.
The reaction didn’t.
He didn’t adjust.
Just continued.
Iris watched him.
The pattern held.
Function intact.
Continuity intact.
Something else—
slightly displaced.
They stopped near the edge of the site.
Beyond it, the ground dropped off toward a lower service corridor.
Caleb looked out over it.
“You ever get the sense something’s already happened,” he said, “and we’re just catching up to it?”
Iris considered.
“Yes.”
He nodded.
“Yeah,” he said.
Silence.
Wind moved across the open section, carrying dust and a faint metallic smell.
Caleb checked his device.
“I’ve got another pass to do,” he said. “You need anything else.”
“No.”
He looked at her again.
Another pause.
Shorter this time.
“Good seeing you,” he said.
“You too.”
He gave a small nod, then turned and headed back toward the center of the site.
His pace was steady. Unchanged.
He integrated back into the movement without friction.
No one around him reacted.
Nothing marked him as different.
Iris remained where she was.
Watching.
He moved through the work with the same ease he always had.
Confident. Direct. Alive.
She held that.
Alive.
The word settled.
Then shifted.
He had been the most alive person she knew.
The tense registered.
Past.
She corrected it.
He is.
The correction held.
Not fully.
She watched him a moment longer.
Then turned and left the site.
Nothing had happened.
Nothing confirmed.
The pattern continued.
