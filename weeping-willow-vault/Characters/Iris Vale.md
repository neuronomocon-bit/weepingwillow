# Iris Vale
**Protagonist. POV of all of Book 1, and of Book 2's 15 Present chapters.**
**The only known survivor of direct contact with [[Willow]].**

---

## What was taken
Her ability to feel love for one specific person: her younger sister, **[[Lena Vale]]**.

Not all love. **Just one person.**

**What remains:** she remembers Lena exists, that they grew up together, that Lena mattered. She feels nothing — no warmth, no attachment, no grief, no urgency. The emotional anchor, the protective instinct, the meaning behind the relationship: gone.

**She knows: *"I should care."* But she does not.**

## Secondary damage (the bleed)
It was not a clean removal. Collateral:
- **Emotional delay** — reactions come late or not at all
- **Decision instability**
- **Memory gaps** around high-stress moments
- **False certainty** — she thinks something is true, and it is not
- Sometimes mimics emotions based on expectation rather than feeling

## Her dangerous thought
> *"Maybe I was better after."*

The pain tied to Lena is gone, and that absence is quieter. **This is terrifying, and it is the thought Book 3 hands back to her.**

## Profession
Systems recovery specialist. Works in abandoned infrastructure recovering lost or corrupted data. Known for getting in, getting data out, and not asking questions — one of the few people willing to take sealed-site jobs. **This is why she is the one who can read the drives, and it is the last intact thing she has.**

## Unique ability
Because the Taking was incomplete, she can **sense Willow's proximity**: pressure in her head, subtle disorientation, emotional flattening intensifying. Not supernatural — a residual connection from interrupted contact. Established in published Book 1 Ch7 and Ch8.

**What Book 2 adds is technique, not power** — holding attention at the front of the skull deliberately, something she taught herself in the second week after Book 1.

> [!warning] This makes her valuable to others and dangerous to herself
> *"She was the only one who could sense it. That was not an advantage. It was a limitation."* (Book 1 Ch16)

## Her arc
- **Book 1 — Awareness.** Realizes what was taken, tries to operate despite it, still believes she can fix things
- **Book 2 — Compensation.** Flat throughout and **worse than Book 1 left her.** Runs on scaffolding she built herself: a morning list beside the light switch, index cards along the doorframe, a dated inventory of her own erosion in her own handwriting. Recovers the complete account of the origin, understands it perfectly, **and feels none of it.** Ends the book permanently unfinished and aware of it
- **Book 3 — Degradation to collapse.** More loss, then structural loss. **The professional competence goes**, which is the last intact thing and the only instrument she has

**Her Book 2 arc in five words:** compensating → recovering → understanding → recognizing → owed a second visit.

## Her two compensation systems, and they do different jobs
- **The morning list** beside the light switch is **instructions**: *Water. Coffee. Check device. Check the map. Eat.*
- **The index cards** along the doorframe at eye height are **measurements**, each carrying a date, a duration and a category: *Retrieval, four seconds, site address.* *Sequence loss, cold coffee, approx. eleven minutes.* *Wrote note, cannot account for it.*

**The wall holds nineteen days at Day 21**, and she reads all of it once a week. **She has learned from the reading rather than from the living that the intervals are getting shorter.**

A **short row above the doorframe** holds the measurements that have not moved.

> [!warning] Keep the two symptom categories apart
> **`Retrieval`** is a delay in producing something stored.
> **`Sequence loss`** is a gap in the record of what happened.
> These never blur across any chapter. *Erosion* is her general word for the whole process.

## Writing rules
**She is flat in every present-day chapter. That is *affect*, not *syntax*.** Book 1 rendered her condition as fragmented prose because Book 1's whole voice was fragmented. **Book 2 does not**, and her flatness has to survive in complete, varied, joined sentences.

Her clinical logging — *she noted*, *she registered*, *Expected/Observed* — **is character voice and is permitted, and it has been used enough. It must vary or advance every time, never repeat.**

> [!warning] She does not grieve on the page in Book 2
> Not for Caleb, not for Marin, not for herself. She reconstructs what a thing should cost,
> correctly and completely, and nothing arrives. **No almost-arrivals.**
> Do not give her a moment where something nearly gets through.

**And rigor is her characterization** — a sentence she could not defend is a defect *in her*. See [[Audit Method]].

## Who she was before
Protective, especially of Lena. Emotionally present. Sharper edges, more reactive. Impatient. Funny. Said things with edge on them and regretted them immediately. **Reckless and competent in the same gesture — the trait that eventually walks her through a cut fence.**

**This version of her is never dramatized on the page anywhere in the series.** The reader only ever learns who she was from what other people expect of her and do not get.
