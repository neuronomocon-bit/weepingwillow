# Willow — the entity
**The antagonist. Present in all three books.**
**She is not a villain and there is no malice anywhere in her.**

---

## What she is
A bio-mechanical organism grown on [[Meridian Neurological]]'s therapeutic substrate from the complete pre-treatment emotional map of **[[Willow Halloway]]**, a deceased patient, after her sister **[[Marin Halloway]]** removed the integration limiter to reach it. Marin was later consumed entire, and consented.

**Structurally:** a dead younger sister's emotional map, plus a living older sister's grief for her, integrated into a system built to neutralize suffering, **with no instruction about what to do with either.**

> [!warning] Nobody explains the name, ever
> The entity is called Willow because Willow Halloway was a person. Book 1 uses the name
> in narration without introduction. Book 2 shows the reader why — **Iris *finds* it in Ch15.**
> Book 3 has the entity say "Willow Halloway" once, flat, in passing, never returned to.

## Satisfaction, not pleasure — hard rule
A complete taking registers as **completion**: a process reaching its endpoint. Clinical throughout.

**Banned in narration and in figurative description, in every book:** hunger, craving, appetite, relish, savoring, enjoyment, pleasure, feeding.

**Pleasure would make her a person who enjoys things. Satisfaction keeps her a system.**

**And the mechanical consequence is the engine of Books 2 and 3:** if a complete taking satisfies and a partial one does not, then **finishing is the only thing that satisfies**, and every partial Taking in Book 1 is an open job with a drive attached to it.

## Why she becomes a killer
Her method has been failing since the sealed years. She removes what breaks people and the remainder is still not the thing she is reconstructing. Eighteen months of small takings across [[Lowport]] resolved nothing.

So she increases the dose. She takes more, then she takes everything, and taking everything kills.

**She is repeating Marin exactly** — escalating in small defensible steps because the previous step did not deliver. Marin removed the limiter for the same reason. **She kills because she is a researcher with a failing protocol.**

**Three accelerants:** ingestion (rage is a dense emotional structure, so the first killing is contamination rather than decision) · frustration ([[Iris Vale]] is the first thing that ever refused her) · Marin (guilt, grief, and the specific knowledge of what she had made, taken whole).

## What she is actually building
**Not a collection of emotion. A person.** She has been assembling toward the pattern the map describes since the sealed years, and nothing she takes ever completes it, **which is why she keeps increasing the dose.**

## The face — run this ladder in order
Book 1's template stays: **"features learned from observation rather than born to them."** She is assembled from what she takes. Fragments produce a face that will not settle; whole people produce whole structure.

**She gets more beautiful, more convincing, and more human the worse she gets.**

| Where | State |
|---|---|
| Book 1 · B2 Ch1–10 | An assembly, unsettling at any distance |
| **B2 Ch11** | After [[Caleb Ward]], the first whole structure she has ever taken. Something settles |
| **B2 Ch18** | Contradictory second-hand accounts. Witnesses disagree about what they saw |
| **B2 Ch19** | **Almost.** Passes for a person to a stranger, most of a minute, ordinary daylight |
| **B2 Ch22** | Specific. A face assembled toward **one woman**, and the reader has met her |
| Book 3 Ch12 | Complete, and **[[Simon Virek]] recognizes it** |

**No character in Book 2 recognizes whose face it is.** Virek could. Virek is not in that book.

## Physical form
Female-coded, elongated — limbs slightly too long. Posture subtly off: forward tilt, predatory balance. Pale skin, almost translucent in places, faint dark filaments moving beneath. Surface alternates between smooth ceramic-like sections and matte organic patches.

- **Spine** — the most inhuman element. Raised central ridge, embedded cable-like tendrils with slight independent movement, faint pulsing when active. Most of her processing seems to happen here
- **Hands** — normal at first glance. Fingers slightly too long, joints articulate too smoothly. On contact, fingertips split microscopically and filament structures extend, barely visible
- **Internal** — layered, shifting mass. No fixed organs. Density changes with how much she has taken

## Limitations — these are the tension
1. **Requires contact.** True through Books 1 and 2. **Removed at Book 3's Act I turn**
2. **Cannot take everything** — some things resist and fracture instead of transferring
3. **Overload risk** — too much too quickly causes instability and degraded physical control
4. **Not omniscient** — she only knows what she has taken
5. **Can be damaged** — extremely resilient, but damage is possible
6. **Drawn to complexity** — which drives her targeting

**Physical rules apply absolutely.** No teleportation, no phasing, nothing supernatural. She obeys space, and never moves faster than a body can.

## Speech across the series
- **Book 1** — fragments. *"This part of you hurts." / "You don't need it." / "I can take it."*
- **Book 2** — **three lines in the entire book, all in Ch22**, and her voice is ordinary: *"You are not finished." · "It does not close." · "I do not know why."*
- **Book 3** — complete, careful sentences with every word placed. Observational, clinical, almost kind. The only real conversation in the series is Ch21
