## Summary
Weeks after [[Book 1 - The Absence]]. Taken people start dying — not killed, exactly. They stop eating, they walk into traffic, they do not seek treatment, **because nothing in them objects to anything anymore.** [[Elena Rourke]] has a mortality curve and no cause of death that fits, because the cause was removed months ago.

[[Daniel Kade]] opens the post-shutdown [[Meridian Neurological]] crates and [[Iris Vale]] does the only thing she has ever been good at: she recovers the data. What comes off the drives is **Dr. [[Marin Halloway]], her therapeutic memory editing program, and her younger sister.**

Meanwhile the entity's method is failing. She removes what breaks people and the remainder is still not the thing she is reconstructing, so she **increases the dose** — and then she starts **going back to finish jobs she left open.** [[Caleb Ward]] dies at the midpoint. Every Taken person in the published novel is now an open job.

**Iris is an open job.**

## The engine — one number
Everything is filed under **research identifiers, not names**. Iris spends eleven chapters reading a woman's complete story with a number where the name should be.

**The number is `MN-R-0338`.** [[Willow Halloway]] reads it off her own consent pack in Ch4 and calls it a parking space. **The reader knows whose number it is from Ch4. Iris does not until Ch15.** That gap is the engine, and it only runs because the string is concrete.

The string never changes and it belongs to **two people**: issued once to a veterans'-cohort patient, recycled when its allocation hold ran out, issued again to Willow at intake — and the closed record never came off the live index, because a company was dissolving. So Ch15's first join **returns a man**, provably wrong on dates. **The record that survives intact is the dead one, because nobody touched it after it closed.** None of that is ever stated.

- **Ch20 — Marin's consumption.** The longest chapter. She consents. **She is happy.** Do not undercut it
- **Ch22 — the third refusal.** Willow comes back to finish Iris and cannot, and **the failure and the plant are the same moment**: stuck on the empty place where Iris's love for her sister used to be, she is looking at the shape of a younger sister. **Lena is located.** The reader carries it into Book 3 alone

## What this book explains, and never states
- **Why the entity is called Willow** — Iris *finds* the name. Nobody explains it
- **Why she took Iris's love for Lena specifically** — recognition, not collateral. Ch21 gets to within one step and stops
- **What every environmental oddity in Book 1 Ch1 actually is**

> [!warning] Why Willow cannot complete Iris — never stated on the page
> What she removes is **the charge attached to a structure.** Iris's structure for Lena is
> still there and the charge on it is already gone, because Willow took it in Book 1.
> **There is nothing left to subtract.** She is running a procedure against a value already
> at zero, and a system with no inhibition cannot stop running a procedure that has not returned.
> **No character in Book 2 approaches this. The reader may assemble it. The prose must not help.**

## The hard gates
0. **No fragmentation.** Book 1's staccato voice does not carry into Book 2 in any form
1. **No villainy.** [[Marin Halloway]] never does the wrong thing *in a scene* — and the gate runs both ways: do not name what is missing and **do not certify what is there**
2. **No frame device inside a Recovered chapter**
3. **The reader must like [[Willow Halloway]]**
4. **Nobody explains the name**
5. **No appetite language for the entity** — satisfaction is completion, never pleasure
6. **Continuity locks from published Book 1** — see [[Continuity Locks]]

## Ends on
Willow walks away and Iris lets her and neither of them knows why. **It ends without ending.**
