**The question:** *What was taken from me?*

## Summary
[[Iris Vale]], a data recovery specialist, takes a contract to pull flagged data out of a sealed biotech facility on the [[Lowport]] coast — job ref **MN-44-7**. Inside [[Meridian Neurological]] something is still active. She makes contact, loses thirty-four minutes, and drives home unable to remember why the photograph on her dashboard matters.

What was taken is her ability to love one specific person: her sister [[Lena Vale]]. Not all love. One person.

Behavioral anomaly cases spread across the city — people who came back *less*. [[Elena Rourke]] has hundreds of them and a framework that calls it neurological and treatable. [[Daniel Kade]] has locations he recognizes from his own logistics work. Iris maps the cases and finds not a cluster but **a path**. At the midpoint [[Caleb Ward]] is Taken on the page, in his own POV, the only chapter from his perspective in the book.

She tracks the path into the outer zones and finds [[Willow]]. They stand in a dark space and look at each other. Iris loses more of herself and withdraws, and Willow lets her go, and neither the reader nor Iris is told why.

## Structure
- **Act I — Discovery** (Ch 1–7) — the condition, the pattern, and the turn: *it is something external and it moves*
- **Act II — Escalation** (Ch 8–16) — Caleb Taken at the midpoint; Iris deteriorates; the turn: ***"It's one."***
- **Act III — Contact** (Ch 17–22) — tracking, the reveal, partial loss, exit

## What it establishes for everything after
- **The horror is subtraction, not violence.** No spectacle, ever
- **Contact is required.** Book 1's tension is *don't let her touch you* — a solvable problem, which is why Book 3 takes it away
- **Nobody dies in it.** That changes in Book 2
- **Every Taken person is a partial result** — which Book 2 turns into an open job, retroactively raising the stakes of a published novel without changing a word of it
- **The name "Willow" appears only in Ch21–22, only in narration, never spoken by any character**

## Ends on
> She is closer to Willow than anyone else alive. She is also closer to Willow than she is to herself.

## Questions it leaves open
1. Why did Willow let Iris go — both times?
2. What is Iris's unique resistance?
3. Who caused the deviation? *(answered in Book 2, and Iris never learns the name)*
4. What is Willow's actual goal?
5. How much of Iris is left?
