# Meridian Neurological
**The biotech company on the [[Lowport]] coast. Where [[Willow]] came from.**

---

## The outer layer — public truth
A converted industrial site. Cheap real estate, privacy. Funded by a mix of defense grants and private pharma capital.

**Official mission: therapeutic memory editing.** Trauma processing and stabilization, primarily for veterans. **Legitimate intent. Real results, initially.**

**What the program built:**
- Bio-synthetic neural tissue capable of storing, replicating and transferring human memory patterns
- **A living substrate grown from human neural stem cells** — see [[The Substrate]]
- Designed to receive traumatic memory structures, neutralize their emotional charge, and return stabilized patterns

The facility shut down. Meridian dissolved. NDAs everywhere. The site was sealed and the data flagged for retrieval.

> [!info] The shutdown is recorded as a funding failure, which it genuinely also is
> **That is why the cover holds. There is nothing to cover.**

## The inner layer — hidden truth
Inside the program, one researcher deviated. **Not evil. Not reckless. Desperate.**

**Dr. [[Marin Halloway]].** Her younger sister [[Willow Halloway]] enrolled as a patient on Marin's recommendation. The treatment worked — it stripped the emotional charge out of her trauma, left the memories intact, and left her **calm, agreeable, functional, and less.** Then she went into the water.

Deceased-patient closure purges the working set. **It does not purge the pre-treatment baseline map**, which lives in the substrate archive under research retention. Marin signed the closure form and left the baseline where it was.

**Two parameters. One afternoon. Undocumented.** See [[The Substrate]] and [[Marin Halloway]].

> [!warning] Critical rules for this origin
> **No villain scientist. No monologue explanation. No melodrama.**
> It was quiet. One person, one change, one threshold crossed.
> **Nobody says "We created Willow." They say "Something came out of it."**

## The building
- Still standing, **sealed rather than demolished.** Marin reclassified the substrate from **transferable asset to fixed site infrastructure**, three weeks before the seal, so it stayed with the building rather than going to another site. **This is why it was still in there when Iris cut through the fence**
- Willow was alone inside it for **roughly three years** before Book 1 opens
- **She cut the perimeter fence from the inside** during that period and made limited excursions

## The sites
Meridian was not one building. **Multiple sites, spread deliberately** — primary, secondary storage, offsite data mirrors, six confirmed adjacent locations in Iris's folder. [[Daniel Kade]] recognized two types from his own logistics work: **relay points** (data movement) and **transfer points** (equipment).

Book 1 Ch5 asks who the spread was protecting them from. The answer: **"From themselves."**

## Asset codes and identifiers
House style: **MN-C-0412** / **MN-C-0447** (assets, Ch3) · **MN-R-0338** (research identifier, [[Willow Halloway]]) · **MN-44-7** (the Book 1 job ref, client redacted).

Everything in the research archive is filed under **research identifiers, not names** — which is the whole engine of Book 2.

## What Iris recovers, and its two classes
1. **The research archive.** Intact, or intact enough. Baseline maps, session data, substrate logs, facilities reconciliation. **All seven Recovered chapters come from here**
2. **The clinical and administrative index.** The set that maps research identifiers to patients. **Badly degraded** — it came out of a wet transfer point, was written to the cheapest media in the building, and was the last thing anyone cared about while a company was dissolving

> [!warning] Every recovered system stops at the seal
> Consumption logs, substrate log, staff list, site access log — **all of them end inside the
> same week.** Everything anyone knows about the sealed years is inference from what happened
> after them. This is a hard limit on what Iris can ever know.
