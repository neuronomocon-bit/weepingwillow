# The Escalation Ladder
**How the three books differ, run on parallel axes so they cannot repeat each other.**

---

## The spine

| | The threat | [[Willow]] |
|---|---|---|
| **Book 1 — The Absence** | You lose part of yourself | **Instinct.** Contact required. **Nobody dies** |
| **Book 2 — The Ending** | You lose yourself, or you die | **Method.** Over-takings leave bodies. First deliberate return. Contact still required |
| **Book 3 — The Silence** | She is coming, and she will kill you | **Strategy.** Hunting. Intent. **Contact no longer required** from the Act I turn |

## The parallel axes
- **Capability** — contact → proximity → nothing
- **Proximity of victim** — strangers → colleagues → **family**
- **Scale** — individuals → clusters → the city
- **Visibility** — hidden → hunted → public → **welcomed**
- **Number** — Book 1's *"It's one"* is taken away by Book 3 Ch17 and never given back

> [!warning] Why the contact requirement has to go
> Book 1's tension was **don't let her touch you**, which is a solvable problem.
> Once it is gone there is nowhere left to stand.

## The face runs the opposite way
She becomes **more beautiful, more convincing, and more human the worse she gets.** The horror runs backward from every other monster in fiction. See [[Willow]].

## Speech
- **Book 1** — fragments
- **Book 2** — three lines in the whole book, all in Ch22, one of them an admission of a limit
- **Book 3** — complete careful sentences. The only real conversation in the series is Ch21

## The horror lever, per book
- **Book 1** — nobody believes it is happening
- **Book 2** — the city gets the category wrong, and **having a name makes it worse, because now there is a process**
- **Book 3** — **everybody can see it and they are grateful**
