# Prose Rules
**Book 2 onward. The single source is `series-bible/03-themes-and-tone.md`.**

> [!warning] Book 1 is exempt and stays as written
> The Absence predates these rules. Its sparser, fragmented voice is intentional and is in
> print. **Do not retro-edit it.** It is also the measurement baseline — see [[Audit Method]].

---

## The hard rules
- **No default single-line fragmentation.** Fragments are emphasis only, a few per chapter at most
- **No "Not X. Just Y." / "It's not X, it's Y" contrast framing.** State the affirmation directly
- **No "wrong" / "off" as atmospheric shorthand.** Show the specific discrepancy; the reader names the wrongness
- **No "nodded once"**
- **No labeled pauses / labeled silences**
- **No em dashes** in prose or dialogue
- **No AI-writing tics**
- **No "the kind of…" / appositive-as-thesis**
- **No narrator editorializing**
- **No repeated comparative crutches**

## The two failures that are the same failure
> [!warning] Fragmentation and run-on comma chains are one rhythm repeated, from opposite directions
> **Screen the connector while drafting, not after.** The Ch2 first draft came in with ten
> sentences over the threshold **one chapter after this gate was written down**, because the
> screen was run as a post-pass instead of applied at the sentence.
>
> **Flag any sentence with 3+ commas, 3+ instances of "and," or 45+ words**, then justify or
> fix each one. Legitimate survivors: colon-introduced lists, serial verbs, deliberate
> accumulation, and dialogue where a character is genuinely spilling.

## The trailing explanatory sentence
A good image followed by a line telling the reader what to make of it. **Thirteen of these came out of Ch2 in the author's pass.**

**It passes every mechanical screen** — the sentences are well formed, varied, and not run-ons — **which is why it has to be checked by hand.**

> **The screen: read the last sentence of each paragraph alone and ask whether the paragraph
> is finished without it.** If it is, cut it.

The related failure is the trailing clause on an otherwise clean sentence.

## Narration contractions — ruled 2026-09-02
**Book 2's own register is the standard, and nothing is swept in either direction.**

Book 2 narration runs **98–100% expanded** on *did not, was not, had not, could not*. Book 1's runs 50–75% and says *didn't* 86 times in 18,953 words of narration.

> **Book 2 narration keeps the full form as its default, in both registers**, and a contraction
> in narration is the marked choice. **Dialogue is untouched and runs the other way, as it must.**
> Do not reopen this by measuring it again.

## The three tic migrations — all caused by our own rules
| Migration | Book 2 | Book 1 | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| *nobody* → *no one* → **anyone** | 18 | 1 | thirteen of them one construction |
| **, which** | 11 | 0 | the em-dash ban relocating the parenthetical |
| **copular opener** | 37 | 9 | the fragmentation ban relocating emphasis. **Half legitimate** — Book 2 writes *"It was a compliment."* where Book 1 wrote *"A compliment."* |

## US English is a hard requirement
**Fourteen categories checked across both books. Book 1 has zero hits in every one**, so any hit is a divergence from a published baseline. **Fifteen defects were found across six chapters and all fixed.**

> [!warning] Every screen we had was morphological, and these are lexical
> *nought*, *per cent*, *fortnight* got through every existing check.
> ***"Nought point four"* is not a misspelling of anything. It is the wrong word, spelled perfectly.**
> Also caught: *lift*, *queue*, *travelling*, *car park*, *straight away*, *cancelling*,
> *storeys*, *"Half eleven"*, *half six*, *half past two*, *"He was sat in the chair"*.

**Deliberately kept** — converting them is over-correction: *quarter past nine*, *twenty past five*, *twenty to four*, *twenty to six*, *ten past five*. **Only the bare *half six* form is British-only.**

**Three standing false positives — do not "fix" them:** **flat** (the adjective, this series' central word) · **"different to see it"** (the American infinitive, not the British comparative) · **"a good deal of"** (standard American).

**The lexical lists are open. Grow them every time one gets through.**

## Word overuse — a live watch item
Ch1 ran *thing* at **37.5 per 10k against Book 1's 6.0**, six times the rate, with *instrument* at 4 and *reading* at 6 where **Book 1 has zero of each.** Now 4, 1 and 2.

## Drift corrections
| Drift | The note to give |
|---|---|
| Too dramatic | *Pull back. Flatter. Iris doesn't feel this intensely* |
| Too much dialogue | *Less talking. More silence. Let the scene sit* |
| Too explanatory | *Cut the exposition. Trust the reader* |
| Willow too villainous | *Willow is calm. Observational. She believes she's right* |
| Too much action | *Slow this down. The tension is in proximity, not movement* |
| Purple prose | *Simpler language. Observations, not performances* |
| Marin as villain | *She is competent and grieving. Every step is small and defensible* |
| The lab scene as horror | *No flickering lights, no ominous readouts, no warning ignored for effect. The deviation is paperwork and an afternoon. Make it almost boring* |
| Recovered chapter as document | *Delete the frame. This is a scene* |
| The entity with appetite | *She does not hunger, crave, relish, savor or enjoy. She is finishing, not feeding* |
| Iris feeling the origin | *She understands it structurally, correctly and completely, and nothing arrives* |
| Reaching for the reader's knowledge | *If the sentence would collapse for someone who hadn't read Book 1, it's doing the reader's job* |
| Willow Halloway as symbol | *She's a person. Difficult, funny, scared. Make her annoying* |
| The name explained | *Cut it. Nobody in this series ever explains where the name came from* |
