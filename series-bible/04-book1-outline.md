# BOOK 1 — "THE FIRST CUT" — DETAILED OUTLINE

---

## PREMISE

Iris Vale, a data recovery specialist, is contracted to retrieve files from a sealed biotech facility on the Lowport coast. Inside, she encounters something still active. She survives — but something is taken from her. As behavioral anomaly cases spread across Lowport, Iris begins to piece together that they are not isolated incidents. They are the work of a single entity. By the end, she has seen it. And it has seen her again.

---

## ACT I — DISCOVERY (Chapters 1–7)

### Purpose
Introduce Iris and her condition. Establish the anomaly pattern. Hint at Willow without revealing her. Build the world of Lowport. End with Iris accepting this is not a psychological phenomenon — it's something external.

### Act I Beats

**Chapter 1 — The Meridian Job**
- Iris enters the sealed Meridian facility for a data recovery contract
- The job is routine — or should be
- Something inside the facility is wrong: ambient wrongness, environmental cues
- Contact occurs (shown in fragments — not fully depicted yet)
- Iris exits. Memory is incomplete. She knows something happened but can't fully reconstruct it.
- **Hook:** She drives home and realizes she can't remember why a photo on her dashboard matters.

**Chapter 2 — Caleb Ward**
- Introduce Caleb through a work interaction — another job, another site
- Establish his personality: sharp, conversational, alive
- He notices something off about Iris — she's slower, flatter, less reactive than he remembers
- Iris deflects. She doesn't know how to explain it.
- **Function:** Baseline for "normal human" before the horror strips it away

**Chapter 3 — The First Case**
- Iris hears about a behavioral anomaly case through Kade — someone who came back from a location "different"
- The person isn't violent, isn't distressed. They're just... less.
- Iris visits. Observes. Something about it feels familiar but she can't place why.
- **Seed:** First unconscious connection between what happened to her and what's happening to others

**Chapter 4 — Lila Mercer**
- Iris meets Lila Mercer — the first fully Taken person she interacts with directly
- Lila is calm, present, functional. Makes eye contact. Answers questions.
- But there is nothing behind it. The surface is intact. The interior is gone.
- Iris feels the wrongness viscerally — not emotionally (she can't), but physically. A pressure. A recognition.
- **Tone-setting scene:** This is what "Taken" looks like. No drama. Just absence.

**Chapter 5 — Kade**
- Daniel Kade enters more fully
- He hired Iris for the Meridian job. Now he has more work — adjacent sites, related infrastructure
- He knows something went wrong at Meridian. Doesn't know what.
- The relationship is professional, uneasy. He needs her. She needs answers.
- Hints about Meridian's scope — it wasn't a small operation
- **Function:** Expands the world, introduces the institutional cover-up layer

**Chapter 6 — Rourke**
- Dr. Elena Rourke appears — she's tracking the anomaly cases from the medical side
- She has data, framework, confidence. Her model: neurological, environmental, treatable.
- She and Iris cross paths. Rourke dismisses Iris's observations.
- Friction established — Rourke isn't wrong about the symptoms, she's wrong about the cause
- **Function:** Institutional resistance. Forces Iris to prove what she suspects.

**Chapter 7 — The Pattern**
- Iris maps the cases geographically and chronologically
- A pattern emerges — not random. Concentrated. Moving.
- She presents this to Kade. He doesn't want to believe it.
- **ACT I TURNING POINT:** Iris accepts this is not psychological, not environmental. It's something external. Something that moves.

---

## ACT II — ESCALATION (Chapters 8–16)

### Purpose
Make the threat real, personal, and irreversible. Caleb is Taken at the midpoint — the reader's emotional anchor is destroyed. Iris deteriorates. The Meridian connection surfaces. By the end of Act II, Iris understands that Willow is a single entity.

### Act II Beats (Pre-Midpoint: Chapters 8–11)

**Chapter 8 — Following the Thread**
- Iris and Kade investigate more cases together
- Each one reinforces the pattern: contact-based, localized, progressive
- Kade becomes increasingly uncomfortable — he recognizes locations from his Meridian logistics work
- **Tension:** The sites Willow is appearing near are sites Kade helped coordinate

**Chapter 9 — The City Feels It**
- Lowport scenes showing the subtle strain
- More people acting slightly off — not dramatically, just enough
- Rourke's caseload is growing. She's still confident in her model, but cracks are forming.
- Iris experiences her own symptoms worsening — emotional delay, decision instability

**Chapter 10 — Lena**
- Lena Vale appears — she's been trying to reach Iris
- The scene shows what their relationship used to be (through Lena's behavior) and what it is now (through Iris's absence)
- Lena is warm, direct, persistent. Iris is polite, correct, empty.
- Lena knows something is wrong. Iris can't explain it.
- **Emotional core:** The reader sees the gap. Lena loves her sister. Iris remembers that she should feel something back. She doesn't.

**Chapter 11 — Caleb Shift**
- Caleb on a job. Routine work in an outer zone of Lowport.
- Something subtle changes — a pause that lasts too long, a response that doesn't quite land
- The reader should feel unease before they can name it
- Caleb is still Caleb. But something is starting.
- **Function:** Slow dread. The reader knows what's coming before it arrives.

### MIDPOINT (Chapter 12)

**Chapter 12 — The Taking of Caleb Ward**
- Caleb encounters Willow. This is the first on-page Taking.
- No spectacle. No chase. It's quiet. Intimate.
- Willow touches him. He goes still. Flash of emotional exposure.
- Then it's over.
- Caleb stands. Looks at his surroundings. Functions.
- But the reader — and eventually Iris — can tell: something essential has been removed.
- **This is the scene that defines the book's horror.** Not violence. Subtraction.

### Act II Beats (Post-Midpoint: Chapters 13–16)

**Chapter 13 — After**
- Iris sees Caleb after. He recognizes her. He talks. He functions.
- But the dynamic is gone. The ease, the sharpness, the conversational rhythm — flattened.
- Iris is looking at what she herself might become.
- **Mirror:** Caleb is now what Lila Mercer was in Chapter 4, but this time the reader knew him before.

**Chapter 14 — The Weight**
- Iris processes the loss — or tries to. She can recognize it intellectually but struggles to feel the grief.
- Her own condition is worsening: more emotional delay, more gaps
- Kade notices. Doesn't say anything directly.
- Rourke's cases are multiplying. Her confidence is quieter now.

**Chapter 15 — Deterioration**
- Iris's symptoms intensify
- False certainties: she's sure of things that aren't true
- Memory gaps widening
- She begins to question whether her own investigation is reliable
- **Horror:** The person trying to stop the threat is becoming unreliable — and she knows it

**Chapter 16 — The Realization**
- Iris connects everything: the pattern, the contact mechanism, the movement, the consistency
- This is not a phenomenon. Not a disease. Not environmental contamination.
- **It is one thing. One entity. And it is choosing.**
- **ACT II TURNING POINT:** Iris understands what she's dealing with. A singular, mobile, evolving predator.

---

## ACT III — CONTACT (Chapters 17–22)

### Purpose
Direct confrontation. Iris tracks Willow, enters her territory, and encounters her face to face. The climax is not explosive — it is slow, physical, and personal. Iris survives, but loses more of herself. The book ends with Iris changed, closer to Willow than anyone else alive.

### Act III Beats

**Chapter 17 — Tracking**
- Iris uses the pattern data and her own proximity sense to track Willow's movement
- She's heading into the outer zones of Lowport — industrial, abandoned, near the water
- Kade warns her. She goes anyway.
- **Tension:** Every step closer intensifies her symptoms

**Chapter 18 — The Deeper Zone**
- Iris moves through increasingly abandoned infrastructure
- Environmental horror: empty spaces that should have people, signs of recent presence, the feeling of being observed
- Her proximity sense is constant now — pressure, disorientation, flattening
- She finds evidence of Willow's presence — not dramatic, just traces. A place where someone stood too long. A surface slightly warm.

**Chapter 19 — Closing Distance**
- Iris is close. She knows it physically before she knows it intellectually.
- The passive field is strong — she feels calm, detached, her reactions slowing
- She has to fight through it to keep moving
- **Internal horror:** Part of her doesn't want to fight it. The calm feels like relief.

**Chapter 20 — Contact**
- Iris sees Willow.
- First full reveal: dim environment, partial light, a figure that looks human until something shifts
- Willow is aware of her. Recognizes her.
- No immediate aggression. Curiosity. Assessment.
- Proximity is overwhelming — Iris's senses are compressed, dulled, narrowed
- **The scene is quiet. Two figures in a dark space. One of them is not human.**

**Chapter 21 — Partial Loss**
- Interaction escalates — not to violence, but to near-contact
- Willow reaches. Iris resists. But something transfers anyway — proximity alone seems to have an effect at this range
- Iris loses something more. Not as dramatic as the first Taking, but measurable.
- A memory goes soft. An emotional response she still had — gone.
- **Cost:** Iris got close enough to confirm everything. But the price was more of herself.

**Chapter 22 — Exit**
- Iris withdraws. Not a dramatic escape — a retreat. Willow lets her go.
- The question: why?
- Iris returns to Lowport. She is different. Quieter. Thinner in some internal way.
- Final scene: Iris sits somewhere familiar. Looks at something that should trigger a response. Nothing comes.
- **Closing beat:** She is closer to Willow than anyone else alive. She is also closer to Willow than she is to herself.

## BOOK 1 — KEY QUESTIONS LEFT OPEN

These carry into Book 2:
1. Why did Willow let Iris go — both times?
2. What is Iris's unique resistance? Why can't Willow fully resolve her?
3. Who was the Meridian researcher who caused the deviation?
4. What is Willow's actual goal — if she has one beyond feeding?
5. How much of Iris is left?
