# ARC REVISION — 2026-08-26

**Status:** Supersedes `06-book2-outline.md` entirely and the ending sections of `07-book3-outline.md`. Book 1 — The Absence is published and unchanged.

This document records the decisions. The outlines and briefs are rewritten from it, not the other way around.

---

## WHY THE PREQUEL WAS DROPPED

The 2026-08-04 pivot to a prequel fixed a real problem: the series' emotional bomb — Willow was born from grief — was being reported in a single chapter of Virek talking in a cabin rather than dramatized. That diagnosis was correct and the fix is preserved below.

What the prequel cost:

1. **No forward carry.** Marin, Willow Halloway, Feld, Terri, Pell, Raman appear in no other book. Marin is consumed at Ch16. Roughly 70,000 words of cast the series cannot spend again.
2. **Dramatic irony as sole engine.** The destination is known from page one. No question pulls the reader forward, and the Iris thread was explicitly forbidden from carrying tension ("no unease, no premonition, no Meridian").
3. **Book 3 overloaded.** The Silence was carrying the entire retired Hunger arc *plus* the original endgame in 22 chapters.

The revision keeps the dramatization and discards the stall.

---

## DECISION 1 — BOOK 2 IS A SEQUEL. THE ORIGIN IS RECOVERED.

Book 2 moves forward from the end of The Absence. Marin's origin story arrives as **fully dramatized chapters** — complete scenes, not summary, not documents — which are the material Iris is recovering off Meridian's drives.

**Craft rule: the recovery licenses the Marin chapters' existence. It does not narrate them.** Marin's chapters run as straight third-person past scenes with full interiority. The frame appears only at the seams: Iris ends a chapter opening a file, the next chapter is Marin at full immersion, and Iris's following chapter carries the reaction. No voiceover, no document formatting, no cutting back mid-scene.

**Why this works:** Iris is a data recovery specialist. The series set this up in Book 1 and never cashed it. The origin arriving through her profession is the most on-brand delivery available.

**The central image:** a woman whose grief was removed, reading a stranger's grief in perfect detail, understanding it structurally and feeling nothing. Marin's program did to Willow Halloway what the entity did to Iris. Iris is recovering the manual for her own condition.

**The recognition:** Iris assembles most of why the entity took her love for Lena and stops one inch short — or reaches it and cannot feel what it means. The reader completes it. This is tension *and* irony, where the prequel had only irony.

### The name — no contradiction

Verified in the published text: **"Willow" appears in Book 1 only in Ch21 and Ch22, and only in narration.** No character ever speaks it. The prose simply begins calling her that at the moment of contact and never explains how it knows.

Iris can therefore encounter "Willow Halloway" in the recovered files with zero contradiction — she never claimed the name, the narrator did. The reader watches a word they have read for a whole book turn out to have been a person's name.

**The old rule stands: nobody explains the name.** It is now found, not explained.

## DECISION 2 — WILLOW BECOMES A KILLER

Subtraction alone is one note, and three books of one note is a flat series. The threat level escalates.

### Why she turns — the method is failing

Established canon: the sealed years produced eighteen months of small takings across Lowport that **resolved nothing**. She removes what breaks people and the remainder is still not the thing she is reconstructing.

So she increases the dose. She takes more, then she takes everything, and taking everything kills.

**She is repeating Marin exactly** — escalating in small defensible steps because the previous step did not deliver. Marin removed the limiter for the same reason. The daughter of the program inherits the program's logic. She kills because she is a researcher with a failing protocol.

### Three accelerants

- **Ingestion.** She learns behavior from what she takes. Rage is an emotional structure, and a dense one, which is her stated selection criterion. The first killing is contamination, not decision.
- **Frustration.** Iris is the first thing that ever refused her. A system with no inhibition meeting something it cannot process generates pressure that turns outward. This makes Iris's resistance causal rather than a late-book device.
- **Marin.** She consumed a woman full of guilt, grief, and the specific knowledge of what she had made. That material is inside her.

### The escalation ladder

| | Threat | Willow |
|---|---|---|
| **Book 1** (published) | You lose part of yourself | Instinct. Contact required. Nobody dies. |
| **Book 2** | You lose yourself, or you die | Method. Over-takings leave bodies. First deliberate kill. |
| **Book 3** | She is coming, and she will kill you | Strategy. Hunting. Intent. |

### Parallel escalation axes — run these so the books cannot repeat each other

- **Capability:** contact required → proximity sufficient → no contact needed. Book 1's tension is "don't let her touch you," which is solvable. Remove the touch requirement and there is nowhere to stand.
- **Proximity:** strangers → colleagues → family
- **Scale:** individuals → clusters → the city
- **Visibility:** hidden → hunted → public → welcomed
- **Number:** Book 1 Ch16's payoff is *"It's one."* If Book 3 takes that certainty away, it detonates the beat rather than contradicting it, and opens the door the series name deliberately holds open.

### Deaths that are hers without her touching anyone

Retained from the original design and still in force: Taken people die because what was removed was keeping them alive. No fear, no self-preservation, no reason to seek treatment. **Willow Halloway drowned.** That mechanism scales to a city-wide mortality curve that Rourke cannot explain and nobody can attribute.

## DECISION 3 — SATISFACTION, NOT PLEASURE

She does not enjoy killing. **A complete taking registers as completion** — a process reaching its endpoint. Clinical throughout.

**The mechanical consequence is the engine:** if a complete taking satisfies and a partial one does not, killing becomes the only thing that satisfies. She is not feeding. She is finishing. Every partial Taking in Book 1 is an unfinished job, and she now has the drive to go back and close them.

**Do not write this as appetite, hunger, craving, or relish.** Pleasure would make her a person who enjoys things. Satisfaction keeps her a system.

---

## DECISION 4 — SHE RESOLVES PHYSICALLY

Book 1's template: "features learned from observation rather than born to them." She is assembled from what she takes. Fragments produce a face that does not settle; whole people produce whole structure.

**Progression:** an assembly → a face that almost holds → a face that passes → **a specific face.**

The completed face is Willow Halloway's. The horror runs backward from every other monster: she becomes more beautiful, more convincing, and more human the worse she gets.

**This solves Virek**, whose Book 3 role was thin. He is the only living person who ever saw Willow Halloway. He can recognize her. Nobody else in the series can.

---

## DECISION 5 — THE ENDING

**The Water, sprung by the Compulsion Trap.**

Iris opens herself completely and sets a trap baited with the only thing Willow cannot refuse. Willow reaches the irreducible core and cannot complete it — and cannot stop, because stopping is the one thing satisfaction-drive cannot do.

The attempt supplies the last material she needed for the *other* reconstruction, the one running since the sealed years. She finishes becoming Willow Halloway.

Then she stands, walks past Iris, and goes into the water, because that is what the person she just completed does.

**Iris does not kill her. Iris finishes her.**

The locked line — Willow says "Willow Halloway" once, flat, in passing — is no longer an easter egg. It is the hinge the entire ending turns on.

**Meaning:** the treatment always ended this way. It took six years and a hundred people to finish the job.

**Staging note:** a quiet drowning after a book with a body count reads as anticlimax unless the violence peaks *before* the water. Kade, Lena, and the city take the damage first, and the final confrontation is genuinely lethal with people dying in the room, so the water lands as the thing that finally stops it rather than as a soft landing.

## WHAT SURVIVES UNCHANGED

- All prose hard rules and sentence-variety gates in `03-themes-and-tone.md`
- **No villain.** Marin is competent and grieving. Every step small and defensible. No scene where she does the wrong thing.
- **No horror-movie beats in the lab.** The deviation is paperwork and an afternoon.
- **Nobody explains the name.**
- **The reader must like Willow Halloway.**
- Thematic pillars 1–5, including "Pain Has Purpose" — the escalation strengthens it rather than replacing it
- All continuity locks from published Book 1

## WHAT IS RETIRED

- Book 2 as prequel; the 12/6/3/1 thread allocation; the Iris-intact six-chapter thread
- "No Iris foreshadowing" as a Book 2 gate — she is post-Book-1 now and the rule no longer applies
- Book 3's ending as a purely passive contradiction failure
- Willow as strictly non-lethal

## DECISION 6 — IRIS ENDS AMBIGUOUS

She is alive at the end of Book 3 and **the text refuses to say how much of her is there.** Not survival-and-hollowed, not death, not recovery. The reader is given enough to argue either way and no line settles it.

This preserves the series' stated tone — "not nihilistic, but not hopeful either" — and it holds the door open for a fourth book without promising one. The series is called Weeping Willow, not a trilogy, for exactly this reason.

**Craft rule:** the ambiguity must come from what Iris does, not from the prose withholding. No coy narration, no cutting away at the decisive moment, no final line engineered to be unreadable. Show her completely and let the reader be unable to tell.

---

## DECISION 7 — CALEB IS THE BOOK 2 MIDPOINT

Willow returns to a previous subject because the partial result was unsatisfactory, and finishes him. **Caleb Ward dies at the midpoint of Book 2.**

Why this and not a new character: the reader has already grieved him once, in Book 1 Ch12, and got him back as a diminished version they had to accept. Killing that version takes something twice.

**What it does to the published book:** every Taken person in The Absence becomes a walking open job. Lila Mercer is in danger. So is everyone else she touched. It reaches backward into a published novel and raises its stakes retroactively without altering a word.

**What it does to Iris:** she is partially Taken. She now knows what a second visit is for, and that she is owed one.
