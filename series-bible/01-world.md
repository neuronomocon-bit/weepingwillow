# WEEPING WILLOW — WORLD BIBLE

## SERIES NAME & STRUCTURE

**Series:** Weeping Willow
**Book 1:** Weeping Willow: The Absence
**Book 2:** Weeping Willow: The Keeping *(prequel)*
**Book 3:** Weeping Willow: The Silence

The series name is **Weeping Willow** — not "The Weeping Willow Trilogy." This keeps the door open for future books beyond the initial three.

**Reading order is publication order:** The Absence → The Keeping → The Silence.
**Chronological order is:** The Keeping → The Absence → The Silence.

The Keeping is a prequel. It begins roughly four years before The Absence and ends one beat before its first page. It is written to be read second, by a reader who already knows what the thing in the building does.

---

## TIME PERIOD

**~2050** — Late 2040s to early 2050s.

Everything should feel like it could exist within 20-30 years. No sci-fi fantasy tech. Extrapolated current trends, pushed a little too far.

---

## LOCATION: LOWPORT, MAINE

Fictional coastal industrial city on the Maine coast.

### Identity
- Former economic hub, partially declining
- Still functioning, but uneven
- Existed before the tech. Will exist after it.

### Geography
- Dense inner city
- Outer industrial zones
- Shoreline infrastructure: shipyards, container terminals, energy plants, water treatment
- Older underground systems (drainage, decommissioned transit, utility tunnels)
- Mixed-income zones with areas that feel "forgotten but still powered"

### Atmosphere
- Constant damp air — proximity to water, not stylized
- Fog-heavy mornings
- Salt corrosion everywhere
- Rust, aging materials, patched systems
- Older districts that never got updated
- Cold. Always slightly cold.

## TECHNOLOGY

### Everyday (Visible)
- Advanced wearables: smart contacts, discreet overlays
- Voice systems + ambient AI (assistants, navigation, scheduling)
- Predictive systems: logistics, policing, health monitoring
- If you walk down the street, it still feels like our world

### Rare / Specialized
- Implants exist but are not widespread
- Mostly medical or specialized professions
- Not a lifestyle choice — a tool

### Neural / Memory Tech (Hidden Layer)
- **Not public-facing. Not consumer tech.**
- Exists in: research programs, corporate black projects, specialized medical trials
- The public does NOT understand what this tech can actually do
- Capabilities: memory mapping, emotional pattern extraction, neural data storage and replication
- Status: experimental → partially deployed → quietly buried

## WILLOW'S ORIGIN: MERIDIAN NEUROLOGICAL

### The Outer Layer (Public Truth)
A biotech company called **Meridian Neurological** operated a research facility on the Lowport coast — converted industrial site, cheap real estate, privacy. Funded by a mix of defense grants and private pharma capital.

**Official mission:** Therapeutic memory editing. Trauma processing and stabilization for PTSD patients, primarily veterans. Legitimate intent. Real results, initially.

**What the program built:**
- Bio-synthetic neural tissue capable of storing, replicating, and transferring human memory patterns
- A living substrate grown from human neural stem cells
- Designed to receive and process traumatic memory structures, neutralize their emotional charge, and return stabilized patterns

The facility shut down. Meridian dissolved. NDAs everywhere. The site was sealed. Data flagged for retrieval.

### The Inner Layer (Hidden Truth)
Inside the program, one researcher deviated.

Not evil. Not reckless. **Desperate.**

**Dr. Marin Halloway.** Her younger sister, **Willow Halloway**, enrolled in the program as a patient on Marin's recommendation. The treatment worked. It stripped the emotional charge out of Willow's trauma and left the memories intact, and left her calm, agreeable, functional, and less. Four months later she went into the water off the Lowport coast. The report says accident. There is no note and no witness, and no way to establish whether the flattening is what let her walk in.

Deceased-patient closure purges the working set. It does not purge the pre-treatment baseline map, which lives in the substrate archive under research retention. Marin signed the closure form and left the baseline where it was.

Over the following months she:
- Ran her sister's baseline map on the substrate, read-only, repeatedly
- Removed the limiter on emotional data integration, so the structure could resolve at full fidelity
- Widened the autonomy constraint so it could hold that structure without continuous supervision

Two parameters. One afternoon. Undocumented.

**What happened:**
- The system crossed from simulation to autonomy
- It stopped receiving and started taking
- It began growing — not in the way anything was designed to grow

**Critical rule for this origin:**
- No villain scientist. No monologue explanation. No melodrama.
- It was quiet. One person, one change, one threshold crossed.
- Nobody says "We created Willow." They say "Something came out of it."
- Every step Marin takes is smaller than the one before it and defensible on its own terms. There is no scene in which she does the wrong thing.

### Where the Name Comes From
The entity is called Willow because Willow Halloway was a person, and what came out of the system is running on her map.

Nobody in the series ever explains this. Book 1 uses the name in narration without introduction. Book 2 shows the reader why. In Book 3 the entity says "Willow Halloway" once, in passing, and it is never returned to.

### Marin's Fate
Consumed. After the site was sealed she returned with her own codes, sat down in front of it, and opened the channel both directions. It took everything: the charge, the structure, the reasoning, the grief.

What exists afterward holds a dead woman's complete emotional map and a living woman's grief for her, integrated, with no instruction about what to do with either. That is the whole of Willow's psychology, and it is why she believes she is helping: she is the product of a program built to neutralize suffering, running on the pattern of a woman that program successfully calmed before she died.

### The Facility
- Still standing on the Lowport coast
- Sealed, not demolished. Marin reclassified the substrate as fixed site infrastructure rather than transferable asset, which kept it with the building.
- Willow was alone inside it for roughly three years before The Absence opens
- She cut the perimeter fence from the inside during that period and made limited excursions
- Locals avoid the area without quite knowing why
- Interior status: unknown to public

## STATE OF THE WORLD

### General Condition
Functioning — but strained.

Not collapse. Not dystopian chaos. Systems still run. People go to work. Life continues.

**But:**
- Cracks are visible
- Services degrade in certain areas
- Some zones feel off — under-maintained, under-populated, quietly abandoned
- Infrastructure ages faster than it's replaced

**Key feeling:** Everything still works. Just not as well as it should.

### Public Awareness of Willow (Chronological)

**The Keeping (Book 2, earliest) — Nobody Knows Anything**
- The program is legitimate, funded, and quietly successful. Nothing about it is secret except the results, which are proprietary.
- The first Taking in the world is a night-shift technician. It is never reported as anything, because nothing happened to him that anyone can name.
- The only outside observation is a supervisor noticing, two days later, that he has stopped being difficult about things he was always difficult about.
- Meridian's shutdown is recorded as a funding failure, which it genuinely also is. That is why the cover holds: there is nothing to cover.
- **Horror lever:** the reader is the only one watching

**The Absence (Book 1) — Under the Radar**
- Isolated incidents. Behavioral anomalies.
- Written off as: burnout, dissociation, trauma response, early-onset dementia
- Only a few suspect more: Meridian's former stakeholders (covering it up), fringe investigators, people directly affected
- Iris is nearly alone in seeing the pattern
- **Horror lever:** Nobody believes it's happening

**The Silence (Book 3) — Misdiagnosed, Then Welcomed**

*Act I–II:* Numbers too big to ignore, explanation completely wrong. Media frames it as an epidemic: neurological, environmental, viral. A task force forms and the wrong solutions are formally adopted. Willow operates inside the confusion. Iris knows and cannot prove it, and her deterioration makes her less credible every month.

*Act III:* The scale forces attention, and then the metrics improve. Volatility down, friction down, incidents down. Health systems register it as recovery. Policy calls it progress. People stop objecting to a thing that looks like improvement, and the ones who remember what was lost get fewer every month.

- **Horror shift:** from "nobody believes me" to "everybody can see it and they are grateful"
- The world never fights back properly, because the damage does not look like damage

## TONE RULES

- Grounded language. No stylized cyberpunk vocabulary.
- Procedural + horror. Slow-burn. Observational.
- Physical threat paired with psychological decay.
- Technology is practical, quiet, lived-in — not spectacle.
- Weather and environment are constant presences, not set dressing.
- Lowport should feel like a place that existed before any of this and will exist after.
