## TARGET LENGTH

**78,000–85,000 words.** Roughly 3,600 per chapter. The per-chapter budgets below total 79,800.

Book 1 came in at 23,405 across 22 chapters, roughly 1,060 each. That was a consequence of its fragmented voice: a page of single-line paragraphs holds a fraction of what a page of joined prose holds. This book gets its length from fuller scenes, not padding.

**Where the length comes from, legitimately:** environmental description written as flowing sentences instead of noun lists; scenes that play out rather than being summarized; dialogue with actual back-and-forth, including the parts that go nowhere; interiority in full sentences rather than clipped diagnostic lines.

**Where it must not come from:** restating an observation that already landed; atmosphere for its own sake after the location is established; Iris-style clinical logging used as filler.

**A chapter under 2,000 words has been written in Book 1's voice and needs rewriting, not expanding.**

**Length drift is the established failure.** Every chapter drafted so far landed under budget — 3,286 against 3,800, 2,855 against 3,200, 3,387 against 3,800 — and every cut was correct and nothing was restored. The fix is fuller scenes at the drafting stage, not padding afterward. Assess at the end of each act.

## PROSE DIRECTIVE

The full gates live in `03-themes-and-tone.md` → SENTENCE VARIETY and PROSE CONVENTIONS (HARD RULES). The three that have actually cost rewrites:

**1. No fragmentation.** Book 1's staccato voice is published and stays. It does not carry into this book in any form. Single-line fragment paragraphs are emphasis only, a few per chapter at most.

> **Banned as default:** The lab. Cold. Every third light on. Racks in rows. She stood in the doorway. Waited.
>
> **Required:** The lab held its cold the way the whole lower level did, steady and without source, and only every third fixture was lit, so the racks ran off into bands of shadow that made the room look longer than it was. She stood in the doorway until her eyes adjusted.

**2. No run-on comma chains.** The overcorrection, and it has to be screened at the sentence, not in a post-pass. Flag any sentence with 3+ commas, 3+ instances of "and," or 45+ words, then justify or fix it. Legitimate survivors: colon-introduced lists, serial verbs, deliberate accumulation, and dialogue where a character is genuinely spilling.

**3. The trailing explanatory sentence.** A good image followed by a line telling the reader what to make of it. It passes every mechanical screen, so it is checked by hand: read the last sentence of each paragraph alone and ask whether the paragraph is finished without it. If it is, cut it. Thirteen came out of one drafted chapter in the author's pass and four more out of another.

The worst instance so far, and the shape to watch for: *"Marin thought so at the time and would have said so if anybody had asked her."* Both halves point forward and signal that this gets revisited, which is dramatic irony leaking into the prose. It became "It was a good answer."

**Highest-risk chapters for fragmentation:** 11 (Caleb POV), 20 (the consumption), 22 (contact). Grief, contact, and non-human perception will all tempt line breaks for weight. The weight comes from what is described.

## THE TWO THREADS

**Present 15 · Recovered 7 = 22.** By POV: Iris 14 · Marin 7 · Caleb 1.

### Thread A — Present (15 chapters)

Iris, Lowport, weeks after The Absence. Carries all the plot, all the tension, and the clock.

**Her arc:** compensating → recovering → understanding → recognizing → owed a second visit.

### Thread B — Recovered (7 chapters)

Marin Halloway's story in **full dramatized scene**: third person past, complete interiority, no framing device inside the chapter.

**The craft rule, restated because it is the whole design: the recovery licenses these chapters' existence. It does not narrate them.** No voiceover, no document formatting, no timestamps, no file headers, no cutting back to Iris mid-scene. Iris ends a chapter opening a file; the next chapter is Marin, whole; Iris's next chapter carries the reaction.

**Do not attempt to account for these chapters in-world.** Nobody asks how a waiting-room conversation got onto a drive. The recovery is a licence, the way a flashback is a licence. The moment the book starts explaining its own delivery mechanism, the mechanism is all the reader will see.

The one deliberate exception is Ch15, the Act II turn, where the threads touch inside a chapter for the first and only time. That is a structural event, and it works because fourteen chapters established the separation. See the brief.

## CONTINUITY SPINE

### Willow's escalation across this book

| Stage | Chapters | What she is doing |

| Completing by neglect | 2 | Nothing new. Book 1's partial takings are finishing themselves, because the removed material turns out to have been load-bearing. She is not present for these. |
| Over-taking | 6, 18 | Increasing the dose. Everything removed at once. The body continues for hours and then stops. |
| Returning | 11 | Going back to a previous subject because the partial result was unsatisfactory. The first deliberate killing. |

| Failing | 22 | The third refusal, and the first thing she has ever been unable to complete. |

**Contact is still required in this book.** That axis does not move until Book 3. What escalates here is dose, deliberation, and visibility.

### Her face

Book 1's template stays: "features learned from observation rather than born to them." Whole people produce whole structure, so the resolution tracks the killings.

| Chapter | State |
|---|---|
| 11 | Better than Book 1 and still an assembly. Caleb has nothing left in him to be unsettled by it. |
| 18 | Described at second hand, by witnesses who disagree with each other about what they saw. |
| 19 | Passes for several seconds at conversational distance. Iris identifies her visually before her body reacts. |
| 22 | Close enough that Iris can see it is a specific face and not a general one. **Iris cannot know whose.** |

### No appetite language, anywhere

Satisfaction is completion, not pleasure. Never hunger, craving, relish, savoring, enjoyment, or appetite, in narration or in figurative description of her. A complete taking registers as a process reaching its endpoint. She is finishing, not feeding.

### Locked from published Book 1

Cut fence, forced outward, "not recently" · selective neglect, thin dust, no drifted paper · broad faint dust disruptions matching no maintenance route · standby power rising and falling, relays "not synchronized, not random either" · wall calendar stopped three years earlier · side service door, dead keypad, mechanical lock, bolt "louder than it should have been" · job ref MN-44-7 with redacted client · "Meridian didn't shut down clean" · multiple sites spread deliberately, "From themselves" · Kade's access codes never revoked · relay points and transfer points · Iris "closer to Willow than anyone else alive" · **"Willow" appears in Book 1 only in Ch21 and Ch22, and only in narration. No character ever speaks it.**

### The name — how it is found and never explained

Iris has never had a word for the thing. In Book 1 she called it "it" and "one"; the narrator supplied the name and never accounted for it.

The sequence is: **Ch15** she reads "Willow Halloway" as a patient name and it means nothing to her. **Ch16** she derives, correctly and from the records, that the thing in the building is structurally that patient, and relabels the marker on her map. She never says the word aloud and nobody says it to her.

**Nobody explains the name in any chapter, ever.** The reader detonates it. The prose must not help.

### Chapter 1 — Aftermath

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Year 0, three weeks after Book 1 · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Iris's apartment, Lowport. The drive inland. Caleb's building near the outer maintenance yards.
**Goal:** Show what she is now, show the scaffolding she has built to stay operational, and show Caleb settled at the level Willow left him.

**Conflict:** The apartment has organized itself around the work, with a second layer over the first: index cards along the doorframe, paper clipped to the table edge, and a list beside the light switch in her own handwriting. *Water. Coffee. Check device. Check the map. Eat.* She reads it every morning because she wrote it so that she would. Left to her own sequence she arrives at the end of a task without a usable account of the middle.

Then the card she does not remember writing. Pinned at eye height where she will pass it on the way out. *See Caleb. Today.* The date on the back is last night's. She can reconstruct the shape of the evening and not its content: the table, the screens, and at some point a decision that Caleb mattered enough to fix in writing. The decision was hers. The reasoning did not stay.

She reduces it to two possibilities, because reduction is the method she still trusts. Either the note is sound and she has lost the path back to it, or the note is a product of the same erosion that made her write it down. Neither is testable from inside the apartment. What she has is a card in her own hand telling her to do a small verifiable thing, set against her own live judgment, which she trusts less every week. The card is the stronger source.

At Caleb's she runs the assessment she used on Joel and on Lila: sleep, memory, appetite, the small structural questions. The answers are correct and none of them extend past the edge of the question. He asks how she is doing, and the question is placed correctly, in the right tone, at the right point in the visit. She looks for the thing underneath it and the question does not have one.

**Outcome:** Caleb is stable. Three weeks on, he has settled where Willow left him — no further loss, no recovery. From outside, the settling looks like a man who has become calm. The note was sound.

**Emotional Beat:** In the van afterward she can build a case for the note and cannot confirm it is the case she held last night. She arrived at a logic that fits and filled the space with something that works. Caleb does the same with the space behind his questions.

**Continuity:** Three weeks since the outer zone. Lena's message thread, unanswered, stacked back to the night in the café. Kade sends coordinates without context, which is the arrangement. Iris's proximity sense is quiet in this chapter and she does not comment on that.

**Craft:** This is a re-entry chapter for readers who finished Book 1 some time ago. It re-establishes her condition through operation, not summary. **No recap paragraph anywhere.** Nothing in it may explain Book 1 to the reader.

**Salvage:** `archive/DRAFT-hunger-ch01-aftermath.md` matches this brief closely at roughly 1,500 words. It is a good draft in the wrong voice register in places, and it wants expansion to budget through the apartment description as flowing prose, a fuller drive, a longer Caleb scene, and the closing van beat. **Do not expand it by adding reflection.** Add scene.

### Chapter 2 — Mortality

**POV:** Iris · **When:** +3 days · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Rourke's building. A records room or a shared office, not a morgue.
**Goal:** Change the threat. Establish that Taken people are dying, and that nothing killed them.

**Conflict:** Rourke has a curve she cannot explain and has agreed to see Iris because the curve is worse than her opinion of Iris. Her caseload from Book 1 — behavioral flattening, dissociative presentation, emotional attenuation, hundreds of files, no full recoveries — has begun producing deaths at a rate her category does not predict.

None of the deaths have a cause that fits. A man stopped eating over eleven days and did not report being unwell, because he was not distressed by it. A woman walked into traffic at a crossing she had used daily for nine years, without hurrying and without stopping. Three did not seek treatment for conditions that were treatable, and answered every question honestly when finally asked, and had simply never been moved to go.

**Lila Mercer is on the list.** The reader met her in Book 1 Ch4, polite and correct and hollow. She died of dehydration in her own apartment with water in the taps. Rourke's file calls it failure to self-advocate.

Rourke is still in her Book 1 posture and the ground is moving under it. She will not accept Iris's frame and she has run out of her own. What she says is that these are not suicides, because a suicide requires a decision, and there is no evidence any decision was made about anything.

**Outcome:** The mechanism is on the table and nobody in the room can name the cause: the thing that was removed from these people was keeping them alive. No fear, no self-preservation, no objection to anything, including their own condition. Book 1's damage is now lethal on a delay.

**Emotional Beat:** Iris reads Lila's file. She can reconstruct exactly what it should cost her and nothing arrives. She notices that the reconstruction is getting faster and more accurate as the response gets smaller.

**Continuity:** Rourke's framework and language from Book 1 Ch6 and Ch14 carry directly. She uses question marks; Iris does not. Rourke's steadiness has tightened again rather than cracked — she is not ready to break, and this book must not break her. Book 3 does that.

**Craft:** No autopsy scene, no bodies on the page, no procedural spectacle. This is two people and a list of the ordinary ways a person stops. **The horror is administrative.** Resist the urge to have anyone say what the mechanism is out loud; the reader assembles it from five case summaries.

### Chapter 3 — The Files

**POV:** Iris · **When:** +3 days · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** A rented lockup on the industrial side of Lowport. Cold, concrete, roller door, one working strip light.
**Goal:** Open the Meridian material, establish what exists and where, and set the recovery job that structures the rest of the book.

**Conflict:** Kade has been sitting on this since Book 1 Ch8, when he recognized the sites from his own logistics work and said he moved things through them without asking what was in them. He has now opened the unit and laid it out: crates with site codes, manifests, hard drives in anti-static sleeves, and the sibling contracts to MN-44-7 — the job with the redacted client he sent Iris on in the first place.

He walks her through it and does not stop talking, which is unlike him. He is asking to be forgiven and calling it work. Iris hears the request accurately and has nothing to answer it with, so she answers the work instead, which is not unkind and is not kind either.

What is actually here is administrative: HR, procurement, facilities, asset schedules, badge logs. Useful and thin. **The research archive is not here.** It went to a data mirror site during the decommissioning, one of the deliberately spread locations, and that site's lower level has been under water for years.

Kade's access codes still work, which he says without pleasure. He knows what it means that nobody ever revoked them.

**Outcome:** The job exists. Iris will go into the water and get the archive out.

**Emotional Beat:** Kade says something close to a confession and Iris responds correctly and on time. Afterward she is aware that the response was assembled, and aware that he could not tell, and cannot locate an opinion about either.

**Continuity:** All Book 1 Ch5 and Ch8 locks land here: "Meridian didn't shut down clean," the sites spread deliberately, "From themselves," relay points and transfer points, live access codes, MN-44-7 with the client field redacted. Terri Roth's name may appear on an HR schedule. It is not remarked on and nobody knows who she is.

**Craft:** Kade is warm and uses question marks. Do not let him deliver exposition in a monologue; the information comes out of him in pieces while he is doing something with his hands. **The trailing-explanation gate is at its highest risk here** — every crate wants a sentence explaining what a crate means.

### Chapter 4 — RECOVERED: Intake

**POV:** Marin Halloway · **When:** Year −6 · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** Meridian Neurological, Lowport coast. Reception, intake suite, the corridor after.
**Goal:** Establish the program as legitimate and working, and introduce both sisters through the act of one admitting the other.

**Conflict:** Marin walks Willow through intake for a program Marin helped build: the forms, the consent language, the baseline mapping session that will take most of the afternoon. Willow is difficult in the specific way of someone who agreed to something and is regretting the agreement in real time. She reads the consent form aloud in a stupid voice. She asks the intake nurse questions designed to be funny. She is deflecting and both sisters know it.

The building is shown properly here, because the reader has already walked through its ruin in Book 1 and will spend six more chapters in it alive.

**The exchange the chapter is built on:** Willow asks whether it will change her. Marin gives the honest clinical answer, that it removes the charge and leaves the memory. Willow says that sounds like the same thing. Marin explains why it is not, and **she is genuinely persuasive.** This is the thesis of the entire series being argued correctly, by a competent person, in a waiting room, to someone who is about to be proved right.

**Outcome:** Willow enrolls. The reader has met a person, not a case file.

**Emotional Beat:** In the corridor afterward Marin identifies a feeling precisely and does not examine it further: relief. She has been carrying her sister for nine years and someone else is going to hold it now.

**Continuity:** Willow's trauma is long-standing and has cost her years. **The originating event is never narrated anywhere in this book.** The reader sees only its shape: the jobs that did not last, the apartment she has been in too long, the way Marin talks to her. The program's cohort has expanded past its original veteran population and Willow qualifies under that expansion.

**Craft:** No dread, no ominous equipment, no foreshadowing, no lingering shot of anything. The reader must like Willow immediately and must believe the science. Both are load-bearing.

*Approach and site.* Fog to mid-morning, wipers clearing salt rather than rain. ### Chapter 5 — Read

**POV:** Iris · **When:** +3 days · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** The flooded data mirror site, lower level. Then her apartment.
**Goal:** Get the archive out, and put the first Recovered chapter into Iris's hands so the reader learns what she does with it.

**Conflict, first half — the job.** This is Iris doing the thing the series established her for in Book 1 and never showed. She is competent here and it should be a pleasure to read, which is the only pleasure in the book.

The sub-level has been under a foot and a half of water for years. The material is salvageable and the way you lose it is by being in a hurry: you do not power a wet drive, you do not dry one with heat, and you do not trust a bridge that has been sitting in damp air. She builds the extraction the slow way. The bridge trips at ninety minutes and she resets it and loses nothing, because she planned for ninety minutes.

**R. Oyelaran** appears here, as a name on maintenance notes going back years — a facilities technician who filed the same escalation about the sub-level pump four times and was closed out four times. Iris reads all four. Nobody did anything wrong that she can point at. Oyelaran never appears in person, in this book or any other.

**Conflict, second half — the read.** Back at the apartment, the archive begins surfacing. Most of it is nothing: procurement, calibration runs, a decade of ordinary institutional life. Then the material starts resolving into a person's work, and the person is careful, and the person is grieving in a vocabulary that measures things.

**Outcome:** Iris has the intake material. She now knows there was a program, that it worked, and that the researcher who ran it admitted her own sister to it.

**Emotional Beat, and the center of the book:** a woman whose grief was removed reads a stranger's grief in perfect detail and feels nothing. She reconstructs Marin's relief in the corridor correctly and completely — what it was, why it came, what it cost to have needed it — and nothing arrives. She is reading the manual for her own condition and cannot tell.

**Craft:** **Iris does not grieve on the page. Ever, in this book.** She understands structurally, correctly, and completely, and the response does not come. Do not let a single sentence hint that it almost does. And do not let the prose reach for the reader's knowledge: no line may nudge the connection between Marin's program and what happened to Iris.

**Note:** the flooded sub-level material is salvaged from the retired `chapters-book2/02-recovery.md`, which built it around intact pre-Book-1 Iris. The job survives. The Iris in it does not — she is flat here, and the competence reads differently when nothing is riding on it emotionally.

### Chapter 6 — Too Much

**POV:** Iris · **When:** +3 days · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** An apartment on the inland side of Lowport. Ordinary, tidy, occupied.
**Goal:** The first death that is unambiguously a killing, and the first evidence of the increased dose.

**Conflict:** **Adele Cray** cared for her dying mother for two years in this apartment. The mother died six weeks ago. Adele had not yet moved anything.

She was found sitting upright in a chair, awake, and stayed that way for eleven hours. Not unconscious. Not distressed. Present, responsive to sound, and entirely without instruction — nothing in her initiated anything, including the things that keep a body going. She did not eat, did not stand, did not speak unless spoken to, and did not object to any of it. Then, some time in the evening, it stopped.

The distinction from Ch2 matters and the chapter has to make it without stating it. Those deaths took weeks and were the arithmetic of Book 1's subtraction completing. This one took a day, and it took a day because everything was removed at once.

Iris walks the apartment. The mother's oxygen concentrator is still plugged in in the next room, running, because nobody had unplugged it.

**Outcome:** The threat has changed. It is not only taking now.

**Emotional Beat:** Iris runs her proximity sense in the room and gets a residue: faint, located, and older than a day. She was here. The reading is the strongest since Book 1 and Iris registers it as a measurement.

**Continuity:** Adele Cray is not in Rourke's caseload. She was never partially Taken. This is a first contact that went all the way, which is why it is different from a second visit, and Iris does not have the second-visit concept yet — that arrives in Ch14.

### Chapter 17 — RECOVERED: The Limiter

**POV:** Marin · **When:** Year −4.6 · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Meridian, the substrate lab on the lower level, over one afternoon. Then upstairs.
**Goal:** The deviation. It must be almost boring.

**Conflict, first half — the slide, compressed and shown, not summarized.** Six months of small steps, each smaller than the last and each defensible on its own terms. **Conflict:** The deaths have passed the threshold where a city can not-notice them. Nine in eleven days, spread across neighborhoods, with no shared exposure, no shared workplace, and no shared anything except a profile nobody outside this book can see.

The institutional answer arrives in the shape institutional answers arrive in: a probable environmental factor, an advisory about an old industrial corridor, a number to call, and a request not to speculate. It is not stupid. It is the best available explanation for people who cannot be told the actual one.

**Rourke reads the statement.** She is publicly confident and word-perfect. Iris watches her do it. Afterward, in a corridor, Rourke says something short and completely unguarded, and it is the last thing she says on the subject in this book.

**The second named victim, and the selection criterion made visible:** the dead are all people who were carrying a great deal. A man three weeks into a divorce he did not want. A woman who had been running her father's business since he stopped recognizing her. Nobody assembling the public explanation can see the pattern, because the pattern is not in any of the fields they collect.

**And the approach changes.** These are daylight contacts in occupied places — a stairwell with people two floors up, a parking structure at four in the afternoon. Contact is still required and she is no longer hiding to get it.

There are three witnesses across the nine and they contradict each other about what they saw. One says a woman in her thirties. One says someone tall. One says he thought it was somebody he knew and then it was not, and cannot get further than that.

**Outcome:** The city is engaged with the wrong problem, competently. Iris is now the only person operating on the correct one, and Rourke's data, given off the record, is the only institutional support she will get.

**Emotional Beat:** Iris sits in a public briefing while a room full of professional people are wrong, and cannot generate frustration about it. She notes that she would once have found this intolerable and cannot reconstruct what intolerable felt like. She takes accurate notes.

**Continuity:** Rourke's Book 1 framework is now something she maintains rather than believes. Book 3's public arc — the task force, the misdiagnosis formally adopted, the city eventually welcoming it — must **not** be spent here. This book gets an advisory and a number to call. That is all.

**Craft:** Do not write a panicking city. Lowport is not panicking. It is doing paperwork about a health advisory, which is what cities actually do. The three contradictory witness accounts are the chapter's only description of Willow and must never be reconciled.

### Chapter 19 — Almost

**POV:** Iris · **When:** +5 days · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** A public interior in daylight. Then the recovered material.
**Goal:** She nearly passes for a person. And the last two records the book needs.

**Conflict, first half — the sighting.** Iris sees her. Across a room with other people in it, in the middle of an ordinary afternoon, and for several seconds Iris does not identify her as anything except a woman standing there.

**That is the horror of the chapter and it is built on an inversion.** In Book 1 Iris's body always knew first: the pressure in her head, the flattening, the proximity sense. Here the visual identification comes first and the proximity sense arrives behind it, late, the way everything else in her arrives late now. Her most reliable instrument has joined the general delay.

The face is not right, and the reader learns that only through what is wrong at close range — the micro-delay in expression, the tracking that is too precise. At conversational distance and for a few seconds, it holds.

**And she waits.** Willow looks at Iris and does nothing. She is not surprised. She does not approach and she does not withdraw. Then she leaves, unhurried, through a door with people going through it.

Iris reads it correctly and coldly: she was not avoided and she was not attempted. She was deferred.

**Conflict, second half — two records.** In the recovered material, two things surface that Iris files without being able to use.

**The Virek trace.** An escalation raised once, six years ago, by a researcher on the substrate side: unaccounted integration events and an autonomy constraint that does not match its logged value. The program director's response is on the file and is entirely reasonable — the site is consolidating, the wind-down completes next spring, everything in the substrate is scheduled for termination. The ticket is closed by the person who raised it, with no complaint. **That is the whole record.** A man noticed, asked once, got a good answer, and stopped.

**The asset line.** Eleven weeks before the seal, one line on a schedule was moved from portable research equipment to fixed site infrastructure. Portable equipment goes to the other sites. Fixed infrastructure stays with the building. The change is signed by M. Halloway and is the reason the substrate was still in there when Iris cut through the fence.

**Outcome:** Iris has a name for the witness and no way to find him. She has the mechanism by which the thing was left in the building. And she has been seen, and let alone, deliberately.

**Emotional Beat:** She works out what deferral means and gets it right on the first pass. She is not the hardest job available. She is the one being saved for last, because completing her is the one that resolves something, and it will be attempted when there is nothing easier to finish first.

**Continuity:** Virek is **not found** in this book and does not appear. Book 3 Ch10 and Ch12 own that. Do not make his ticket poignant; it is a closed ticket. Raman's answer stays correct.

**Craft:** The sighting is short and quiet and there is no pursuit. Nobody runs. **No chase scene exists anywhere in this series.** The tension is in proximity, not movement.

### Chapter 20 — RECOVERED: The Last Session

**POV:** Marin · **When:** Year −3 · **Length:** 4,400 — **longest chapter in the book**
**Location:** The sealed Meridian building, the lower level, the substrate lab.
**Goal:** Marin is consumed, entire and consented, and she is happy, and the prose does not undercut it.

**Conflict:** She comes back to a sealed building. She has been coming for months — the seal is administrative and she knows the side service door and the mechanical lock, and the reader has walked through this exact route in Book 1 Ch1 from the other end of three years.

The intervening period arrives through what she does, not through summary. She knows where the light works. She has a routine. She stopped recording the sessions a long time ago. She knows about Pell and said nothing, because saying it means termination, and the shape of that knowledge is in how she moves and never in a paragraph explaining it. The asset schedule is already signed. She made sure it would stay here.

**What is down there.** Follow the physical progression exactly: contained biological mass on a support frame, not humanoid, capable of local movement and of extending far enough to reach someone who comes close. It does not pursue. It cannot.

**She sits down in front of it.** The contact is hers. It has never once been otherwise.

**She has understood for weeks that she is not going to be able to leave, and has decided that this is acceptable.** That decision happened off the page, before the chapter starts. She does not deliberate here.

**She opens the channel both directions.** The limiter has been off for one identifier for two years. She takes herself off it too.

**What it takes:** everything. Not just the charge. The structure, the reasoning, the grief, the two years, the nine years before that.

**Outcome:** Marin is gone. What remains in that building now holds a dead woman's complete map and a living woman's entire grief for her, integrated, with no instruction about what to do with either.

**Emotional Beat, and the book is built to deliver this one thing:** her last awareness is that it is doing to her exactly what the program was designed to do, correctly and gently, and that the relief is enormous.

**She is happy. Do not undercut it.** No terror at the end. No regret. No flash of understanding what she has made. No line that lets the reader off. She gets what she wanted and what she wanted was to stop carrying it, and she stops.

If she speaks at all it is one word and it is her sister's name.

**Craft:** **The most tender scene in the series and the most horrifying, and those are the same fact.** Do not choose one. Write the tenderness completely and let the horror be entirely the reader's.

**Fragmentation risk is at maximum here** and the temptation will be to break lines for weight through the final pages. Refuse it. This scene is written in full, joined, unhurried sentences all the way to the last one.

**What is in that room is not her sister**, and Marin is not confused about this, and she goes anyway, and the prose never clarifies the distinction on her behalf.

**No horror imagery.** No writhing, no reaching, no wet sounds, no description that asks the reader to be disgusted. It is quiet and it is gentle and that is what makes it unbearable.

### Chapter 21 — Why Lena

**POV:** Iris · **When:** +6 days · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** Iris's apartment. Lena present for part of it.
**Goal:** Iris assembles all of it but the last step, and stops one inch short.

**Conflict:** She has everything now and she puts it in order.

The thing was grown from the complete emotional map of a younger sister. Into that was integrated the entire grief of an older sister for her. It selects for emotional density, and density is the criterion because the map is dense and nothing else matches it. It takes what it recognizes.

And in Book 1 it reached into Iris and took one thing: her love for her younger sister.

**She has all four facts. She writes them on four cards and puts them on the wall in order.** She looks at them. They are correct, they are complete, and they do not resolve into the fifth thing, because the fifth thing is not a deduction. It is a recognition, and recognition requires her to know from the inside what a sister is, and that is the exact piece that was removed.

**The prose must render this as a technical failure, not an emotional one.** She has the inputs. The operation on them will not run. She checks her work twice and the work is right.

**Lena is in the apartment for part of this.** She is doing something ordinary — talking about her week, being in the next room, being loud about nothing. She is four feet from a wall that explains, in her sister's handwriting, exactly why she is in danger, and neither woman in the apartment can read it.

**Outcome:** Iris has the correct and complete account of what happened to her and cannot use it. She does not warn Lena, because there is nothing to warn her about in the account as Iris understands it: the thing took a capacity, and the capacity is gone, and there is nothing left to take.

**That reasoning is wrong and it is not stupid.** It is what the evidence supports if you cannot feel what the evidence is about.

**Emotional Beat:** For one moment she gets closer than she has been. Looking at the four cards, she has something that is not a feeling but is adjacent to the place feelings come from — a sense that the answer is directly in front of her and is a shape she used to be able to see. It does not resolve. It is the closest thing to grief in the entire book and it lasts a sentence and it is about a wall, not about her sister.

**Craft, and this is the gate the whole book has been protecting:** **the reader completes it.** They have had the answer since Ch16 and they watch a woman with better information than they have fail to reach it. Not one sentence may nudge. Not one may almost-say it. **If a line would collapse for a reader who had not read Book 1, it is doing the reader's job.**

**Do not resolve the Lena question and do not let Iris nearly solve it in the last paragraph.** She stops, and she moves on to something else, and the something else is ordinary.

### Chapter 22 — The Second Visit

**POV:** Iris · **When:** +2 days · **Length:** 3,800
**Location:** Iris's apartment, at night.
**Goal:** The third refusal. The first thing Willow has ever wanted and been unable to get. And the last page of Book 2 hands Book 3 its midpoint.

**Conflict:** She comes for Iris.

There is no siege and no chase. She comes in, because the building is a building. Iris knows before she sees her, and the proximity sense is finally, uselessly, ahead of the visual.

**Iris does not run and does not fight.** Both were assessed and discarded in Ch14's procedure, in writing, in advance, when she still had a working process and no fear to interfere with it. What she does instead is stay still and observe, because observation is the only capacity she has left that is fully intact and because she has wanted to understand this since Book 1.

**The face.** Close enough now that Iris can see it is a specific face rather than a general one. It resolves to a person. **Iris cannot know who, and the prose does not tell her or the reader.** The reader has met her.

**The contact.** It is the third time. She reaches, and she works, and it goes further than Book 1 went. Iris feels the process from inside with complete clarity and describes it accurately while it is happening, because that is what she is.

**And it does not complete.** Something in Iris does not resolve. It is not resistance in the sense of effort — Iris is not straining, and she could not stop it if she tried. It is that the thing keeps arriving at something it cannot process and returning to it and arriving again.

**THE PLANT, and it is the last thing this book does.** In the middle of the attempt, Willow finds the empty place where Iris's love for her sister used to be. She stops there.

It is the only part of the structure that matches something. She stays with it longer than she stayed anywhere else. She examines the shape of what is missing, which is the shape of a younger sister, and she is looking at it when she stops.

**Nothing explains this. No line comments on it. Iris registers it only as a duration** — a pause, in one location, longer than the others — and files it as data, and it goes on her wall in the morning as a measurement, and she never works out what it was.

**Willow speaks. Three lines at most, short, incomplete, no more articulate than that.** She is between Book 1's fragments and Book 3's careful sentences. One of them should be an observation about Iris being unfinished. One of them, and this is the important one, should be an admission of a limit — something in the register of *I don't know why* — because it is the first time in her existence that a process has not completed and she has no framework for it either.

**No cruelty, no taunting, no relish, and no appetite.** She is a researcher whose procedure has failed on the same subject three times.

**Outcome:** She stops. She stands up. She leaves.

**Neither of them knows why, and neither of them is told.**

**Emotional Beat, and the last page:** Iris is intact in the sense that she is no less than she was that morning, and she is now the only object in the world that this thing has failed at, and it knows.

She goes to the wall. She writes what happened, in plain handwriting, on a card, and pins it with the others. Then she writes the second card, which is the conclusion, and it is short, and it is the last line of the book's argument: she is permanently unfinished, and it will be back.

**What changed in Willow — and the book must not state it.** She has now encountered the first thing she cannot complete. That pressure is what makes her strategic in Book 3, and it is what makes the ending of the series possible. **Do not explain it. Do not have Iris deduce it. It happens in the room and the reader feels it and nobody says it.**

**Craft:** Fragmentation risk is high through the contact sequence. Iris's interiority under contact is written in complete, clear, joined sentences, because clarity is the last thing she has and the prose should give it to her.

**Do not resolve anything.** She walks away, and it lets her, and neither of them knows why yet. That is the ending.

## AUDIT GATES — CHECK ON EVERY CHAPTER

In addition to the standard five passes:

0. **No fragmentation.** Single-line fragments are emphasis only. Read every page aloud. If it reads like a list, rewrite it.
1. **No run-on comma chains.** 3+ commas, 3+ "and," or 45+ words gets examined at the sentence, while drafting.
2. **No trailing explanatory sentence.** Read the last sentence of each paragraph alone. If the paragraph is finished without it, cut it.
3. **No villainy.** Marin never does the wrong thing in a scene.
4. **No frame device inside a Recovered chapter.** Ch15's coda is the one authorized exception and it sits after a scene break.
5. **The reader must like Willow Halloway.**
6. **Nobody explains the name.** Iris finds it in Ch15 and derives it in Ch16. Nobody explains it, in any chapter, ever, and she never says it aloud.
7. **No appetite language for the entity.** Satisfaction is completion. Never hunger, craving, relish, savoring, enjoyment.
8. **No cruelty.** She has never intended harm and still does not. She is increasing a dose that is not working.
9. **Iris does not feel the origin.** She reconstructs it correctly and completely and nothing arrives. No almost-arrivals.
10. **No prose nudging.** If a sentence would collapse for a reader who had not read Book 1, it is doing the reader's job.
11. **No Book 1 register leaking in.** Scan for "she noted," "she registered," "Expected/Observed," "the absence of." Flat affect is not fragmented prose. Iris observes in complete sentences.
12. **Continuity locks from published Book 1.** See the spine above.
