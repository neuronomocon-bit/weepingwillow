## THE THREE DECISIONS, RULED BY THE AUTHOR 2026-09-04

**1. POV — all Iris, single POV, twenty-two chapters.**

No second thread. Book 1 gave Caleb one chapter; Book 2 gave Marin seven; **Book 3 gives nobody one.** The collapse is the spine, and staying locked inside a failing instrument for the whole book is the strongest version of it — **the reader never gets relief from her.**

**The consequence is structural and it governs four chapters.** Lena's partial Taking (Ch13) and Kade's (Ch18) are both **seen from outside, after the fact**, exactly as Book 2 played Caleb's death: *a phone call, a drive, and a man ending politely.* **Do not stage either as a rescue or a witnessed attack.**

**At most one coda seam in the book**, and Ch22 is where it would go. Book 2 had two and used them well. **Do not spend it early.**

**2. Timespan — about five months. June to October.**

**Day 0 is the end of Book 2 Ch22**, which is Day 74 of Book 2 and lands in **early May**. Book 3 opens **weeks after that** and runs to **early October.**

**The reason it is longer than Book 2 is the city.** A task force has to form, be wrong, adopt wrong solutions formally, and then be **thanked**. That arc is the slowest thing in the book and it cannot be run in eleven weeks without breaking the competence floor.

**3. Length — 68,000–72,000. Same as Book 2.**

Twenty-two chapters at roughly **3,200 words**. **The per-chapter budgets below are a ceiling and a diagnostic, not a target.** A chapter at 87% of budget with every brief beat present is finished. **A chapter under about 75% is structurally incomplete, not brisk** — that held for nine consecutive Book 2 chapters and the missing material was always *consequences*, never beats.

**This licenses neither padding nor trimming.**

## THE SPINE — what replaces Book 2's investigation

**Book 2 ran on a number resolving. That is spent.** This book runs on **three clocks that do not converge until Act III**, and the briefs have to keep them separate.

**1. The capability clock.** She selects, she travels, she studies, she returns to inspect the result — and **at Ch7 she stops needing to touch anyone.** Book 1's entire tension was distance. **Once it is gone there is nowhere left to stand**, and the book must not give the reader a new place to stand.

**2. The instrument clock.** Iris is the only detector and she is failing. The proximity sense **arrives late** from Ch1. **The professional competence goes at Ch7**, which is the last intact thing and the only instrument she has. By Act III she operates on scaffolding.

**3. The city clock.** Wrong answer formally adopted (Ch3) → the answer holds while bodies accumulate → Rourke breaks (Ch15) → **and the metrics improve anyway, and the city is grateful** (Ch16).

**⚠️ The three clocks are what make Act III inevitable rather than convenient.** By Ch19 the detector is failing, the rule is gone, and the population has stopped objecting. **The plan is not clever. It is what is left.**

## CONTINUITY SPINE

### Willow's face — continue the ladder, do not jump it
Book 2 ended at **specific: a face assembled toward one woman, and the reader has met her.**

| Chapters | State |
|---|---|
| 1–11 | As Book 2 left her. Specific, and **not yet complete.** A person who has seen a photograph of Willow Halloway would hesitate; nobody in Lowport has |
| **12** | **Virek recognizes it.** Not "resembles." He knows who it is |
| 13–19 | Complete enough that witnesses stop disagreeing, which is itself the tell — Ch18's contradictory accounts do not recur |
| **22** | **Finished.** The face is complete and it is a specific face and it is somebody's |

### Satisfaction, not pleasure — unchanged and absolute
**Banned in narration and figurative description: hunger, craving, appetite, relish, savoring, enjoyment, pleasure, feeding.** She is finishing, not feeding.

**⚠️ And the Book 2 corollary, which is harder here:** what Ch22 left in her **has no name.** Book 3 has to earn any word it uses for it. **Do not inherit one.**

### The negative gates — screened every chapter
1. **No appetite language, anywhere, including codas**
2. **Nobody explains the name.** Willow says *"Willow Halloway"* once in Ch21, flat, in passing, **and it is never returned to.** Not by Virek, not by Iris, not by the prose
3. **No character theorizes about the entity's interiority**, and the narration does not either
4. **The irreducible core is felt by the reader and never explained by the text**
5. **Iris does not grieve on the page.** Not for Lena, not for Kade, not for herself. **No almost-arrivals**
6. **Nobody in this series is a villain.** Not Willow, not Rourke, not the task force

### The competence floor — this book's highest-risk rule
**Book 2's most dangerous defect.** An institutional response inadequate to the risk **as visible at the time** does not weaken a scene, **it inverts the book** — from *nothing in the system was built to catch this* into *these people were bad at their jobs.*

**⚠️ Book 3 has four institutional set pieces — Ch3, Ch15, Ch16 and Ch20 — which is four times Book 2's exposure.** Walk the chain on every one: anomaly → containment → operator → affected systems → immediate mitigation → monitoring → ownership → closure criteria → **and what later changed.**

**Put the failure in a changed assumption, never in a missing step.**

### The access chain — the most dangerous defect class in this project
**For every fact a character states, name the sentence that gave it to them.** Iris has no police powers, no subpoena, and no employer. **Her channels are: Kade, Rourke, the recovered drives, public record, and asking a person.** That is the whole list. **When a brief below hands her a fact, it names the channel.**

### Chapter 1 — Weeks After

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 30 · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Iris's apartment. Then a recovery job on the east side that she completes correctly.

**Goal:** Establish the position Book 2 left her in without restating it, and **show that the complete and correct account of the origin produces nothing in her.**

**Conflict:** The wall is different now. It is not a question any more. It holds a finished account — the woman, the program, the sister, the map that was kept, the afternoon two parameters were changed — **and Iris reads all of it once a week the way she reads the cards, and it returns the same nothing it returned in Book 2 Ch16.** The reading is a maintenance task.

The three cards from Book 2's last page are still on the table with *Monday* on the corner, and **Monday was seven weeks ago.** She has done Kade's. She has not done Rourke's. The third one is not a thing you do.

**⚠️ The new symptom, and it is the book's first clock:** the proximity sense **arrives late.** Not weaker — late. She establishes it on the east-side job, where she registers the ordinary background of an empty building **about four seconds after she has already walked past the room it was in.** A detector with a lag is a different instrument from a detector with a range, and she writes the distinction down because it changes what she can be used for.

**Outcome:** She adds a card. **The instrument is now on the list of things being measured**, which is the position the whole book runs from.

**Emotional Beat:** Lena is in the apartment when she gets back, because Lena has a key now and used it, and she has moved two things and cleaned nothing, which is deliberate. **She is not asking how Iris is. She has stopped asking and she is simply present**, which is the decision she made in Book 2 Ch9 and has kept. Iris registers the arrangement as stable and does not examine it.

**⚠️ And the consideration does not occur.** Nothing in the chapter marks that having Lena here might be dangerous. **Not a suppressed thought. The consideration does not happen.** No line, no hesitation, no beat. Ch13 collects on this and the collection only works if nothing here points at it.

**Continuity:** Quote the wall rather than restating the position. Her case card reads *Second attempt did not complete. Expectation unsupported and not withdrawn.* The last line of the record work reads *Will it come back. No prior case of a subject it did not finish. Outside the data.* **She has not withdrawn it and she has not closed it.**

**Craft:** **The mystery is spent and this chapter is where a draft will try to re-run it.** Do not summarize the origin. Do not let her walk the reader through the wall. **The wall is furniture now**, and its being furniture is the horror: a complete account of the worst thing that ever happened to her, read weekly, filed. **Show her reading it in under a hundred words and moving on to the cards.**

### Chapter 2 — Method

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 34 · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Joe E.'s consulting room above a shop on the north side, and the street outside it.

**Goal:** **The pattern break, and it is not geographic.** Somebody studied him first.

**Conflict:** **Joe E. is a counselor**, and his entire professional life was built around emotional depth: listening, holding, processing other people's pain. He is emptied. **A counselor being emptied of emotional capacity is its own specific horror** — the person who helped others feel is now someone who cannot.

He is functional. He kept his Tuesday appointments. **Two of his clients noticed nothing and one did**, and the one who did could not say what, and said so apologetically.

**The break: there is no geographic connection to anything.** Joe E. has never been near a Meridian site, a transfer point, a relay point, or any of the four hundred and sixteen. **Iris's entire map is a map of proximity and this man is not on it.**

**⚠️ And then the six days.** He was **watched** before contact. This is the chapter's whole finding and **the access chain has to be named**: Iris does not have cameras and does not have police. What she has is **his own diary** — a counselor keeps one — which records the same figure at the same bus shelter opposite his window on six of the nine working days before, noted twice as *the woman waiting*, once with a time, because he was a man who noticed people and wrote things down for a living. **She gets it because his practice partner gives her the diary, and the partner gives it to her because she asks and because nobody else has.**

**Outcome:** **Selection.** Not proximity, not opportunity, not exposure. Somebody chose him, studied him, and came back. Iris writes it as **a change of method with no cause attached**, because she does not have a cause and will not invent one.

**Emotional Beat:** She sits in his chair, opposite where the clients sit, and runs the physical check she has been running since Book 1 Ch4 and **gets nothing, as she has since Book 2 Ch14.** She records location, duration, absence. **The record is now three books long and it is the only continuous document of her that exists.**

**Continuity:** The escalation ladder. **Book 2's method was the dose and the return; this is neither.** Do not let Iris merge them. ⚠️ **And do not let her call it hunting.** She does not have that and neither does the prose.

**Craft:** **Joe E. was upgraded at the 2026-08-27 rebuild from a name in a case file to an on-page chapter**, and the reason is the six days. **The six days are the chapter.** Everything else is the setting for them.

### Chapter 3 — The Wrong Answer

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 38 · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** A civic hall. Then Rourke's office, briefly, afterward and outside.

**Goal:** **The wrong answer is formally adopted**, and it is adopted competently.

**Conflict:** This is not Book 2 Ch18's public meeting and **it must not read like it.** Ch18 was a county officer with an advisory and a phone number. **This is the answer arriving:** a named probable cause, a task force with a chair and a budget line, a monitoring program, a sampling regime, and a set of public recommendations that people can follow.

**The cause they land on is a probable environmental neurotoxin**, and it is the correct thing to land on. **⚠️ Walk the chain and make every step defensible.** They have a cluster with no shared clinical history on the certificates, a geography that failed to produce a source in Book 2, a population-level presentation nobody has a category for, and an obligation to act. **An environmental factor is the only hypothesis that can be tested by the people who have been given the job.** They test it properly. **The failure is in a changed assumption, not a missing step.**

**Rourke stands on the panel and reads a statement she stopped believing in Book 2 Ch12**, and **does it well.** She does not hedge, she does not signal, and she does not perform reluctance. She gives the number of deaths, the period, the age range, and the limits of the finding, and she is the most credible person in the room.

**Outcome:** **The room believes her, and the room is right to.** A process now exists. **Having a name for it makes it worse, because now there is a process**, and the process consumes the only people who could have looked elsewhere.

**Emotional Beat:** Afterward, outside, Rourke does not defend the statement and does not apologize for it. **She asks Iris for the thing Iris has not given her** — the second card, *ask her*. And Iris, who has the whole mechanism and could hand it over, **does not.** Her reasoning is on the card and is defensible: *Would want it. Would be worse off with it. Both true.* **She says something accurate and insufficient, and Rourke accepts it, and that acceptance is worse than an argument.**

**Continuity:** ⚠️ **Rourke's contraction rate is a continuity lock against a published book.** Book 1 measured her at **7% expanded**; a Book 2 draft drifted to 61% and was corrected to 21%. **Her panel statement is her recitation register and will run high. Everything outside the hall must not.**

**Craft:** **The competence floor is the whole chapter.** If a reader finishes it thinking the task force is stupid, it has failed. **They must be right about everything except the one thing nobody could have known.** The horror is that a correct process, run correctly, arrives at a wrong answer and then **defends it well.**

### Chapter 4 — Enzo

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 45 · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Enzo's flat, afterward. Then the street between his building and one other.

**Goal:** **Prove selection by emotional profile**, on the page, out of physical evidence.

**Conflict:** Enzo is a musician. Iris walks his home after: instruments, photographs, forty years of somebody's actual life, **and unfinished work on a stand with the pencil still on it.** She sees exactly what was selected for, and she can name it, and naming it is the problem.

**⚠️ THE STAGING PROBLEM, AND THE BRIEF SOLVES IT RATHER THAN LEAVING IT.** The outline says *"Willow passes an easier target on the page."* **The POV is Iris and she was not there.** So it cannot be witnessed. **It has to be reconstructed, and the reconstruction has to be honest.**

**The build:** between the transit stop and Enzo's door there is a **ground-floor flat with an open window and a man in it who has been in Rourke's caseload since March** — a partial, flattened, alone, unlocked, and **completable.** He is the easier target by every measure the previous two books established. **He was not touched.** Iris establishes he was not touched by the only channel available: **she goes and asks him**, and he answers correctly and pleasantly and is exactly as he was, and she confirms it against Rourke's summary.

**Outcome:** **She passed him.** Iris writes it as an exclusion rather than a discovery: *An open job on the route was not closed.* **That is the strongest form of the finding and it is the form she can defend.**

**Emotional Beat:** She stands in a room full of the evidence of a rich interior life and **identifies it as a selection criterion.** She is doing Willow's assessment, accurately, on a dead man's furniture. **She notices that she is doing it and does not stop**, and the noticing goes on a card. **No horror in the prose. It is a professional observation and it is filed as one.**

**Continuity:** ⚠️ **This is the first appearance of the beat that becomes Ch17.** Ch17 is where she does it deliberately and writes down that she is doing it. **Here she does it once and clocks it. Do not let Ch4 do Ch17's work.**

**Craft:** **The unfinished work on the stand is the chapter's one permitted image and it must not be explained.** No sentence about what it means that the work is unfinished. **Put it on the page and leave.** The trailing explanatory sentence is this project's most persistent defect and this is exactly where it will appear.

### Chapter 5 — Kade

**POV:** Iris, Kade present throughout · **When:** Day 48 · **Length:** 3,200
**Location:** A transfer point on the water side, and Kade's vehicle.

**Goal:** **Kade is not confessing any more. He is doing something**, and what he is doing is the seed of Ch18.

**Conflict:** He has been going back to the transfer points **on his own time**, with his own equipment, and he has been doing it since before Ch1. He has photographs, a route, a schedule, and **a set of observations about which sites have been revisited since the shutdown and which have not.** It is competent work and it is not what she asked for.

**⚠️ He opens on the answer she gave him.** Book 2's last chapter handed him a true thing — **Marin did not know what she was going to make** — written on a card **without the reasoning**, with a note underneath that whether he wanted the answer is a separate question she could not run. **He has had it for seven weeks.** He tells her what he did with it, and what he did with it is this: **he stopped needing to be forgiven and started needing to be useful.** He says it plainly and without weight, with his hands busy, **because he never delivers exposition in a monologue.**

**Outcome:** He has a proposal, and the proposal is containment. **Not destruction — containment.** He argues it practically: the building held it for three years, the sites are already sealed, and **the thing has a physical body that obeys space.** It is a good argument made by a man who moves things for a living. **Iris does not refuse it. She files it**, and the filing is the seed.

**Emotional Beat:** He asks her, directly, whether she is going to be all right, and **she gives him the accurate answer** — the delay, the lag, the cards, the fact that the instrument is on the list — and he receives it the way he receives a manifest. **Neither of them dramatizes it.** ⚠️ **This is the chapter that has to make the reader like him again before Ch18**, and the way it does that is by showing him competent and unafraid, **not by showing him vulnerable.**

**Continuity:** His guilt is **logistical, never dramatic.** He moved boxes to locations he did not ask about, and **the worst thing he ever did was write down the answer he was given.** ⚠️ **Do not re-run the confession. It was spent in Book 2 Ch3.**

**Craft:** The shortest chapter in Act I on purpose. **It is a working scene between two competent people and it should feel like relief**, which is what makes Ch18 land.

### Chapter 6 — Weston

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 52 · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** Weston's home, mid-morning. He offers her coffee and she takes it.

**Goal:** **The dangerous thought arrives in a form she cannot dismiss.**

**Conflict:** **Weston is not empty. He is better.** He was volatile, anxious, and genuinely suffering, and now he is not, **and he says so unprompted, and he is right.** Every metric agrees with him. He sleeps. He is easier to be around. **He does not miss anything.**

**⚠️ Tone: pleasant, warm, and entirely sincere.** He is grateful. **Caleb was hollow; Weston is grateful, and grateful is worse**, and this is exactly why Weston carries this chapter and not Caleb.

He describes what his life was like before, accurately and without self-pity, and what it is like now, **and the second description is better in every particular a person could name.** He is not making a case. He is answering a question she asked.

**Outcome:** Watching a man be untroubled and grateful, Iris thinks it plainly for the first time: **he is not in pain, and she is not in pain about Lena**, and **maybe what was removed from both of them was weight rather than value.**

**Emotional Beat:** **She writes it down, because writing things down is what she does now. Seeing it in her own handwriting is worse than thinking it.**

**⚠️ DO NOT RESOLVE IT.** No counter-argument, no correction, no beat where she talks herself out of it. **This sentence is what Willow hands back to her in Ch21**, and Ch21 only works because **Iris cannot reject it, having already had it.**

**Continuity:** The card goes on the wall. ⚠️ **It should go in the short row above the doorframe — the measurements that have not moved** — and it should be the only thing added to that row in Act I. Book 2 put two things there in twenty-two chapters.

**Craft:** ⚠️ **The gate runs both ways here and it is the hardest gate in the book.** Do not name what is missing in Weston **and do not certify what is there.** Book 2 Ch10 had *"and she meant it"* about Willow Halloway and it had to come out. **Render behavior. The reader decides whether Weston is a man who got better or a man who got smaller, and the prose must be genuinely unable to say.**

### Chapter 7 — Without Touching (ACT I TURN)

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 59 · **Length:** 3,800
**Location:** A launderette on a main road, in the afternoon. Then a recovery job the same week, and the apartment.

**Goal:** **The rule the entire series built its tension on is gone.** And the last intact thing starts failing.

**Conflict:** **Someone is Taken across a room.** No contact. **Not proximity in the sense Book 1 meant** — not a hand, not a touch, not a passing brush in a corridor. A woman folding laundry, a figure by the door about four meters away, **and about ninety seconds.**

**Three witnesses, and they agree.** ⚠️ **That agreement is itself the tell**, and it is the first thing in the series that has been corroborated. Book 2 Ch18's witnesses contradicted each other about the face; **these three do not contradict each other about the distance.** Iris takes all three accounts separately, and does it properly, and gets the same four meters three times.

**⚠️ The access chain:** she gets to them because the launderette is two streets from a name on Rourke's list and **Kade heard about it from the yard within a day**, which is the channel Book 2 Ch18 established and the only one that works this fast.

**And the second half, the same week: she makes an error on a recovery job.** Not a delay and not a lag. **An error** — she runs a pass on the wrong volume, gets a clean result, and reports it, and finds the mistake herself two days later because she checks her own work now. **Nobody was harmed and the client never knew.**

**Outcome:** Two entries. **Contact no longer required**, which she can defend on three corroborated accounts. And **Retrieval, sequence loss, and now this**, which she does not yet have a category name for and leaves uncategorized on the card, **because inventing a category would be building the missing part out of nothing.**

**Emotional Beat:** **The professional competence was the last intact thing and it is the only instrument she has.** She does not panic and she does not despair. **She works out what a person does when their instrument is unreliable, and the answer is that they check it against something else, and there is nothing else.** She writes that down too.

**Continuity:** ⚠️ **This detonates Book 1's central tension rather than contradicting it.** Book 1 Ch1 through Book 2 Ch22 required contact, every time, without exception, and that was true. **Do not let any character say the rule has changed as though the rule was ever announced.** Nobody in the world knew there was a rule. **Iris knew, and now she does not have it.**
