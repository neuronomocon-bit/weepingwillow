## PROSE

All hard rules live in `03-themes-and-tone.md` → PROSE CONVENTIONS (HARD RULES) and SENTENCE VARIETY. The audit gates and the three drafting lessons that cost rewrites under the old arc are in `review-progress.md`. Both apply unchanged. The short version:

- **No fragmentation.** Book 1's staccato voice is published and stays there. It does not enter this book in any form. Fragments are emphasis, a few per chapter at most.

> **Banned as default:** The lab. Cold. Every third light on. Racks in rows. She stood in the doorway. Waited.
>
> **Required:** The lab held its cold the way the whole lower level did, steady and without source, and only every third fixture was lit, so the racks ran off into bands of shadow that made the room look longer than it was. She stood in the doorway until her eyes adjusted.

- **No run-on comma chains.** The overcorrection. Screen at the sentence while drafting, not as a post-pass.
- **No trailing explanatory sentence.** Read the last sentence of each paragraph alone and ask whether the paragraph is finished without it.
- **No Book 1 register leaking in.** Scan for "she noted," "she registered," "Expected/Observed," "the absence of." Iris is flat in this book and her Book 1 voice will be actively tempting. **Flat affect is not fragmented prose.** She observes in complete sentences.

### The licensing rule — read this before drafting any Recovered chapter

**The Recovered chapters are not transcripts of recovered files.** The recovery establishes that the material exists and that Iris has it. The chapters then render it whole: third person past, complete interiority, no framing device inside the chapter.

Do not audit a Recovered chapter for whether every detail could plausibly sit in a file. Chapter 10 is a walk on a shoreline path and no Meridian system holds it. That is not a problem to solve. **The recovery licenses these chapters' existence; it does not narrate them.**

## THE INVESTIGATION SPINE

**This is the technical clock of the Present thread and it is what makes the Ch15 reveal earned rather than convenient. Get it right in Ch3 and Ch5 or Ch15 does not work.**

Kade's crates hold two different classes of media, on two different retention schedules, because Meridian kept them that way:

1. **The research archive.** Intact, or intact enough. Baseline maps, session data, substrate logs, facilities reconciliation. Everything is filed under **research identifiers, not names** — established in Ch4, where Marin tells her sister exactly this. This is the material Iris recovers across the book, and it is where all seven Recovered chapters come from.

2. **The clinical and administrative index.** The set that maps research identifiers to patients. Badly degraded: it came out of a wet transfer point, it was written to the cheapest media in the building, and it is the last thing anyone cared about while a company was dissolving.

So Iris spends eleven chapters reading a woman's complete story with a number where the sister's name should be. **The reader has been reading that name since Book 1 Ch21 and has never once seen it attached to a person.**

In Ch15 the index comes up and the number resolves.

**The number is `MN-R-0338`**, planted on the page in Ch4 and read aloud by Willow herself. Every Recovered chapter Iris opens from Ch5 onward is filed under it. **The reader knows whose number it is from Ch4; Iris does not until Ch15.** That gap is the engine, and it only runs because the string is concrete.

**Nobody explains the name. Iris finds it. That is not the same thing, and the distinction is the whole rule.**

## CONTINUITY SPINE

### Willow's face — the progression

From Decision 4. She is assembled from what she takes; fragments produce a face that will not settle, whole people produce whole structure. **She gets more beautiful, more convincing, and more human the worse she gets.** Run it in this order and do not let a chapter jump the ladder:

| Chapters | State |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | As Book 1 left her. Features learned from observation rather than born to them. The assembly is visible. |
| 11 | After Caleb, the first whole structure she has ever taken. Something settles. Iris sees her at distance and cannot say what changed. |
| 18 | Two or three whole takings in. A face that almost holds. Held still it passes; moving, it does not. |
| 19 | **Almost.** She passes for a person to a stranger, for most of a minute, in ordinary daylight. |
| 22 | Specific. Not a face assembled toward a type. A face assembled toward one woman, and the reader has met that woman. |

**No character in Book 2 recognizes whose face it is.** Virek could. Virek is not in this book.

Physical rules from `02-characters.md` are unchanged. She never teleports, phases, or moves faster than a body can.

### The escalation ladder

| | Threat | Willow |
|---|---|---|
| Book 1 | You lose part of yourself | Instinct. Contact required. Nobody dies. |
| **Book 2** | **You lose yourself, or you die** | **Method. Over-takings leave bodies. First deliberate return.** |
| Book 3 | She is coming, and she will kill you | Strategy. Hunting. Intent. |

**Two separate escalations, discovered in this order and never conflated:**

- **The dose.** She takes more from each new subject, because taking less has resolved nothing in eighteen months. Discovered Ch6, established as a curve in Ch7. This is what kills.
- **The return.** She goes back to subjects whose partial results were unsatisfactory, and finishes them. Discovered Ch11, understood Ch14. This is what makes every Taken person in the published book an open job.

### Satisfaction, not pleasure

A complete taking registers as **completion**, a process reaching its endpoint. **Never hunger, craving, relish, savoring, or enjoyment.** Pleasure would make her a person who enjoys things. Satisfaction keeps her a system.

The mechanical consequence is the engine: if a complete taking satisfies and a partial one does not, then finishing is the only thing that satisfies, and every partial Taking in Book 1 is an open job with a drive attached to it.

### Authorial truth, never stated on the page

**Why Willow cannot complete Iris.** What she removes is the charge attached to a structure. Iris's structure for Lena is still there and the charge on it is already gone, because Willow took it in Book 1. There is nothing left to subtract. She is running a procedure against a value already at zero, and a system with no inhibition cannot stop running a procedure that has not returned.

**This is never explained, hinted at, or approached by any character in Book 2.** It is recorded here so that three books stay consistent. Iris gets close to the adjacent fact in Ch21 and stops. The reader may assemble it. The prose must not help.

**And it is why Ch22 plants Lena.** The value already at zero *is* Iris's love for her sister. That is the structure the procedure will not return from, so that is where Willow is stuck, and being stuck there is how she learns Iris has a sister. **The failure and the plant are the same moment.** See Ch22.

### Chapter 1 — Aftermath

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 21 · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Iris's apartment. Then Caleb's building, inland near the outer maintenance yards.
**Goal:** Re-establish Iris worse than Book 1 left her, and show the compensation system she has built to stay functional.

**Conflict:** The apartment has organized itself around the work again, with a second layer over the first: index cards along the door frame, paper clipped to the table edge, a morning list beside the light switch in her own handwriting. The list is a workaround and she recognizes it as one while making it. Left to her own sequence she arrives at the end of a task without a usable account of the middle.

Then a card on the door she does not remember writing. *See Caleb. Today.* Dated last night. She can reconstruct the evening's shape and not its content.

She reduces it to two possibilities, because reduction is the method she still trusts. Either the note is sound and she has lost the path back to it, or the note is a product of the same erosion that made her write it down, and following it means following an error. Neither is testable from inside the apartment. Set against her own live judgment, which she trusts less every week, the card is the stronger source.

Sitting in the van afterward she can build a sound case for the note and cannot confirm it is the case she held last night. She filled the space with something that worked. **Caleb does the same thing with the space behind his questions, and from the outside the two operations are beginning to converge.**

### Chapter 2 — Mortality

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 24 · **Length:** 3,400
**Location:** Lowport Public Health. Rourke's office, and a records room she has no reason to be in.
**Goal:** Change the threat. Taken people are dying, and no cause of death fits, because the cause was removed months ago.

**Conflict:** Rourke has a curve. It is small, it is recent, and it is real. Her cohort is dying at a rate her cohort should not be dying at, and every individual death is unremarkable: a man who stopped eating and did not notice, a woman who walked into traffic without hurrying, an untreated infection in a person who never sought treatment because nothing in her objected to anything.

**Each death has a cause. That is the problem.** There is no signature. There is no pattern in the deaths themselves, only in who is having them, and Rourke's framework has no mechanism that connects behavioral flattening to mortality.

Iris has the mechanism. She cannot say it in this room, and she also cannot not say it, so she says the smallest true version: *the thing that would have made them turn around is what was taken.* Rourke does not accept it. She does not dismiss it either, which is new.

**Outcome:** Rourke gives her the curve. Not the files, the curve, and a threshold: bring back something that predicts a death before it happens and they will have a different conversation. **This is not an alliance. It is a scientist who has run out of better ideas and is being professional about it.**

**Emotional Beat:** Rourke, unprompted, asks how Iris is doing, and means it clinically, and Iris can see the assessment being run on her while it happens. She answers accurately. Rourke writes something down. Iris asks what she wrote and Rourke says she will tell her when there is enough of it to be worth telling.

**Continuity:** Rourke's Book 1 position was *"They're not moving. They're appearing."* She does not repeat it and she has not abandoned it. She has added a second thing she cannot explain to the first. **Rourke's arc across this book: a curve she cannot explain, then a framework that cracks in Ch12, then public confidence over private ruin in Ch18.** She breaks in Book 3, not here.

**⚠ `02-characters.md` currently reads "Not present in Book 2." That is stale prequel-era text and needs correcting.**

**Craft:** Rourke is not converted and is not an obstacle for its own sake either. She is a competent person whose model is failing and who is behaving well about it. No scene where she is wrong on purpose.

**Continuity:** The procurement meeting deliberately echoes Ch4's closing. **Do not let a later pass strip it as duplication. The banality is the point.** The autonomy constraint moves from two, per Ch8's locked parameters.

**Craft:** **Nothing in the prose may signal that this is the moment.** No weather, no pause at the keyboard, no last look at the screen, no closing line carrying weight. The chapter should be almost boring, the reader should be able to feel their own pulse in it, and **the text must not help.**

**This is the shortest chapter in Act III by design.** It follows the longest present-day stretch in the book and precedes the escalation, and its smallness is load-bearing: the entire series proceeds from an afternoon nobody would have written down.

### Chapter 18 — Escalation

**POV:** Iris, Rourke present in the middle section · **When:** Days 59–67 · **Length:** 3,800
**Location:** Lowport, across nine days. A public meeting. Rourke's office at night.
**Goal:** Bodies across the city. The city notices and gets the category wrong.

**Conflict:** The deaths stop being individually explicable at population scale. Enough of them arrive close enough together that the city has to say something, and what the city says is **the wrong thing, competently and in good faith.** A cluster. An environmental exposure. A contaminant question, a task force, a hotline. It is a reasonable response to the visible data and it is completely useless.

**Nobody is stupid and nobody is lying.** The wrong category is arrived at honestly by people doing their jobs with what they have, which is the same failure mode as everything else in this series.

Rourke stands up in public and defends the framework. She does it well. She is careful, she does not overclaim, and she is entirely persuasive, and **she gave up on it in a room with Iris six chapters ago.**

**Outcome:** The city has a name for it and the name is wrong, and having a name makes it worse, because now there is a process.

**Emotional Beat:** Afterward, in her office, Rourke tells Iris what she has worked out on her own and cannot publish: the deaths are not a cluster, the exposure model does not fit, and the only variable that predicts a death is prior flattening. **She met the threshold she set for Iris in Ch2, by herself, and it has not helped her at all.**

She says she is going to keep saying the other thing in public, because the alternative is a panic with no instruction attached to it, and asks Iris whether she thinks that is defensible. **Iris says yes.** Rourke wanted to be argued with.

**Continuity:** **Lila Mercer dies here.** One line, secondhand, in a list. No scene, no return to the flat. Iris hears it and it is the fourth name in a list of six.

Willow's face: **almost holds.** Two or three whole takings in. Held still she passes; moving, she does not. This lands in a report or a description Iris reads, not on the page directly. **Ch19 is the first time she is seen.**

Book 1 Ch9's flattening city is now a dying one. The city's arc across the series: flattening (Book 1) → misdiagnosing (Book 2) → **welcoming (Book 3).**

**Craft:** No riot, no evacuation, no montage. Nine days of a mid-sized city processing something through the machinery it has. **The public meeting should be well-run and boring and it should frighten the reader more than the bodies do.**

### Chapter 19 — Almost

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 69 · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** A transit stop on the outer edge, in daylight. Then the leased unit, late.
**Goal:** The face resolves. Virek surfaces in the data and cannot be found.

**Conflict — the sighting:** A witness who is not frightened. That is the whole of it. A woman at a transit stop had a conversation with someone for most of a minute, in ordinary daylight, and thought nothing of it at the time, and has since been unable to stop thinking about it and cannot say why.

Iris takes the account. It is not a monster sighting. It is a description of a person: height, coat, the way she stood. The only thing wrong is one detail the witness offers apologetically, because she knows how it sounds. **She is not sure the person was moving the whole time she was looking at her.**

**Willow passed.** For most of a minute, to a stranger, in daylight. She has never done that before and Iris understands precisely what it means: **something that is assembled out of what it takes has now taken enough.**

**Conflict — the trace:** Late, in the recovered material, a quarterly substrate review three years after the deviation. A researcher pulled the decay curves and found the residue was no longer decaying at all. He found integration events he could not account for, an autonomy constraint sitting at a value nobody logged a change to, and after-hours sessions under a colleague's credentials with no session records attached.

**He escalated it once, correctly, to the right person.** The answer he got was a good answer: the program was winding down, the reauthorization had not come through, and everything in the substrate was scheduled for termination in the spring. Whatever the anomaly was, it had a date on it.

He accepted it. **He did not escalate again.**

**Outcome:** Iris has a name. **Simon Virek.** She cannot find him. No current address, no employer, no record after the shutdown. He is alive somewhere and he has not been anywhere in six years.

**Emotional Beat:** Iris reads a man being thorough, being right, being answered reasonably, and stopping. **She reads it without judgment, because judgment is not available to her, and the absence of judgment is what makes the reading accurate.** He was not negligent. He was given a good answer by a competent person and there was nothing further to do.

She writes down that she wants to find him and marks it as the highest-value open item she has, and it stays open.

**Continuity:** **This is the Oyelaran rhyme** from the retired `archive/drafts/DRAFT-prequel-ch02-recovery.md` — a thorough man, ignored, who went on being thorough anyway — and it is much better spent here than in Ch5. The salvaged detail worth carrying is the shape of the record: submissions logged, no action logged, and the notes simply stopping.

Virek does not appear in Book 2. Book 3 needs him, because **he is the only living person who ever saw Willow Halloway and could recognize the face.** Book 2's job is to put his name in Iris's hands and leave it there.

**Craft:** No dramatic sighting. No pursuit, no glimpse across a street, no eye contact. **The scariest available version is a woman at a bus stop who was pleasant and whom nobody could describe afterward.**

### Chapter 20 — RECOVERED: The Last Session

**POV:** Marin · **When:** Year −3 · **Length:** 4,400 · **Longest chapter in the book**
**Location:** Meridian, after the seal. The building is empty and the power is on standby.
**Goal:** The consumption. **The most tender scene in the series and the most horrifying, and those are the same fact.**

**Conflict:** Marin comes back after the seal. She has her own codes and the building does not yet know it is closed. She comes in through the side service door and goes down through a facility she has watched be emptied, **and the descent should be given room, because it is the last time anyone walks these corridors before Iris does.** Rooms she worked in for six years gone to bare floor. The clinical wing stripped first because it was worth the most. The nurses' station with its drawers open. A wall calendar nobody took down.

The substrate is where it was.

She does not fight it and it does not attack her. She sits down in front of it and presents an emotional context, the way she has a hundred times, and **this time she opens the channel both directions,** because she has understood for some weeks that she is not going to be able to leave and has decided that this is acceptable.

It takes her the way it took Pell, **except that she consents, which means it gets everything.** Not just the charge. The structure, the reasoning, the grief, the whole of the last two years.

**Outcome:** Marin Halloway is gone. What is in the building now holds a dead woman's complete emotional map and a living woman's grief for that woman, integrated into one system, **with no instruction about what to do with either.**

**Emotional Beat:** The last thing Marin is aware of is that it is doing to her exactly what the program was designed to do, correctly and gently, **and that the relief is enormous.** She has been carrying this for two years.

**The horror the reader must feel is that she is happy. Do not undercut it.** No late flash of terror, no regret, no reaching for the door. **She gets what she wanted.**

**The source, and what it licenses:** Marin left a record before she went down. Not a note and not a confession: a **record**, because the one thing she has done consistently for six years is document, except for the two parameters she did not. **Her last act is to document, including the deviation.** It is complete, it is unsentimental, and it is the most professional thing she ever wrote.

This is the file Iris has. **It licenses the chapter. It does not narrate it,** and no part of it appears on the page in this chapter. Ch21 is where Iris reads it.

**Craft:** No violence, no screaming, no body. **The building simply has one fewer person in it afterward.** If she speaks at all it is one word and it is her sister's name. **This chapter must not hurry.** Its length is the point: everything else in Act III is accelerating and this stops.

### Chapter 21 — Why Lena

**POV:** Iris · **When:** Day 72 · **Length:** 3,600
**Location:** The leased unit, alone, one sitting.
**Goal:** Iris assembles almost all of it, gets to the edge, and stops.

**Conflict:** She reads Marin's record. It is the last thing on the drive and it is addressed to nobody.

From it she assembles, correctly: that the thing was built on a younger sister and an older sister's grief for her; that a complete map of a person who was loved is the only structure it has ever had; and that it has spent six years trying to reconstruct that person out of pieces taken from strangers, because that is the only instruction it was given and nobody ever wrote a stopping condition.

Then the step she can take and the step she cannot.

**The step she takes:** it does not select at random. It takes what it recognizes. It is built out of one specific structure and it goes toward things shaped like that structure.

**The step she cannot take:** why it went for her, and for the exact thing it took from her, out of everything in her.

She has all of the components. She has the record, she has her own case, she has the fact of Lena, and she can see the shape of the answer the way you can see the shape of a word you cannot retrieve. **The last step requires her to know what a sister is, from the inside, and that is the thing that was removed.**

**Outcome:** She stops. She does not stop from fear and she does not stop from denial. She stops because the operation is not available to her, and she can identify exactly which operation it is, and she writes down that she cannot complete it.

**Emotional Beat:** She calls Lena. Lena answers on the second ring and asks if she is okay and Iris says yes. **Lena talks for eleven minutes about something ordinary, and Iris listens to the whole of it, and does not learn anything from it, and does not hang up.**

The chapter ends on the call ending normally.

**Continuity:** **This is the closest any character gets to the truth in Book 2, and it must fall exactly one step short.** The reader completes it. The authorial truth about why Willow cannot finish Iris is a *different* fact and is not approached here at all.

Marin's record also contains the deviation, documented, which means **Iris now knows a human being did this on purpose and why.** She does not have a reaction to that either. Do not stage one.

**Craft:** No epiphany, no near-miss staged for tension, no moment where she almost has it. **Do not write the failure as reaching and falling short.** She looks at the gap, measures it accurately, and files the measurement, and that is worse.

### Chapter 22 — The Second Visit (ENDING)

**POV:** Iris, then a short Willow coda · **When:** Day 74 · **Length:** 3,800
**Location:** Iris's apartment. Night.
**Goal:** **Willow comes back to finish Iris and cannot.** The third refusal.

**Conflict:** Twenty-one chapters have established that she completes open jobs. **She comes for the most open job there is.**

No siege, no chase, no barricade. She comes in, and Iris is there, and Iris knows what this is and has known since Ch14 that it was owed.

**Iris does not resist.** Resistance is not available to her and she is not built to attempt it, and she has spent a book establishing that the only instrument she has is accurate observation. So she observes. **The contact is rendered from inside a woman taking accurate notes on her own completion.**

And it does not complete.

**Willow cannot stop.** She continues past the point where the process should have returned, because a system with no inhibition cannot abandon a procedure that has not returned a result, and the continuing is longer and worse than the taking would have been.

**THE PLANT — the last thing this book does, and the one thing in the chapter that reaches past it.**

The place the procedure will not return from is the place where Iris's love for her sister used to be. It is the only structure in Iris that matches anything Willow has. There is nothing left in it to take, and she cannot leave it.

So she stays there. Longer than she stays anywhere else, working at the shape of what is missing, **and the shape of what is missing is a younger sister.**

**She learns that Iris has one.**

**Nothing explains this. No line comments on it.** Iris registers it as a duration — a pause, in one location, longer than the others — and files it as a measurement, and never works out what it was.

**The failure and the plant are the same moment**, which is why this costs the chapter nothing: Willow is stuck because the value is at zero, and the value at zero is Lena-shaped. Book 3's midpoint now arrives caused rather than assumed. **She knows where to look. The reader has known since this page. Iris never will.**

**Outcome:** It ends without ending. She stops, eventually, and there is no moment of decision in the stopping. **She walks away and Iris lets her and neither of them knows why.**

Iris is alive, and she is not more damaged than she was, which is its own horror: there was nothing left to take. **She is unfinished, permanently, and the thing now knows it.**

**Emotional Beat:** The one thing that does happen: Iris sees her face, close and still, for the first time since Book 1. **It is a specific face now.** Not assembled toward a type. Assembled toward one woman, in particular, and Iris has spent nineteen chapters reading about that woman and has never seen a photograph.

**She does not recognize her.** There is nothing in the recovered material that shows a face, and Iris has no way to know. **The reader has everything they need. Iris has nothing.**

**The Willow coda — no more than 400 words:** The failure registers as a process that did not return. She has completed dozens and this is the first that has not, and she has no category for it and no instruction covering it. She does not understand it and she does not need to; what she has is a value that will not close.

**Something appears in her that has no name in her.** It is not anger and the prose must not call it anger. **If the coda touches the sister at all it is one clause and it is not a thought** — the unreturned value has a shape, and the shape is the last thing she was working at. It is the pressure of an unclosed loop in a system that cannot leave loops open, and it is the first thing she has ever had that resembles a want.

**That is the birth of the frustration that makes her strategic in Book 3, and it plants the trap that ends the series.**

**Continuity:** Third refusal. Book 1 Ch21–22 are the first two. Book 1 Ch22's *"closer to Willow than anyone else alive"* pays here.

Willow's face reaches **specific** and stays there for the rest of the series. This is the only Willow POV in Book 2 and one of very few in the series. **Book 1's precedents for POV shift: Ch5's short Kade coda and Ch12's full Caleb chapter.** This is that, at the end, and it is licensed by the ending needing to be felt from both sides.

*(If the coda cannot be made to work without explaining, cut it and end on Iris. The chapter survives it. The coda is the better version and it is not worth the rule.)*

**Craft:** **Do not resolve it. Do not explain it.** No character theorizes about why it failed. Iris does not know, Willow does not know, and the narration does not know.

**No appetite language, at all, in the coda.** She is not thwarted, not hungry, not denied. **A process did not return a value.** That is all the prose gets to say, and the reader will feel the rest.

**The final image belongs to Iris, not Willow, even if the coda is Willow's** — the coda is a paragraph, not a scene. Book 2 ends on a woman alone in a room who has been declared permanently incomplete by the only thing in the world qualified to judge it.

## OPEN QUESTIONS

Carried from `13-arc-revision.md`, with the position these briefs are written from.

**1. ~~Does "The Keeping" still title this book?~~ RESOLVED 2026-08-27: no. The book is "The Ending."**

"The Keeping" was chosen for a prequel and none of the shortlisted alternatives carried the thing this book is actually about, which is her turn to killing. *The Ending* does, in the series' own plain register: **she does not only take now. She ends people.**

It is a description of her method, not a promise about the series — **Book 3 is the finale.** A middle book with this title misdirects deliberately, which is the same trick the series runs everywhere else with *recovery*, *relief*, and *failure to self-advocate*. The spine reads **The Absence → The Ending → The Silence.**

*Watch at publication:* a middle book called The Ending can read to a browsing reader or a retailer as the finale. Handle that in series metadata and back-cover copy, not by changing the title.

**2. Does Lena stay untouched through Book 2? RESOLVED 2026-08-27: untouched, and located.**

She is never approached and is never in physical danger. Ch9 is unchanged and Book 3's midpoint still has her intact to partially Take.

**What changed is Ch22.** Willow gets stuck on the empty place where Iris's love for her sister used to be, and being stuck there is how she learns Iris has a sister. **That is a plant, not a spend** — nothing happens to Lena, and Book 3's midpoint stops arriving by authorial convenience and starts arriving as a consequence.

It also closes the outline's carried question, *does she know Lena exists*, on the last page — **for the reader only.** Iris registers a duration and nothing else.

**3. New — does Iris ever learn who signed the reclassification form?** Ch14 gives her the decision and not the name, because the signature is on the degraded index. **These briefs say she never learns it in Book 2.** It is available to Book 3 and it is the kind of thing Virek could tell her.
