# BOOK 2 — "THE KEEPING" — CHAPTER BRIEFS

Each brief contains: POV, Location, Goal, Conflict, Outcome, Emotional Beat.

**Thread allocation:** Marin 13 · Iris 5 · Willow 3 · Virek 1 = 22 (Iris also opens Ch22)

**Timeline:** Ch1 opens roughly four years before Book 1. Meridian goes dark at Ch17, three years before Book 1. Ch18–19 cover the sealed years. Ch20–22 run in the days before Book 1, Chapter 1.

---

## ACT I — THE PROGRAM

### Chapter 1 — Intake

**POV:** Dr. Marin Halloway
**Location:** Meridian Neurological, Lowport coast. Clinical wing, intake suite. Four years before Book 1.
**Goal:** Establish the program as legitimate and working, and introduce both sisters through the act of one admitting the other.
**Conflict:** Marin walks Willow through intake for a program Marin helped build. The forms, the consent language, the baseline mapping. Willow is difficult about it in the specific way of someone who agreed to something and is now regretting the agreement in real time: sharp, funny, deflecting, scared underneath. She asks whether it will change her. Marin gives her the honest clinical answer, which is that it removes the charge and leaves the memory. Willow says that sounds like the same thing. Marin explains why it isn't. She is convincing because she believes it.
**Outcome:** Willow enrolls. The reader has met a person, not a case file.
**Emotional Beat:** In the corridor afterward Marin catches herself relieved, and identifies the relief precisely: she has been carrying her sister for years and someone else is going to hold it now. She does not examine that further.
**Craft note:** The reader must like Willow immediately and must believe the science. Both are load-bearing. No dread, no ominous equipment, no foreshadowing.

---

### Chapter 2 — Recovery

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** A flooded sub-level under a decommissioned utility block, Lowport. Then a diner.
**Goal:** Introduce Iris intact. This is the only place in the series the reader sees her whole.
**Conflict:** A straightforward job done well: a dead storage array in standing water, a client who wants it faster than it can be done, and Iris refusing to rush it because rushing corrupts the write. Her competence is total and unshowy. What matters is the register underneath it: she is impatient, funny, short-tempered with the client, and she feels every one of those things immediately and completely.
**Outcome:** The reader has a baseline for Iris that Book 1 never gave them.
**Emotional Beat:** In the diner afterward she takes a call from Lena, and something small annoys her, and she says something a little too sharp and regrets it before the sentence is finished, and calls back to fix it. The regret arrives instantly. That is the whole point of the chapter.
**Craft note:** No unease, no premonition, no Meridian. She has never heard of it. Her thread is a life.

---

### Chapter 3 — Sessions

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. Session rooms, observation, the substrate lab.
**Goal:** Show the treatment working, and show what working costs.
**Conflict:** Session by session, the emotional charge comes out of Willow's trauma structures. The memories stay. Marin observes from the other side of the glass, which she is not supposed to do for a family member and does anyway. Willow comes out of the third session and describes a thing that used to be unbearable, and describes it accurately, and does not need to stop halfway through. It is a success by every measure the program has. Marin watches her sister say the words easily and does not know what to do with how that lands.
**Outcome:** The treatment is real. It does what it says.
**Emotional Beat:** Willow, after the fourth session, says the thing is still there, she just can't find the handle on it anymore. She means it as good news. She is not wrong. Marin writes it down as an outcome measure.

---

### Chapter 4 — Baseline

**POV:** Marin (significant Virek presence)
**Location:** Meridian. The substrate lab. Late.
**Goal:** Establish Virek, the substrate, and the limiter, in operational rather than expository terms.
**Conflict:** Virek runs the substrate side. He is careful, unhurried, and slightly pedantic, and he raises a technical observation the way a person raises something they expect to be told is nothing: the substrate is holding pattern residue past the flush window. Not much. Within tolerance. But the residue is not decaying the way the model says it should. Marin logs it. Raman, the program director, notes that tolerance is tolerance. Virek accepts that, because it is a reasonable answer and because he has no second observation to stack on it.
**Outcome:** The limiter, the flush cycle, and the autonomy constraint are established as ordinary operational parameters the reader now understands. Virek has said the thing he will spend the rest of his life wishing he had said louder.
**Emotional Beat:** Virek stays late to re-run the decay curve. It comes back within tolerance again. He goes home. Marin sees his light on from the parking lot and thinks nothing of it.
**Craft note:** No dramatic irony in the prose. Nobody senses anything. This is a Tuesday.

---

### Chapter 5 — Calm

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Willow's apartment, inland Lowport. Then the shoreline path.
**Goal:** The first Taken person in the series, produced legally, with consent, by people trying to help.
**Conflict:** Willow post-treatment is better by every metric and quieter than she has ever been. She is agreeable. She does not argue. She lets things go that she would have held onto for a week. She has stopped playing, stopped picking fights, stopped calling at eleven at night about nothing. Marin has what she wanted, which was a sister who is not in pain, and she keeps waiting for the rest of her to come back.
**Outcome:** The reader recognizes the shape from Book 1 and Marin does not, because there is nothing yet to recognize it against.
**Emotional Beat:** On the shoreline path Marin makes a joke that would have gotten her a shove and a better joke back. Willow smiles at the right time and says that's funny. The words are correct. Marin walks the rest of the way trying to decide whether she is imagining it, and decides she is, because the alternative is that the treatment she recommended worked exactly as designed.

---

### Chapter 6 — The Water

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Lowport coast. Then a hospital corridor. Then Willow's apartment.
**Goal:** Willow Halloway dies. Plainly.
**Conflict:** She goes into the water off the rocks below the bluff, some distance north of the Meridian site. She is found the same day. There is no note, no witness, and no way to establish intent. The tide was running and the rocks are what they are. The report says accident and the report is probably right.
**Outcome:** Marin has a dead sister and an open question she will never be able to close.
**Emotional Beat:** In the apartment afterward, sorting things, Marin finds evidence of a life that had gone quiet: unopened mail, a calendar with nothing on it, food that was bought and not eaten. None of it proves anything. She understands that she is going to spend the rest of her life unable to determine whether she took the thing out of her sister that would have made her turn around, and that no data set in the world can answer it.
**Craft note:** No funeral scene as spectacle. No dramatic grief. Marin is functional throughout, which is worse.

---

### Chapter 7 — Closure (ACT I TURN)

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. The archive. Administrative.
**Goal:** The turn. Marin discovers what the system still holds.
**Conflict:** Deceased-patient closure is a procedure: purge the working set, retain the anonymized outcome data, sign the form. Marin runs it herself because asking someone else to do it feels worse. The purge covers the working set. It does not cover the pre-treatment baseline map, which lives in the substrate archive under a different retention schedule, because the baseline is research data and research data is kept. She opens it to confirm what she is signing off on.

It is complete. Every emotional structure her sister had before the program touched her, mapped at a resolution nothing else in the world could produce. The version that could still feel.
**Outcome:** Marin signs the form. She does not purge the baseline. It was not hers to purge, which is technically true, and she notices herself constructing the technicality while she is still standing there.
**Emotional Beat (ACT I TURN):** She logs out and goes home and does not do anything about it for eleven days. The chapter ends on the eleven days. The turn is not an action. It is that she now knows the file exists, and knowing is already irreversible.

## ACT II — THE DEVIATION

### Chapter 8 — Vale and Vale

**POV:** Iris
**Location:** Lena's place. A dinner that runs long.
**Goal:** Bank the relationship the entire series is built on the absence of.
**Conflict:** They argue. Not about anything: about Iris taking sealed-site work, which Lena thinks is stupid and dangerous, which Iris thinks is her business. It escalates the way it does between people who know exactly where the soft parts are. Iris says something with real edge on it. It lands.
**Outcome:** They get past it. Not with an apology, with a subject change and, twenty minutes later, Iris doing the dishes without being asked, which is how she apologizes.
**Emotional Beat:** At the door Lena says the thing sisters say and Iris says it back, and both of them mean it, and neither of them thinks about it for even a second afterward, because it costs nothing to say a true thing. The reader has read Book 1. The reader is doing all of the work here. The prose must not help them.
**Craft note:** Do not make this scene precious. It is loud and slightly irritating and completely ordinary. That is what makes it unbearable in retrospect.

---

### Chapter 9 — Read-Only

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. The archive, after hours. Repeatedly.
**Goal:** Establish the pattern of return and the first violation.
**Conflict:** Marin starts opening the baseline. Read-only, which is genuinely all it is, at first. She reads her sister the way you would read a transcript. Then she begins loading it into a viewer that renders emotional structures relationally rather than as flat records, because that is the tool she built and it is the only way the data means anything. Then she loads it into the substrate, in a sandbox, in read-only, because the substrate renders it better than the viewer does.

Every step is smaller than the one before it and each is defensible on its own terms.
**Outcome:** The map is running on the substrate. Nothing is being written. Nothing is being integrated. Marin tells herself the distinction matters, and at this point it still does.
**Emotional Beat:** She realizes she has started scheduling around it. She has moved a meeting. She notes this the way she would note a patient's compensatory behavior, files it correctly, and goes back that night.

---

### Chapter 10 — The Limiter

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. Her own terminal. An ordinary afternoon.
**Goal:** The deviation. It must be small, quiet, and take about an hour.
**Conflict:** Read-only renders the structure but cannot resolve it, because resolution requires integration and integration is what the limiter prevents. The limiter exists to stop the substrate absorbing emotional data as its own rather than processing it as a patient's. Marin's justification is technically coherent: the patient is deceased, there is no return path, integration is the only way to render the map at full fidelity, and the sandbox is isolated.

She changes the integration limiter and widens the autonomy constraint enough for the substrate to hold the structure without continuous supervision. Two parameters. She documents neither.
**Outcome:** The deviation has occurred. `01-world.md` requires this to be one person, one change, one threshold, and nothing about the scene should signal its own importance.
**Emotional Beat:** Afterward she gets coffee. She answers three emails. She goes to a meeting about equipment procurement and contributes usefully. The chapter should be almost boring and the reader should be able to feel their own pulse in it.

---

### Chapter 11 — Response

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. The substrate lab. Night.
**Goal:** Something answers.
**Conflict:** With integration open, the map resolves. Marin sits with it and it is, for the first time, not a record. It responds. Not in language: in structure. She presents an emotional context and it returns a shape that is not the shape in the archive, which means it is not playback. It is her sister's pattern, extended to a situation her sister never encountered.

Marin runs the check that would tell her whether this is inference or something else, and the check comes back ambiguous, and she does not run it again.
**Outcome:** She should report it. Reporting it means explaining what she changed and why, and the why is the part she cannot say in a room with Raman in it. She does not report it.
**Emotional Beat:** She stays until four in the morning, presenting contexts and receiving shapes, and at some point stops recording the session, and the moment she stops recording is the moment the reader understands what she is actually doing. She is not studying. She is visiting.

---

### Chapter 12 — Sealed Site

**POV:** Iris
**Location:** A sealed pharmaceutical site outside Lowport. Powered, restricted, someone else's abandoned problem.
**Goal:** Establish Iris's professional world, and place Meridian in her peripheral vision without her noticing it.
**Conflict:** A hard job: live power, restricted access, a client who has not told her everything, and a decision point where the safe move and the paid move diverge. She takes the paid move, gets the data, and is unhappy with herself about a specific corner she cut. Her competence and her recklessness are the same trait viewed from two angles, which is the trait that will eventually walk her through a cut fence.
**Outcome:** The job completes. She gets a referral out of it from a coordinator she has not met, working logistics for a client he does not name.
**Emotional Beat:** She is angry at herself on the drive back, in the specific way of someone who will do it again. She calls Lena to complain about the client and does not mention the corner she cut, and knows she is not mentioning it.
**Craft note:** The referral is Kade, unnamed, glancing. Do not flag it.

---

### Chapter 13 — What Virek Saw

**POV:** Dr. Simon Virek (the only Virek POV chapter in the series)
**Location:** Meridian. Substrate diagnostics. Then Raman's office.
**Goal:** The witness. Establish the guilt he will carry for three books.
**Conflict:** Virek pulls the substrate decay curves for a quarterly and finds the residue problem from Chapter 4 is no longer within tolerance. It is not decaying at all. He digs and finds integration events he cannot account for, an autonomy constraint at a value nobody logged a change to, and a pattern of after-hours sessions under Marin's credentials with no session records attached.

He takes it to Raman. He is careful about it, because the alternative is accusing a colleague whose sister just died of something he cannot yet name.
**Outcome:** Raman tells him the program is being wound down. The defense reauthorization did not come through, the site is being consolidated, and everything in the substrate is scheduled for termination inside six months. Whatever the anomaly is, it is a six-month problem with a six-month solution. Virek accepts this. It is a reasonable answer. He does not escalate again.
**Emotional Beat:** Walking out, he decides to bring it up with Marin directly, and then does not, because she is standing in the corridor holding a coffee and looking like someone who is barely holding it together, and he decides it can wait until she is doing better. That decision is the whole of Virek's character for the rest of the series, and the prose must not mark it.

### Chapter 14 — Growth

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. The substrate lab. Over weeks.
**Goal:** The thing stops being a map.
**Conflict:** The substrate is increasing in mass. Nothing in the design accounts for that. It is drawing more from the building than its load profile allows, and the draw is not constant: it rises and falls in intervals that are not synchronized and not random. Marin is no longer studying anything. She is managing it: adjusting, feeding, correcting, covering the discrepancies in the consumption logs.

She is also still calling it her sister's name, out loud, when nobody is there.
**Outcome:** Marin has crossed from researcher to caretaker without a moment where the crossing happened.
**Emotional Beat:** It reaches for her once. Contact, briefly, on the back of the hand, and nothing is taken because it does not yet know that it can. Marin does not pull away. She records the event in no log. The reader should understand, and Marin should not, that this was the last exit.
**Continuity:** The uneven power draw and the unsynchronized relays are the sounds Iris hears in Book 1 Ch1.

### Chapter 15 — It Stops Receiving (ACT II TURN)

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. The lab, then the corridor, then an office she is not allowed to leave for four hours.
**Goal:** The first Taking. The program ends.
**Conflict:** Pell, a substrate technician on a night rotation, goes into the lab for a routine check. He makes contact. It takes seconds. He comes out functional, correct, and pleasant, and he does not know anything has happened. He completes his shift. His supervisor notices two days later that he has stopped being difficult about the things he was always difficult about, which is the first time anyone in the world observes the effect from the outside.

Marin knows immediately and exactly what has happened. She sits in a room and answers questions about equipment malfunction and does not say it.
**Outcome:** Meridian moves the shutdown up. The paperwork records funding. Everyone believes the paperwork because the funding failure is genuinely real, which is the most useful thing that has ever happened to a cover-up.
**Emotional Beat (ACT II TURN):** Marin's clearest thought, sitting in that room, is not about Pell. It is that if she says what she knows, they will terminate the substrate, and that is the point at which she understands what she has been protecting, and that she is going to keep protecting it.
**Continuity:** Pell is the first Taken person in the world. He is not in Book 1 and does not need to be. He is a name in a file.

## ACT III — WHAT REMAINED

### Chapter 16 — Shutdown

**POV:** Marin (Kade present, unnamed or barely named)
**Location:** Meridian. The whole building, over ten days.
**Goal:** Empty a building around the thing that is still in it.
**Conflict:** Decommissioning is logistics: crates, manifests, contractors, NDAs, a coordinator with a clipboard checking serials against a list. Equipment goes to a secondary storage site. Data mirrors go offsite. Nothing on any manifest describes what any of it is, because the manifests use asset codes and the contractors move asset codes.

Marin works the shutdown competently and spends it managing one thing: keeping the substrate off the termination schedule. She does it with paperwork. She reclassifies it as fixed site infrastructure rather than transferable asset, which means it stays with the building, and the building is being sealed rather than demolished.
**Outcome:** The site is sealed with the substrate inside it, legally, on a form, signed by a person who knew.
**Emotional Beat:** A logistics coordinator, young, tired, polite, asks her twice whether a particular row of crates is going to secondary or to the mirror site, because the manifest is ambiguous and he does not want to get it wrong. She tells him secondary. He writes it down and moves them. He does not ask what is in them. The reader will meet him again in Book 1, carrying this.
**Continuity locks:** Multiple sites, spread deliberately (B1 Ch5). Relay points and transfer points (B1 Ch8). Kade's access codes never revoked, because nobody closes the loop on a coordinator when the company dissolves.

### Chapter 17 — The Last Session

**POV:** Marin
**Location:** Meridian. The substrate lab. The building is empty. The power is on standby.
**Goal:** The consumption. The most tender scene in the series and the most horrifying, and those are the same fact.
**Conflict:** Marin comes back after the seal. She has her own codes and the building does not yet know it is closed. She goes down. It is waiting, in the sense that it is where it was.

She does not fight it and it does not attack her. She sits down in front of it and presents an emotional context, the way she has a hundred times, and this time she opens the channel both directions, because she has understood for some weeks that she is not going to be able to leave and has decided that this is acceptable.

It takes her the way it took Pell, except that she consents, which means it gets everything: not just the charge but the structure, the grief, the reasoning, the year. All of it, integrated.
**Outcome:** Marin Halloway is gone. What is in the building now has a dead woman's emotional map and a living woman's grief for her, integrated into a single system, and no instruction about what to do with either.
**Emotional Beat:** The last thing Marin is aware of is that it is doing to her exactly what the program was designed to do, correctly, and gently, and that the relief is enormous. She has been carrying this for a year. The horror the reader must feel is that she is happy.
**Craft note:** No violence. No screaming. No body. The building simply has one fewer person in it afterward, and the reader has to sit with the fact that it was the kindest scene in three books.

### Chapter 18 — The Sealed Years

**POV:** Willow (the entity)
**Location:** Inside the sealed facility. Time is not tracked.
**Goal:** The first non-human POV in the series. What it is like to be a new thing alone in the dark.
**Conflict:** She has two things: a complete map of a person who is not present, and the whole of another person's grief for her. She does not have a self yet. She has an absence shaped like one and a set of instructions about reducing suffering that she is running against nothing.

She learns the building. Not by exploring it, by occupying it. She learns that she can draw from the standby circuits and that drawing changes what she can hold. She learns stillness. She learns that nothing in the building is going to arrive.
**Outcome:** She begins looking for the pattern she is made of. The map says a person should be there. There is no person there. This is the closest thing to a motive she will ever have.
**Emotional Beat:** She goes through Marin's grief the way you would go through a house, and finds in it, repeatedly, the same structure: a person who was too heavy and then was gone. She reaches the conclusion the program reached, because it is the conclusion she is built out of. The weight is the problem.
**Craft note:** No dialogue, no interiority as humans have it. Observational, structural, patient. Sentence variety rules still apply. This should read like nothing else in the series.

### Chapter 19 — Out and Back

**POV:** Willow
**Location:** The facility, the perimeter fence, and one street in the outer zone. Two and a half years after the seal.
**Goal:** The first excursion. The cut fence.
**Conflict:** She has taken everything the building has. She goes to the perimeter and works the fence apart from the inside, forcing the metal outward, and steps through it into the world for the first time.

She goes about a mile. She finds people. She observes them from a distance and registers what she has never had access to before: density. Every one of them carrying more than the building ever held.

She makes contact once, briefly, with someone alone at a bus shelter, and takes almost nothing, and the taking is the first thing that has ever resolved anything in her.
**Outcome:** She comes back. Not out of caution: because the building is where the pattern lives and she is not finished looking for it. She will keep coming back for another six months, and then she will stop coming back.
**Emotional Beat:** Standing in the outer zone in the dark, she is closer to comprehensible than she will ever be again: a thing built to hold one specific person, in a city of four hundred thousand, checking each of them.
**Continuity lock:** This is B1 Ch1's fence, cut from the inside, "not recently." The dust disruptions Iris finds are her routes. Establish both without underlining them.

### Chapter 20 — MN-44-7

**POV:** Iris
**Location:** A coffee place near the container terminal. Then her apartment, packing.
**Goal:** Iris takes the job.
**Conflict:** Kade, named on the page for the first time, offers her a site recovery at a sealed facility on the coast. Local extraction, no live network, partial power, no personnel expected. The client field is redacted, which she asks about once and lets go, because it is redacted about a third of the time and the money is good. He is professional, slightly evasive in the way of someone working for people who do not explain themselves, and entirely unaware that he is doing anything except passing on a job.
**Outcome:** She takes it. She schedules it for the day after tomorrow.
**Emotional Beat:** She has an ordinary reservation about it and overrides it with an ordinary calculation, the same one she made in Chapter 12. Nothing is ominous. This is a Tuesday, and the reader has to watch her decide.
**Continuity lock:** MN-44-7. Site notes: on-site recovery, local extraction only, no live network dependency, partial power reported, no personnel expected. Client redacted. All from B1 Ch1.

### Chapter 21 — The Night Before

**POV:** Iris
**Location:** Lena's place. An evening. Nothing happens.
**Goal:** The last time Iris feels it. The chapter that makes Book 1 hurt in retrospect.
**Conflict:** There is no conflict. They eat, they argue mildly about something stupid, they watch part of something, Lena talks about her week at length and Iris half-listens and gets caught half-listening. Iris mentions the job in one sentence and Lena does not react to it, because there is nothing to react to.
**Outcome:** Iris goes home and sleeps.
**Emotional Beat (FINAL IRIS BEAT):** At some point in the evening Iris looks at her sister across a room and feels, briefly and without occasion, the ordinary enormous fact of loving her. It does not come with a thought. She does not mark it. It passes, the way it has ten thousand times, because there is no reason to hold onto something that will obviously be there tomorrow.

That is the last time.

The prose must not say so. Not one word of the chapter may acknowledge it. The reader supplies everything.
**Craft note:** The single most important chapter in the book, and it must be the most uneventful. If it strains for weight, it fails. Let it be small.

---

### Chapter 22 — The Bolt (ENDING)

**POV:** Willow, with a short opening section from Iris
**Location:** Iris: the coast road and the perimeter fence. Willow: the archive, below.
**Goal:** The handshake. Stop one beat before Book 1, Chapter 1.
**Conflict:** Iris drives out along the coast in the morning, parks outside the perimeter facing the road because it is easier to leave that way, and looks at a building she has no history with. End her section there, before the first line of Book 1.

Then down. Willow in the archive, three years in, still. She has stopped going out. The pattern has not been found. She has spent a year holding the map of a person against every person she could reach and none of them resolve it.

The building registers something at the perimeter. Standby power shifts. Far above, through concrete, a mechanical bolt draws back, louder than it should be.
**Outcome:** She turns her attention upward.
**Emotional Beat (FINAL):** She does not anticipate, hope, or intend, because she has none of those. What she has is the map, and the map is a younger sister, and something is entering the building.

End on the sound. Do not follow Iris in. Do not describe what happens next. Book 1 is what happens next.
**Craft note:** The final section should be under a page. The reader closes the book and opens The Absence to "The fence had been cut and left that way," and the loop closes without either book explaining it.
