Chapter 21 — Why Lena

The last document on the fourth batch was filed seven weeks after the building was sealed, into a system that had been sealed with it.

Iris found it on the Thursday morning and understood what she was holding inside a paragraph, and then did not read it properly for two hours, because she went and did the other work first. She imaged it twice. She verified both closes. She wrote the identifier and the date and the length on a card and put the card on the table. Then she made coffee and drank it while it was hot. She had started doing that again in April, for reasons she had written down and did not entirely believe.

Then she sat down and read it from the front.

***

It was an incident record on the program’s own form, so it answered the questions in the order the form asked them, and the form had been written for a technician reporting a fault.

Nine days of work. The author had filled in every field, including the four that are optional, and had used the third person throughout, so that the pages read as though a stranger had gone through the site with a list and written down what was there.

The word sorry did not appear anywhere in it. Neither did any other word of that kind. Iris checked that a second time later, because it was the sort of absence that is either an omission or a decision, and the rest of the document made it clear which.

It had both parameters.

The exclusion list entry, with the identifier and the reason code and the date. The autonomy value, with the workspace scope stated and the site default stated separately, and a note that the change control form did not cover the first and that this had been raised at the time. The date on the first was given as a range of four days, with the reason the range could not be narrowed. The date on the second was exact.

It had the reconciliation entries, twenty-four of them, listed by month.

It had every session. Not a summary. The whole log, reproduced, with the ones booked under a type and the ones booked under none, and a line saying which had been recorded and which had not.

It had the asset reclassification, three weeks before the seal, with the category codes both ways and a sentence saying the form had been correct. Then a second sentence, immediately after, saying that the author had known what the correct form would produce. The two sat next to each other without a conjunction. Nothing in the document told a reader what to do with them.

And it had the technician. Four paragraphs. The date of the check, the duration, the handover note two days later, what the author had understood and when, and what she had not said and to whom she had not said it. There were no adjectives in any of it and no explanation on the end.

***

Iris read the whole of it twice and then did the only useful work available. She checked it.

She had eleven weeks of independent work on the same six years: the access log, the reconciliation column, the substrate loads, the quarterly with the four findings, the closed ticket, and an identifier she had joined to a name by a route the author of this document could not have anticipated.

She went through both, line by line, for most of the afternoon.

They agreed. Not approximately. On every point where the record made a claim she could test, it was correct, and on the points she could not test it said so, in about the words she would have used. Where she had a range, the record had a range. Where she had a date, the record had that date.

It was better than her reconstruction in one respect and worse in none. It knew why.

Iris sat back and registered that a human being had done this deliberately, for a reason stated on page four in two clauses, and had then written all of it down on purpose so that whoever came next would not have to work it out.

She got no result from that. She had expected none and she checked anyway, because a measurement you take only when you expect a result is not a measurement.

***

The record stops.

It stops at the end of a numbered section, complete, with the last field filled in. There is no closing statement, because the form has no field for one, and the author had added nothing the form did not ask for anywhere in nine days and did not start at the end.

Iris put the filing date against everything else she had, and what she found was that there was nothing to put it against.

The staff list ends at the seal, seven weeks before it. The site access log ends at the seal, because the keypads went down with the power and after that the only way into that building was a mechanical key. The consumption logs end at the seal. The substrate log ends at the seal. Every system she has stops inside the same week, and then, seven weeks later, one of them takes a nine-day document from a person who walked in past a dead panel.

After that there is nothing. A gap has edges and she can measure them. This does not. It is an end.

The next event she can put a date on is eighteen months later and four miles away, and it is a woman going to her own doctor because her husband made her.

What she could defend was that the author filed a complete record on a Thursday, and that she appeared nowhere again in any system Iris had reached.

She wrote that down in those words and did not extend it, because the extension was obvious and obvious is not a finding, and because the difference between a person who stopped appearing in records and a person who died is a difference she had spent nine days on with Simon Virek and had been careful about then.

***

What she assembled that evening came in three parts and she could defend all three.

The first is that it is built on a person. Not a type or a model or an average. One complete map of one woman, taken at the highest resolution the program could produce, on a morning in the fall, with nine years of load on it and fog on the window. The only complete map it has ever had is that.

The second is that the map was of a person who was loved, and the loving is in there too. The older sister presented emotional context into that workspace most nights for nineteen months, and then went back after the seal and set a session to run both ways, and what came off her was not the charge alone. The record says what the session was configured to do. Iris did not have to infer it.

So there are two contents and they are not the same kind of content. A person, mapped. And another person’s whole feeling about that person, taken entire and never returned.

The third is that the procedure has never closed. Six years. It takes, the taking returns no completed result, and nothing anywhere in six years of documentation wrote down a condition under which it should stop.

She checked that part hardest, because it sounded like a claim about intent and it is not. It is an absence in a specification. She had the specification. There is no stopping rule in it, and there had never been any reason to write one, because a session ends when the operator ends it.

***

Then the step she could take, and how far it went.

It does not select at random. She had known the negative half since March, when she ran a hundred and thirty-one cases against age and sex and district and referral route and household composition and got nothing, and wrote that there was a criterion and it had never been put in a field.

What the record gave her was the first candidate for that criterion she had ever had.

Inside that building, in the sessions, the instrument matched structures out of the map against the material the older sister presented to it. That is documented. It is in the session log and it is described twice in the body of the record, and the author had rewritten the description three times because the first three versions were not careful enough.

So: an instrument whose only complete content is one person, observably matching what it holds against what it is given.

She wrote the extension underneath that and then wrote what the extension was, in three lines, because the second list is the one that keeps you honest.

*Observed. Selection outside the building is not random, and nothing in four hundred and sixteen clinical records predicts it.*

*Documented. Inside the building it matched its own material against what was presented to it.*

*Model. Outside the building it does the same operation, and what it goes toward is whatever is shaped like what it holds.*

She did not promote the third line. Promoting it meant establishing that a content taken out of a stranger in a kitchen in February belonged to the same category as a content that went into a machine six years ago, and that is a comparison, and she had not run one of those yet.

She listed the rivals as well, because a model with nothing beside it on the page is a model no one has tested. Proximity. Duration of exposure. Whatever a man on a routine check has available in four minutes that a person passing on a street does not. Something in a person that no form has ever collected and that has nothing to do with a map.

None of them fit as well. Fitting well is not the same as being true, and she wrote that under the list.

***

Then the step she could not take.

She put the two descriptions side by side on the table, in her own handwriting, because that was the method and the method had not failed her yet.

The first was the record’s. It is precise about it. What went into that machine from the older sister was her feeling about her younger sister. The document gives it a name in the technical register and then, once, in the ordinary one, and the ordinary version is four words long.

The second was her own case file. In January something had gone from her, and she had spent four months establishing exactly what by measuring the edges of the place it had been, and she had a good description. She could produce the boundary. She could say what it had been attached to, and what still worked around it, and what had stopped. She had it scored on her own scale, with the interval and the location.

Two descriptions. One of a content that went in. One of a content that came out.

The operation is to determine whether they are descriptions of the same category.

Iris sat with that a while.

She could not run it. She can compare two records and she can compare two boundaries, and both of those return matches that mean nothing, because a boundary is an outline and a great many contents have that outline. The operation requires the second description to contain what it is a description of. It requires her to say what it was from inside it rather than what it was attached to, and that is not a measurement, and it is not in any of the eleven notebooks, and it is the specific item that was removed in the first week of January.

She did not reach for it. There would have been nothing to reach for and she had stopped doing that in February.

What she did was establish which operation it was and check that no substitute would serve. She went through them in order and wrote each one down as she cleared it. Comparison by boundary, insufficient. Comparison by attachment, insufficient. Testimony from Lena, who could describe it from the outside better than any other person Iris could ask, and could not describe it from the position the operation required. Her own record from before January, complete and factual and not containing the item itself, because no one writes that down while they still have it.

Then she wrote the finding.

*Cannot determine whether these are the same category. Operation requires an input I do not have. No substitute identified.*

She read it back. It was accurate. It was the most accurate sentence she had written in six weeks and it closed nothing at all.

It also closed the other one. The third line on the model card needed the same comparison to become a finding, and the comparison needed the same input, and there was no arrangement of the afternoon in which she got it.

***

She wrote the four cards after that and put them above the doorframe in the order they had come.

*A complete map of a younger sister.*

*An older sister’s whole feeling about her, integrated, not returned.*

*It goes toward what it recognizes. Model, not finding.*

*From me it took what I had for Lena.*

She squared the fourth against the third and stood back and read the row.

Four facts. Each of them established, each of them checkable, in her own hand, in order. She ran the operation on them again from the beginning, because a result you get once at a table is a result you check standing up, and it returned what it had returned at the table. The instrument required was not on the premises.

She wrote the time and the duration and the category on a fifth card and put it on the long row with the others.

***

There were two practical items and she did both before the light went.

The first was Kade. He had asked her a question at four in the morning in April, on a phone, about whether the older one had known what she was going to make, and Iris had told him there was nothing in the record that said either way and that the record was what she had.

There is a record now and it says.

It says she removed the limiter to resolve a value the acquisition could not reach, and it gives the value and the ceiling and the two sentences of reasoning, and every part of it is about an instrument and a map. Nowhere in nine days of documentation is there a sentence about what would come of it, because the author did not know, and could not have, and would have written it down if she had.

So the answer to the question he asked is no, and the answer is now supported, and Iris wrote it on a card to give him when she next saw him, in the plainest form it would go into, without the reasoning attached. Whether he wanted the answer was a separate question and not one she could run either. She wrote that down under it and left both.

The second was the document itself.

It is the first object she has had in eleven weeks that a person outside this apartment could act on. A named employee of a named institution describing a deliberate act, in the institution’s own form, with dates. Everything before this was a pattern she could see and could not hand to another person.

She worked out what it was worth and it took under three minutes. It is a copy off drives she cannot account for possessing. Its author cannot be produced. The institution has been dissolved for six years and the site is a sealed building with a management company in Portland on the notice. And what it describes carries no offense anywhere she could find, because no law has ever been written about it, and the county is currently running an environmental investigation into nine deaths and would file this in the drawer marked for people like her.

*Changes what I can prove. Does not change what anyone can do.*

There was one person it would change something for and Iris held that longer than the rest of it.

Rourke had sat on the edge of a desk in April and said that the moment she published the association the next question would be the mechanism, and that she did not have one, and that there was no version of her that got one. She had said it flatly and it had been true when she said it.

Iris had the mechanism on the table in front of her, in nine days of another woman’s handwriting, complete, dated, and checkable in eleven places.

She worked out what handing it over would do. Rourke would read it in an evening. She would find it correct, because it is correct and she is good. And then she would have a mechanism she could not source, could not corroborate, could not put in front of a review board without naming where it came from, and could not act on, because the mechanism does not come with an intervention attached to it any more than the association did. What she would have at the end of the evening is the same four hundred people and one more thing she could not say out loud.

Iris put it as a question with two answers and could not choose between them, and wrote both.

*Would want it. Would be worse off with it. Both true.*

Then she wrote *ask her* underneath, because it was the only version of the problem that had a person in it who could decide, and put the card in the row.

The one thing it would do is corroborate a closed ticket for a man she cannot find, and she wrote that down too, because it went on the row under the card with the capitals on it and that row had held one card and now held two.

***

At ten past seven the buzzer went.

Lena had a bag from the place on the water and her coat over her arm and she talked from the bottom of the stairs, which meant she had decided the first thirty seconds on the way over.

She did not ask if Iris was okay. She had not asked since the end of March, and she was not going to, and the not-asking had become as much a part of the evenings as the too much food.

“The car is legal, I have checked twice, do not start.” She got past into the apartment and put the bag on the counter. “I have had the worst week of my professional life and I am going to describe every part of it.”

“All right.”

“You say that like you think I won’t.”

She did.

It was eleven minutes about a supplier who had sent the wrong scheduling licenses twice and then invoiced for the wrong ones, and about a woman at that company named Bernadette, and about the specific and escalating politeness of four emails Lena had written to Bernadette across a week. She read the fourth one out. It was very good. She had rewritten the last line three times, and she said so, and then read all three versions.

Iris listened to the whole of it.

She had the shape of it early and stayed with it anyway. She laughed twice, at the right size, in the two places built for it. She asked what Bernadette had said back, at the point in the account where the question belonged, and Lena told her at length and it was worth the asking.

Nothing came out of any of it. She had known that before the buzzer went. It went in accurately and completely and stopped where it stopped, and she gave it the whole eleven minutes anyway, and she could not have said what for.

Lena took her coat off at last and put it over the back of the chair.

She was standing about four feet from the doorframe. The long row ran along it at eye height and had gone around the corner in March, and the four cards from that afternoon were at the near end of it, square, in blue ink, in her sister’s handwriting.

She did not look at it. She had looked at it properly once, in March, and had made her decision about it then, and after that it was furniture.

They ate at the table and Lena talked about something else, and the light went off the window at about the hour it had been going off it all week.

At half past nine she got her coat and stood in the doorway with her keys in her hand and said what she said.

“Thursday,” Lena said. “I’ll bring the other kind.”

“All right.”

“And you’ll eat it.”

“I’ll eat it.”

“Right,” she said, and went down.
