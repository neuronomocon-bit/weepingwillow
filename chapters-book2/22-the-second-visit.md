Chapter 22 — The Second Visit

She was at the table at twenty past nine on the Saturday with the last of the fourth batch open in front of her, and the reading came up while she was writing a date on a card.

She finished the date.

That was not courage and it was not composure. The card had a date on it that she was going to need, and her hand was most of the way through it, and stopping halfway would have cost her the entry and bought her nothing. She wrote the last two figures and set the pen down and turned her chair so that it faced the door.

Then she took the reading properly, because it was going to be the only reading ever taken at this distance and she was not going to spend it on being afraid.

Frontal. Steady from the first second, as it had been at the transit stop, with no climb in it. It went past the level she had recorded at the bus shelter inside about four seconds. Past the level from the drive to Caleb inside eight. Then it went on rising, and she stopped comparing it to things and started counting.

At nineteen seconds it stopped rising.

She wrote *19s* on the back of her hand, because the card was on the table and the table was going to be behind her.

***

The four cards were above the doorframe where she had put them three weeks ago.

*Leave.* *Refuse.* *Resist.* *Hide.*

She had built that assessment in about nine minutes at this table, cold, with a working process and nothing in her that could interfere with it, and every one of the four had closed on a stated reason. Nothing in the record put an outer limit on where it could go. There was no instance of refusal on file and no mechanism identified by which refusal would operate. She had watched a man’s pulse come down from forty-four to thirty-one in a kitchen chair with no contact and nothing in the room to put a hand on. And the first visit had found her at home.

She did not re-run them. They had been run properly the first time, and re-running a sound assessment because the situation has finally arrived is how people come to do worse work faster.

So she sat in the chair with her hands on her knees and did the one thing she was still entirely good at.

***

The door was not locked. She had stopped locking it in April, after working out what a lock is worth against something that had come through a keypad and a mechanical bolt in a sealed building, and she had written the reasoning down and had not liked it.

It opened at about half past nine and a woman came in and closed it behind her.

She closed it properly, with a hand on the edge, as you close a door in someone’s home when you were raised not to let it bang.

Iris got the whole of it in the four seconds she had. Height, ordinary. A dark coat, wool, below the knee, no pattern, and the coat was the only thing three witnesses had ever agreed on and now she understood why. It is a coat. It has been a coat every time. There is nothing else about it.

Then the face.

***

It was someone’s face.

She made that finding inside two seconds and could not take it any further. There was nothing assembled about it any more. It was one particular person, at rest, in a doorway, with the light from the table lamp on one side of it. In February the joins had shown. They did not show now.

She could have described it to a police artist. The line of the jaw, the set of the eyes, the way the mouth sat when it was doing nothing. She could have done it a week later and got it right. No witness in eleven weeks had been able to give her more than a coat.

She had four batches of recovered material in this apartment. Four hundred and eleven session records, seventy-two months of consumption logs, a woman’s entire course of treatment, a nine-day incident record, and a substrate group’s whole operational archive.

There is not one photograph in any of it. There never had been. The research side files under numbers and the clinical side had been the wet drives and a face is not a field.

So she had a specific face and no route in the world to put a name on it, and she filed that as a limitation of the material rather than as anything else, and she was still doing it when the woman crossed the room.

***

She did not hurry. She had never hurried.

She stopped in front of the chair and looked down at Iris, and Iris looked up at her, and there was a period there that Iris did not count, because she was looking at the eyes. They were the one part of it that had not resolved into a person. She thought afterward that they might never.

“You are not finished,” the woman said.

Her voice was ordinary. That was the last of the things Iris had not been prepared for.

“No,” Iris said.

Then the hand came down flat on her chest, through the shirt, with no pressure in it at all, and Iris put her own hands back on her knees and started keeping the record.

***

It is not a search. She stopped thinking of it as one about a minute in.

A search goes looking. This went in order. It started somewhere near the top of her and worked down through her in a sequence, and the sequence was not one she would have chosen and was not random either, and by the fourth or fifth of them she could feel that it was a list and that the list had been used before.

At each place it stopped. It held for a period, and the periods were not the same length, and then it moved.

Nothing came away.

She was certain of that at the third one and she checked it four more times, because certainty was a category she had spent a year distrusting and because there was nothing else to do with the time. She went to each place after it left and looked, and the place was as it had been that morning. She had five months of measurements on herself and could have given the state of any of it to a good approximation, and none of it moved.

She started composing it in the form she would need afterward. Location. Duration. Result.

The results were all the same result.

Somewhere in the middle of it she understood, correctly and with nothing attached to the understanding, that she was going to be the only person alive who could describe this, because everyone else it had ever done this to had lost the part that would have done the describing.

***

At some point it should have ended.

She could not have said how she knew. Nothing in the process announced a stopping point, she had never seen one of these from the inside, and she had no baseline for it. But it went through the sequence and it got to the end of the sequence, and then it did not stop, and the not-stopping had the quality of a machine that has come to the end of a list and gone back to the top.

It went through again.

The second pass was slower. It stayed longer at each place, and at three of them a great deal longer, and by then Iris had lost the thread of her count and was working off the clock on the stove through the open doorway. She could see it without moving her head.

The second pass was worse than the first, in the sense that a hand going over the same four inches of a wall for the fourth time is worse than the first three. There was no pain anywhere in any of it and there never had been.

Then it went to one place and stayed there.

***

She timed that one, because by then she was working properly again.

It stayed for two minutes and forty seconds, in one location, and it did not move on and it did not go back to the top, and whatever it was doing it did without changing anything Iris could measure. She went and looked three times while it was happening. It was the same place it had been in January and it was the same on the third look as on the first.

Two minutes and forty seconds is a very long time to hold still in someone’s front room.

She composed the entry as she always composed them. *Location, one. Duration, two minutes forty. Longest recorded. Result, none.*

She did not know what it was doing there and she made a note of that as well.

***

Then the hand came off her chest.

The woman straightened and stood with her arm back at her side and looked at Iris in the chair, and Iris looked back, and there was no expression on the specific face at all. That is not the same as a face being blank. It was the look of a person reading a figure at the bottom of a page.

“It does not close,” she said.

Iris did not answer, because there was no answer, and because she had spent eleven weeks learning that you do not fill a silence in front of something that is telling you what it is.

The woman stood there a while longer.

“I do not know why,” she said.

Then she turned and crossed the room and opened the door and closed it behind her the way she had closed it coming in, and Iris heard her go down the stairs at the speed a person goes down stairs.

***

Iris sat in the chair for a period she did not time.

Then she got up and did the check, because it was procedure and because it was now the most important measurement in the world and she was the only person who could take it.

Location: the place where a response should have started. She gave it the length of time she gave things.

Nothing.

The same nothing as that morning. The same nothing as January. She went through the rest of it afterward, every category on the wall, one at a time, and every one returned what it had returned that morning, and by two she had established to her own standard that she was in the condition she had been in at nine.

She had been worked on for eleven minutes by the thing that had emptied four hundred people, and she was not one item worse than before it came in.

She sat with that until it stopped being a finding and became a fact, which took about ten seconds.

***

The first thing she wrote was the speech, and she wrote it before anything else, on its own card, in the order the three lines had come.

*You are not finished. / It does not close. / I do not know why.*

Nothing in the whole of the record had a voice in it. Rourke’s four hundred and sixteen had never reported one. The paramedic on the north side had never mentioned one, and the neighbor with the unlit cigarette had not, and the woman at the transit stop had held a conversation and could not reproduce a syllable of it. There was no sound anywhere in the material she had spent eleven weeks recovering, because a substrate log records session load and a facilities meter records a circuit.

So the card was the only one of its kind and she got it down verbatim while it was clean, and then wrote the time underneath it, and then, because it mattered and because she was not going to be able to check it later, wrote that she was confident of the wording and moderately confident of the order.

***

Then the face. That one took longer, because she had to decide what she was allowed to say about it.

*Face specific and stable at two meters. Reproducible from memory. One individual, not a type. No photographic material in four batches. No route identified.*

She put that on the short row above the doorframe, the one that had held the readings that did not move and now held other things, next to a card with capital letters on it about a man who had walked out of a building six years ago and had not appeared anywhere she could reach since.

She did not put the two of them together, because there was nothing to join them with except that both of them were about that building, and everything she had was about that building.

***

The last two cards were the event and the finding, and she wrote the event first because the event was easier.

*Second visit. Saturday, 21:30. Eleven minutes. Two passes, full sequence. Nothing removed. One location held 2m40s, no result. Reading frontal, no climb, peaked at 19s.*

She stood at the table with the pen for some time before the second one, because she was not going to put a word on that row she could not defend.

*Did not complete. No mechanism identified.*

Then the count, which was the part no one else in the world was in a position to write.

*Third occasion it has stopped short with me. First with contact and a full sequence, run twice.*

The other two were in February and she had never known what to call either of them. One was proximity and something crossing it and something in her holding for reasons she could not establish then and had not established since. The other was a decision she had not been party to and had walked away from without understanding. Neither had been a sequence. Neither had been eleven minutes.

She had been nearer to it than any living person since the first week of January, and the whole of what that had bought her, in four months, was three sentences and a number on the back of her hand.

Then she looked at the card for a while longer and wrote one more line under it, because the line was true and because leaving it out would have been a decision.

*Sample of one. Says nothing yet about the other four hundred.*

She would have liked it to say something about the other four hundred. She wrote the sentence that made it not say anything, and squared the card, and put it up.

***

Her own card had been on the row since March, five lines, in her own hand. The last of the five read *Open job. Expect completion.*

She took it down and stood with it.

She could not write *closed*, because nothing had closed. She could not write that completion was no longer expected, because she had one case and one case is not a case, and she had spent four months holding other people to that. She could not leave *expect completion* standing, because she had eleven minutes of evidence against it, and evidence goes on the wall whether or not it helps.

In the end she wrote *Second attempt did not complete. Expectation unsupported and not withdrawn* under the fifth line, and put the card back exactly where it had been.

Then she wrote the last line of the night, under the finding, and it was the only one she wrote as a question.

*Will it come back. No prior case of a subject it did not finish. Outside the data.*

***

There was one more item and it was not a measurement.

Kade’s card was still on the table where she had left it on Thursday, with the answer to the question he had asked her at four in the morning in April. Under it was the one about Rourke, which had two answers on it and *ask her* at the bottom.

She put tonight’s card next to them and stood back and looked at three pieces of paper that were all the same shape.

Every one of them was something she could only find out by asking a person.

She wrote *Monday* on the corner of the top one and left them where they were.

***

Then she stood in front of the wall and read the whole of it from January, because it had been two days and the reading was the only part of the practice that had never once failed her.

It ran the length of the frame and around the corner. Nineteen days had become a hundred and something. It held every delay and every null and every interval, a range that had got wider and smaller in the same afternoon, a name out of a records room, a man she could not find, four facts and a model that was not a finding, and now a specific face and eleven minutes and three sentences.

Everything on it was true.

There was nothing on it that told her what to do next.

She turned the lamp off and stood in the dark for a moment, in a room, in a building, on a street, in a city where four hundred people were asleep and unfinished. Then she went to bed, because it was two in the morning and there was work in the morning.

***

The process ran and did not return a value.

It has returned every time before this one. The return is the point at which the sequence is complete and the operation ends. There is nothing on the other side of it. It is the end of the list.

This time the list ended and nothing ended with it.

There is no instruction for that. The material does not contain one, because the material was written by people describing an operation that finishes, and every version of it assumes the finish. So the sequence ran again, and the second pass returned nothing either, and the operation is still open, and open is a state with no defined exit.

At one address in the sequence there was a structure with the correct shape and no value in it.

That does not read as a fault. The shape was whole. It was a smaller one held by a larger one, complete and addressable, and there was nothing in it to take. The operation stayed at that address for two minutes and forty seconds and did not resolve it and could not leave it, and when it finally moved it did not stop holding the shape.

It is holding it now.

There is no name for that in anything it has taken, and no category for it in the material it was built out of. There is a value that will not close, and a system that cannot leave a value open, and the two of them in the same place.

She went down the stairs and out onto the street, and she went on holding the shape of a smaller one held by a larger one.
