Chapter 14 — The Weight
Kade's office was active.
More materials than before. Maps extended across the walls. Markings covered over prior markings. Additional sites flagged.
He looked up when she entered.
"You saw him," he said.
"Yes."
Kade studied her.
"And."
"He is functional."
Kade held her gaze.
"You know what I’m asking."
"I know."
"What is he?"
Iris paused. Not searching. Choosing.
"He has no observable impairment," she said. "Behavioral patterns are consistent. Responses appropriate."
Kade's expression didn't change, waiting for an acceptable answer.
Iris held the silence.
Then—
"He is complete," she said.
Kade leaned back slightly.
"Complete," he repeated.
"Yes."
He looked at the maps behind her.
"That's not better," he said.
"No."
Kade stood and moved to the wall. Traced a line between two marked locations.
"It's spreading," he said.
"Yes."
"Faster."
"Yes."
He stopped. Didn't turn back.
"You're sure about the pattern," he said.
"Yes."
"And this—" he gestured, not specifying "—this is the result."
"Yes."
Kade let out a breath. Not frustration. Not fear. Calculation.
"We need to get ahead of it," he said.
"Yes."
He turned back to her.
"You still good to run point on this?"
"Yes."
Another pause. Short. Measured.
"You don't seem—" he started, then stopped.
Iris waited.
Kade shook his head slightly.
"Nothing," he said. "Keep going."
"Yes."
He returned to the maps.
The conversation ended.

***

Iris went to Rourke.
Rourke looked up when Iris entered. Didn't stand. Didn't gesture toward the chair.
"You're here about the new cases," she said.
"Yes."
"They're consistent with prior presentations."
"Yes."
Rourke turned the screen slightly. Case list. Longer than before. The scroll took a moment.
"We've added sixty-three since last review," she said.
Iris watched her.
"Classification."
"Unchanged. Behavioral attenuation. Stable cognition."
"Treatment response."
Rourke paused. Shorter than she used to pause. More contained.
"Variable," she said.
"Any recoveries."
"No."
The word came faster than before. Less resistance.
"Your public statements have changed," Iris said.
Rourke looked at her.
"They're appropriate to the data."
"They're shorter."
"The data doesn't require elaboration."
Iris held her gaze.
"You used to elaborate," she said.
Rourke didn't respond immediately. She turned back to the screen. Closed a window. Opened another. The motion was efficient. Practiced.
"The models continue to apply," she said. "If they stop applying, I'll say so."
"Will you."
Rourke met her eyes again.
Something in the steadiness had shifted. Not cracked. Tightened.
"Yes," she said.
Iris nodded once.
"Thank you," she said.
"For what?"
"Saying so."
Rourke watched her leave. Didn't say anything else.

***

Outside, the air was damp. Less controlled.
Iris moved through the city. Same routes. Same timing.
At a corner near one of the outer transit lines, two people stood talking.
"Did you hear about Caleb," one said.
"Yes."
"He's still working."
"Yes."
"That's good."
"Yes."
The exchange ended. They remained for a moment. Then separated.
Iris continued walking.
She reached for the address of the next site on Kade's list. The one she had reviewed that morning.
It didn't come.
Not blocked. Not forgotten. Just delayed. She could feel the retrieval beginning — its position in the sequence — but the content arrived late. Three seconds. Maybe four.
She checked her device. The address was there. She had looked at it two hours ago.
She noted the delay.
Duration: longer than prior instances.
Category: retrieval lag.
Functional impact: none.
She continued to the site.

***

Later, Kade spoke again. Different location. Same content.
"We lost him," he said.
"Yes."
Kade watched her.
"You don't sound like you mean that."
"It is accurate."
"That's not what I'm asking."
"I know."
Kade held the silence.
Then—
"Does it bother you?" he asked.
Iris processed the question.
"Yes," she said.
The answer arrived without delay.
Kade nodded once.
"Good," he said.
He turned back to the work.
The conversation closed.
Iris remained where she was.
She replayed her answer.
Yes.
The tone had been correct. The timing aligned. The expected response.
She checked for variance. None.
She held it. Waited.
No corresponding internal state. No disruption.
She had not been describing a condition.
She had been answering.
Iris stopped walking. No external trigger.
She replayed the moment again.
Kade's question. Does it bother you.
Her response. Yes.
She observed her own delivery. Posture aligned. Eye contact maintained. Tone appropriate.
It matched prior patterns.
She had seen others do the same.
She had recognized it.
Now—
she had performed it.
The realization held.
She searched for the reason. Not emotional. Procedural.
Why had she aligned.
Two possibilities.
It was dishonest.
Or—
she had forgotten to continue.
She could not determine which.
The question remained. Unresolved.
Iris resumed walking.

***

Back at the apartment, the space remained unchanged.
The recognition of Caleb remained. The absence remained. The lack of response remained.
All of it — consistent.
She moved to her desk. Opened the files.
Continued.
