## ACT I — DISCOVERY

### Chapter 1 — The Gap

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** Meridian Neurological facility — sealed site on the Lowport coast. Industrial exterior, dark interior, partially powered.
**Goal:** Recover flagged data from Meridian's abandoned servers. Routine job.
**Conflict:** The facility is wrong. Environmental cues — temperature shifts, sounds that shouldn't be there, a sense of presence. Something is still active inside. Iris makes contact with it (shown in fragments, not fully depicted — the reader gets pieces, not the whole event).
**Outcome:** Iris exits the facility. Her extraction gear has data. Her memory does not. There are gaps — minutes she cannot reconstruct. She drives home.
**Emotional Beat:** Iris glances at a photo on her dashboard — her sister Lena. She recognizes the face. She cannot access why it matters. The gap is already there.
**As Written:** Job ref MN-44-7. Iris enters through a cut fence and a side service door with dead keypad. Facility: concrete bunker on the coast, partial power, every third light in the server room. The Taking is shown in fragments — memories surface (kitchen at 19, yellow mug, hospital corridor, red scarf on a winter beach, two girls on a staircase). She sees a pale hand, a flash of Willow's face ("Features learned from observation rather than born to them"). Comes to kneeling by her pack, no memory of the gap. Time jump: 14:07 to 14:41. Drives back to Lowport. Photo of Lena found under a water bottle in the cup holder — printed, softened corners. Recognizes Lena. Feels nothing. Leaves the photo on the dashboard.

### Chapter 2 — Caleb Ward

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** A secondary recovery site in Lowport's industrial zone. Then a diner or work-adjacent location where Iris and Caleb debrief.
**Goal:** Establish Caleb as a real, grounded, alive human being.
**Conflict:** Caleb notices Iris is different — slower, flatter, less reactive. He probes lightly. Iris deflects. She doesn't have language for what's wrong.
**Outcome:** The job completes. Caleb is established as someone the reader likes and trusts. The contrast between his aliveness and Iris's muted state is clear.
**Emotional Beat:** Caleb laughs at something. Iris watches him laugh. She knows she used to respond to this. She waits for the response. It doesn't come.
**As Written:** Job ref HD-12-3 at Harbor Data Storage (converted distribution center, inland from river). Two-person access. Caleb already inside — "Lock was decorative." Work dynamic established: he handles access/physical, she handles systems. He probes her state throughout: "You seem different," "slower, maybe. Or just quieter in a way you weren't before," "Say something / Because you usually would have by now." Dock Nine anecdote — Caleb tells a funny story, Iris responds "It was inconsistent." Diner scene after: Caleb tells a warehouse story, laughs, Iris watches and waits for a matching response that doesn't come. Parting line: "If something's off, you can just say that. You don't have to optimize the answer."

### Chapter 3 — The First Case

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** A residential area in Lowport, north side. The home of the first reported anomaly case.
**Goal:** Iris investigates a person who "came back different" — flagged by Kade as possibly connected to Meridian-adjacent sites.
**Conflict:** The person isn't hostile, isn't distressed. They're just diminished. Functional but flat. Their family is confused, frightened. Medical explanations don't fit.
**Outcome:** Iris observes but can't explain what she's seeing. Something about the case feels familiar — a recognition she can't place. She reports back to Kade.
**Emotional Beat:** Standing outside the person's home afterward, Iris feels a faint pressure in her head — the first hint of her proximity sense, though she doesn't understand it yet.
**As Written:** Subject is Joel (dock worker/logistics). Wife is Leah. Case ref K-01. Joel was gone two days, returned "fine." Responses are correct but empty — "I know what is necessary," "It was appropriate." Iris reports to Kade: "Something has been removed."

### Chapter 4 — Lila Mercer

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** Lila Mercer's apartment or a public space where Iris meets her. Clean, functional, quiet.
**Goal:** Interact with a fully Taken individual up close. Establish what "after" looks like in detail.
**Conflict:** Lila is unsettling not because she's wrong, but because she's too smooth. She answers questions. She makes eye contact. She performs normalcy. But there is no depth behind any of it. Iris recognizes this — not intellectually, but physically. Her body reacts even when her emotions don't.
**Outcome:** Iris leaves knowing this is real. Whatever happened to Lila is the same category as what happened to her — but complete. Lila is what full Taking looks like.
**Emotional Beat:** Lila says something polite as Iris leaves. The words are correct. The tone is correct. Nothing is wrong with it. And that is exactly what is wrong with it.
**As Written:** Lila's apartment — second floor, organized, nothing out of place. Kade arranged the visit ("Daniel said you had some questions"). Lila answers all questions correctly and immediately. Key responses: "I traveled," "It is appropriate" (about tea), "I don't have that information" (repeated — about the person she met, what upset her, whether a conversation mattered), "I respond when required" (about concern for others). Recovery exchange: "Do you want to recover" / "Yes" / "What does that mean to you" / "To return to a previous state of function" / "What was that state" / "I do not have a precise comparison." "Do you feel like anything is missing" / "No." Iris's body reacts physically — tightening in hands, then arms, then shoulders — while her emotions don't. Recognition is somatic, not intellectual. Parting line: "I hope you have a good day." Iris leaves knowing this is the same category as what happened to her, but complete. Does not write anything down. "This was not a question."

### Chapter 5 — Kade

**POV:** Iris Vale (with significant Kade dialogue/presence)
**Location:** Kade's office or operational space — functional, no-frills, maps and logistics pinned to walls.
**Goal:** Expand the scope. Kade has more work — adjacent sites, related infrastructure. The Meridian aftermath is bigger than one facility.
**Conflict:** Kade knows something went wrong at Meridian but is constrained — NDAs, institutional pressure, his own reluctance to dig deeper. He needs Iris's skillset but doesn't fully trust her post-incident. He can tell she's changed.
**Outcome:** Iris takes the expanded work. The relationship is professional and uneasy. Kade reveals hints about Meridian's scale — multiple sites, significant funding, rapid shutdown.
**Emotional Beat:** Kade looks at Iris like he's trying to see what's different. He can't name it. He moves on. This is how everyone responds to her now — a flicker of concern, then nothing.
**As Written:** Kade's office is second floor of a repurposed building — old signage still visible outside, stripped to function inside. Keypad entry, no reception. One wall: layered pinned maps of Lowport and coastline with colored tags and handwritten notes. Opposite wall: shelving with labeled boxes (dates, site codes, some crossed out). Single desk angled to see the room, not for conversation. Kade writes by hand, not typing. He references MN-44-7 directly — knows Iris's Meridian job. Iris discloses the 34-minute gap in her timeline (total time inside: three hours). She did not log an incident. Kade reveals Meridian had multiple sites: primary facility on the coast, secondary storage, offsite data mirrors, some flagged for retrieval before closure. Sites were spread for "risk management" — "From themselves." He provides a folder with six confirmed Meridian-adjacent locations (coordinates, access notes, structural conditions) for Iris to investigate: data retrieval, verification, status, "anything that shouldn't be there." Terms: "Access, retrieval, no questions that get written down." Two maps used: older one showing Meridian-tied sites (logistics, storage, support), newer one overlaid showing recent behavioral anomaly reports — some overlapping Meridian sites, some not. Iris connects Joel (K-01) and Lila Mercer, quotes their language patterns: "I know what is necessary," "It was appropriate," "I don't have that information," "I respond when required." Calls them "constrained" — correct but they don't extend. Kade notices Iris is different: "Before Meridian, you would have pushed harder by now." Iris describes herself as working "more efficiently." Iris's closing line on the cases: "It's moving." Brief Kade-perspective coda after Iris leaves — he looks at the map, tries to remember something specific about her, it doesn't come, moves on. Precedent: first minor POV shift (external observation, not deep interiority).

### Chapter 6 — Rourke

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** Lowport public health office or a clinical setting. Clean, institutional, fluorescent.
**Goal:** Introduce Dr. Elena Rourke and her framework. Establish the institutional resistance.
**Conflict:** Rourke has data on the behavioral anomaly cases. She has models, diagnoses, treatment plans. She is competent and confident. When Iris suggests the cases are connected — that they share a non-medical cause — Rourke dismisses it. Not cruelly. Professionally. She's seen people grasp for patterns that aren't there.
**Outcome:** Iris leaves without an ally. Rourke is not an enemy — she's a wall. Her framework is wrong, but her authority is real.
**Emotional Beat:** Iris realizes she can't feel frustration at Rourke's dismissal. She knows she should be frustrated. She isn't. She notes this absence clinically, like checking a box.
**As Written:** Lowport Public Health building — newer than surrounding block, "inserted" not patched. Controlled air, even lighting, wall-mounted check-in terminal, no waiting room. Frosted glass panels: Behavioral Health, Intake, Evaluation. Rourke's office: desk facing doorway, two straight-aligned chairs, screen with layered data windows. Rourke knows about Iris's case visits — Joel K-01 and Lila Mercer (arranged through third party). Has restricted access to Meridian records, pulls MN-44-7 directly. Iris discloses the 34-minute gap and that she was on-site after shutdown for data recovery. Rourke's dataset: hundreds of cases classified as "behavioral flattening, dissociative presentation, emotional attenuation." Cases span multiple districts, no consistent exposure variable. Neural imaging shows reduced activity in specific emotional processing centers — "not uniform, targeted." No cases recover fully — treatment is "management." Rourke rejects Iris's external-agent theory professionally: "That's not supported." Key exchange: "They're moving" / "They're not moving. They're appearing." Rourke detects Iris presenting with the same markers — flattened affect, delayed response, reduced emotional variance. Recommends evaluation; Iris refuses. Rourke gives Iris a tablet with redacted case summaries — pragmatic, not alliance: "I'd rather you work from something structured than invent the rest." Conditions: "If you find something that fits your model, bring it to me. And if it doesn't, stop forcing it to." Closing exchange: "You're wrong" / "Probably. At some point." / "Now." / "That's not what my data says." / "That's the problem." Final beat: Iris outside, recognizes the space where frustration should be. "Expected: frustration. Observed: none." Closing line: "Inside, the model held. Outside, something was moving."

### Chapter 7 — The Pattern

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** Iris's workspace — wherever she maps data. Possibly her apartment, screens and printouts.
**Goal:** Connect the cases. Find the pattern.
**Conflict:** The data resists easy interpretation, but Iris sees it: geographic concentration, chronological movement, proximity to Meridian-connected infrastructure. This is not random. It's moving. She brings this to Kade.
**Outcome:** Kade listens. He doesn't want to believe it, but he can't dismiss her data. The chapter ends with both of them sitting with the implication.
**Emotional Beat (ACT I TURN):** Iris states it plainly, to herself or to Kade: this is not a condition. It's not environmental. Something is out there. Something that moves. And it touches people.
**As Written:** Iris's apartment, table cleared for work — multiple unsynchronized screens (case files, city map, timelines, interview fragments) plus printouts. She works alone for hours, into evening. Analysis method: filters cases by time, creating a gradient — earliest fade, recent sharpen. A directional path emerges, not a cluster. Two outliers removed — they don't belong to the pattern. Infrastructure overlay (transit routes, utility corridors, maintenance access) filtered to Meridian-adjacent systems only — the case points intersect something beneath them. Overlay is incomplete: old data, sections marked decommissioned without confirmation. Transcript analysis: strips content, leaves only form — "Question → Answer → Stop." Repeated across cases. "Different words. Same shape." Kade call is audio only, connects on second attempt. He pushes back: wants mechanism, source, verification. Key exchanges: "You're saying it's moving" / "Yes" / "People move" / "This isn't people." Cases align with Meridian-era legacy infrastructure. "What are you seeing?" / "Meridian-era systems." "That were decommissioned" / "Partially." Kade asks her to send everything including the outliers — "Don't clean it." Closing exchange: "You've changed" / "Yes." Line closes (shorter than originally planned — the brevity works better). Proximity sense activates while studying the map — faint recognition, not visual or spatial. Logged clinically: "Location: frontal. Duration: brief. Intensity: minimal." No pain. No interference. Fades without resolving. Act I turn spoken aloud to empty room: "It's not a condition." "It's not environmental." "Something is moving." "And it touches people." Delivered flat, exact, no affect. The room doesn't respond. The data doesn't change. Closing: "The pattern remained where it was. Not solved. Not contained. Just visible. For now."

## ACT II — ESCALATION

### Chapter 8 — Following the Thread

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** Multiple Lowport locations — outer industrial zones, abandoned infrastructure near the coast.
**Goal:** Iris and Kade investigate more cases on-site.
**Conflict:** Each case reinforces the pattern: contact-based, localized, progressive. But Kade recognizes some of the locations — they're sites he helped coordinate during his Meridian logistics work. The connection is getting personal for him.
**Outcome:** More data. More confirmation. Kade is visibly shaken but keeps functioning.
**Emotional Beat:** Kade says something about the locations, then stops. Iris watches him decide not to finish the sentence. She doesn't push. She notes that she doesn't care enough to push.
**As Written:** Kade drives. Three Meridian-adjacent sites visited. Site one: industrial perimeter, sealed building, relay point for "data movement." Kade's access code still works — clearance never revoked. Interior stripped: conduits exposed, mounting brackets without hardware, floor markings where equipment was fixed. "Just absence." Case: maintenance contract worker, still functional, family reported change. Center of room — nothing visible, no indication anything occurred. Kade recognizes the site, runs his hand along a conduit, stops. Holds something back. "This wasn't shut down clean." Site two: closer to coast, older construction. Transfer point for equipment. Kade: "Does it matter" / "Yes" / "No" (refuses to specify equipment type). More segmented interior — narrow corridors, central hall. Empty room, no trace. Kade's guilt surfaces: "I moved things through here. Crates. Equipment. Data units. Didn't ask what was in them." "They gave us locations. We routed them. That was it." "Didn't ask why." — stops, doesn't finish. Iris watches him decide not to finish. Doesn't ask him to. Site three: smaller, peripheral, barely maintained, no access system. Case two days recent. Proximity sense fires — faint shift, not pain, not discomfort. Iris says "Something." It doesn't resolve, fades. She says "Nothing." Kade: "You felt something" / "Yes" / "What" / "I don't know." Outside: "These weren't supposed to stay active." Holds back something more specific, doesn't say it. Driving between sites: Iris watches Kade's pauses, notes them structurally not emotionally. "Opportunity to ask. No action taken. She recognized the gap. She did not feel the need to close it." Closing: the path continues — unmarked, consistent, moving. Not contained. Continuing.

**Emotional Beat:** Iris walks through a neighborhood she used to know. Everything looks the same. Nothing feels the same. She can't tell if it's the city or her.

Two people collide — "Sorry" / "It's fine" — separate immediately, no further acknowledgment. Market: customer asks vendor about availability, exchange ends, customer lingers "as if expecting more." ### Chapter 11 — Caleb Shift

**Goal:** Seed Caleb's Taking before it happens. Make the reader feel it coming.
**Conflict:** Caleb is working, talking, functioning. But something is slightly off — a pause that lasts a beat too long, a joke that doesn't land the way it should, a response that's correct but delayed. Iris notices. She's not sure if she's projecting.
**Outcome:** Nothing definitive. The chapter ends without confirmation. Just unease.
**Emotional Beat:** Iris looks at Caleb and thinks: he is the most alive person she knows. The sentence arrives in past tense before she corrects it.
**As Written:** Active job site, outer zone. Kade sent coordinates with Caleb's name attached. Caleb spotted near a temporary rig, speaking to workers — posture loose, balanced, gesturing. ### Chapter 12 — The Taking of Caleb Ward (MIDPOINT)

**POV:** Iris Vale (arriving after) OR Caleb Ward (experiencing it) — strongest as Caleb's POV, the only chapter from his perspective.
**Location:** An outer-zone location. Dim. Industrial. Quiet.
**Goal:** Show a Taking on-page. Make the reader feel it.
**Conflict:** Caleb encounters Willow. There is no chase, no fight. She is there. She touches him. He goes still. A flash of emotional exposure — memories surfacing involuntarily, feelings he hasn't accessed in years suddenly raw and present. Then she takes them. One by one. Not all of them. But enough.
**Outcome:** Caleb stands. Looks around. Functions. He will continue to function. But the thing that made him Caleb — the sharpness, the warmth, the reckless aliveness — is diminished. Removed. Simplified.
**Emotional Beat:** The last thing Caleb feels, before the Taking completes, is something he can't name. Then he doesn't feel it anymore. Then he doesn't notice it's gone.
**As Written:** Caleb's POV — only chapter from his perspective. End of shift at the outer-zone site from Ch11. He stays for one more pass — unnecessary, does it anyway (characterizes pre-Taking Caleb). Control room panel reports normal. Corridor: he stops because something has changed. She is at the far end — he didn't see her enter, she was simply there. Standing in the intersection. Still. Present. No immediate reaction to access, no escalation. He walks toward her at the same pace. Details resolve: human shape, proportions correct, stillness exact. Her face — he tries to focus, the attempt doesn't hold. "Not retained." She closes the remaining space — no visible effort, one moment distance, the next none. Hand on his chest — flat, over center, no pressure, no force. Everything holds. Then something opens inside. Memories surface unprompted: running as a kid (certainty he wouldn't fall), a late industrial shift (someone handed him a coffee he hadn't asked for — "the warmth of it, the fact that they had noticed"), a job gone wrong (improvised solution, the edge, the satisfaction). They come faster — moments with weight, with heat. Emotion not remembered but felt. The Taking: memories thin — not fade, remove. Feeling separates from image, then image follows. "Gone." Each one surfaces, reduces, is stripped of what made it hold. "Not all of them. Not everything. Just — enough." He registers it not as loss but as change. No panic, no resistance, no framework for either. Final memory holds longest: not an event but a state — "the sense that things mattered. That actions carried weight beyond completion." He tries to remain with it. It resists classification. Stays long enough to register. Then separates. Reduced. Thinned. Gone. The space remains empty. He registers it. Then that registration passes. No dialogue from Willow. She is silent throughout. Post-Taking: he looks around, corridor the same, alone. No tremor. Walks back to control room, logs a note: "Response timing remains inconsistent. No confirmed failure." Pauses at threshold — no identified reason. Drives home. "Nothing in him marked the change. Nothing in him noted the absence. The process had completed. He continued."

### Chapter 15 — Deterioration

**POV:** Iris Vale
**Location:** Various — Iris moving through Lowport, working, investigating.
**Goal:** Show Iris's reliability crumbling.
**Conflict:** False certainties: Iris is sure of things that aren't true. She remembers conversations that didn't happen. Memory gaps are widening. She second-guesses her own data, her own conclusions. The person trying to stop the threat is becoming the least reliable narrator of her own story.
